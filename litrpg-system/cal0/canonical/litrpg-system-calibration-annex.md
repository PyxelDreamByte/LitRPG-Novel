## CAL0-Q1B — Factorised developmental foundations

### Governing model

Natural maturation is calculated from shared and local foundation components rather than an age-to-attribute lookup. Age or developmental time may drive component curves, but no direct rule assigns an attribute merely because a birthday or gestational checkpoint was reached.

For person \(p\), foundation component \(k\), and time \(t\), let:

- \(D_{s,k}(t)\) be the species- and life-stage developmental programme for the component;
- \(E_{p,k}\) be inherited or individually seeded endowment;
- \(H_{p,k}(t)\) be cumulative support from nutrition, oxygenation, health, sleep, safety, and compatible environment;
- \(J_{p,k}(t)\) be disruption from disease, deprivation, injury, toxins, developmental conflict, or other typed damage;
- \(F_{p,k}(t)\) be the realised component state.

The initial implementation treats the component target and its lagged realisation as:

\[
\widetilde F_{p,k}(t)
=
D_{s,k}(t)E_{p,k}H_{p,k}(t)-J_{p,k}(t),
\]

\[
\dot F_{p,k}
=
\lambda^+_{k}[\widetilde F_{p,k}-F_{p,k}]_+
-
\lambda^-_{k}[F_{p,k}-\widetilde F_{p,k}]_+,
\qquad [x]_+=\max(0,x).
\]

The separate rise and loss rates permit delayed growth, catch-up, developmental windows, persistent deficits, injury, rehabilitation, and ageing without making those outcomes direct attribute modifiers.

Attribute \(i\) maps from the components it genuinely requires:

\[
R^{\mathrm{mat}}_{p,i}(t)
=
\alpha_{s,i}\,G_{p,i}(t)\,
\mathcal A_i\!\left(F_{p,K_i}(t);w_{i,k},\rho_i\right),
\]

where:

- \(\alpha_{s,i}\) is the species-relative reference scale for that attribute;
- \(G_{p,i}(t)\in[0,1]\) is a structural-availability gate;
- \(K_i\) is the set of relevant foundation components;
- \(w_{i,k}\ge 0\) and \(\sum_k w_{i,k}=1\);
- \(\mathcal A_i\) is a bounded aggregation function;
- \(\rho_i\) controls whether the attribute behaves more like a compensatory average or a bottlenecked capacity.

The first candidate aggregator is the weighted generalised mean:

\[
\mathcal A_i(F;w,\rho)
=
\left(\sum_k w_{i,k}F_k^{\rho_i}\right)^{1/\rho_i},
\]

with the \(\rho_i\rightarrow 0\) limit equal to the weighted geometric mean. Exact weights, exponents, gates, and component curves remain calibratable.

### Initial foundation families

The registry begins with shared families rather than one component per attribute.

| Domain | Candidate foundation families |
|---|---|
| Body | structural scale and integrity; force-generation and transmission apparatus; sensorimotor integration; metabolic and circulatory delivery; biological protection, repair, and adaptation |
| Mind | neural and representational integration; sensory-processing bandwidth; memory and working representation; attention and executive control; developmental stability and recovery |
| Soul | identity anchoring and supported metaphysical volume; internal order and boundary integrity; external coupling and selectivity; load recovery and brain–Soul–body integration |

The mapping is many-to-many. For example, Finesse and Alacrity share sensorimotor foundations but weight precision, initiation speed, force transmission, and control differently. Depth, Coherence, and Resonance share Soul development while remaining independently variable.

### Causal attribution rule

Maturation, organic adaptation, and reinforcement alter one final rated attribute state; they are not three separately usable copies of the attribute. Their changes nevertheless retain provenance:

\[
\Delta R_i
=
\Delta R_i^{\mathrm{mat}}
+
\Delta R_i^{\mathrm{org}}
+
\Delta R_i^{\mathrm{rein}}
+
\Delta R_i^{\mathrm{transform}}
-
\Delta R_i^{\mathrm{loss}}
+
\Delta R_i^{\mathrm{repair}}.
\]

The attribution ledger records which mechanism caused each change. Current fatigue, pain, distraction, low resources, and temporary conditions normally suppress performance rather than rewriting rated capacity.

## CAL0-Q4B — Milestone-anchored monotone developmental splines

### Governing interpolation

Each species-and-component developmental programme is authored through explicit ordered anchors rather than inferred from a direct age-to-attribute curve. For component \(k\), let the anchor set be:

\[
\mathcal M_{s,k}
=
\{(\theta_{s,k,m},y_{s,k,m},\mu_{s,k,m})\}_{m=0}^{M_k},
\]

where \(\theta\) is a declared developmental coordinate, \(y\geq0\) is the baseline component target, and \(\mu\) identifies the causal milestone represented by the anchor. Prenatal phases, birth, childhood transitions, adolescence, mature function, and ageing may all be represented, but an anchor label is descriptive: reaching an age or label does not directly grant an attribute.

Between adjacent anchors, the initial implementation uses a shape-preserving piecewise cubic Hermite interpolant:

\[
D_{s,k}(\theta)
=
\operatorname{PCHIP}(\mathcal M_{s,k};\theta).
\]

The knot slopes are constrained by the Fritsch–Carlson monotonicity conditions. This prevents cubic overshoot, negative foundation targets, and accidental capability spikes between calibrated anchors. A programme that contains both growth and ageing decline is partitioned at declared turning anchors; each segment is monotone even though the whole life-course need not be.

The spline supplies only the unmodified baseline target used by CAL0-Q1B. Individual endowment, support, disruption, structural availability, and the separate rise-or-loss lag still determine realised foundation:

\[
\widetilde F_{p,k}(t)
=
D_{s,k}(\theta_p(t))E_{p,k}H_{p,k}(t)-J_{p,k}(t),
\]

\[
\dot F_{p,k}
=
\lambda^+_k[\widetilde F_{p,k}-F_{p,k}]_+
-
\lambda^-_k[F_{p,k}-\widetilde F_{p,k}]_+.
\]

Consequently, two people at the same chronological age may have different realised foundations, and a missed developmental window, deprivation, injury, catch-up process, magical alteration, or species difference can be represented without editing an attribute directly.

### Boundary and authoring rules

- Anchors are versioned calibration data with explicit species, component, population, and life-stage scope.
- Interpolation never invents a local extremum between monotone anchors.
- Values before the first and after the last anchor use declared boundary policies; silent polynomial extrapolation is prohibited.
- Discontinuities such as birth-related environmental change alter support or coupling functions unless a real structural discontinuity justifies a component anchor break.
- Ageing decline uses separately declared turning anchors and loss dynamics rather than a hidden negative growth coefficient.
- Cohort sampling may vary anchor timing and magnitude through registered correlated distributions, but must preserve order and declared biological constraints.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q4B parameters

- species baseline event timings, coordinate values, and chronological-time interpolation;
- the canonical milestone vocabulary and which components require distinct prenatal anchors;
- anchor timings, target values, turning points, and boundary policies;
- allowed individual timing variation and cross-component synchronisation;
- which anchor changes are ordinary calibration versus transformation or injury events.

## CAL0-Q7B — Hybrid event-anchored developmental coordinate

### Baseline coordinate

Chronological time remains an observable and narratively usable quantity, but it does not directly grant foundation or attribute capacity. Each species supplies an ordered baseline event map:

\[
\mathcal E_s
=
\{(t_{s,m},\vartheta_{s,m},\mu_{s,m})\}_{m=0}^{M_s},
\qquad
t_{s,m+1}>t_{s,m},
\qquad
\vartheta_{s,m+1}>\vartheta_{s,m},
\]

where (t) is chronological time relative to a declared origin, \(\vartheta\) is a dimensionless developmental coordinate, and \(\mu\) identifies a causal transition such as conception, a prenatal phase change, birth, puberty, mature function, or an ageing turn. The species baseline coordinate is:

\[
\Theta_s(t)=\operatorname{PCHIP}(\mathcal E_s;t),
\]

using the same shape-preserving and explicit-boundary rules as CAL0-Q4B. Event labels describe the programme; crossing a baseline event coordinate does not manufacture the structures associated with that event.

### Individual component clocks

Each foundation component (k) reads the developmental programme through its own causally warped coordinate:

\[
\theta_{p,k}(t)
=
\operatorname{Proj}_{\Omega_{s,k}}
\bigl(\Theta_s(t)+\Delta_{p,k}(t)\bigr),
\]

\[
\dot\Delta_{p,k}
=
\eta^+_k Z^+_{p,k}(t)
-
\eta^-_k Z^-_{p,k}(t)
-
\kappa_k\Delta_{p,k}(t).
\]

Here (Z^+) and (Z^-) are registered causal timing influences: inherited timing variation, endocrine or magical developmental signals, nutrition, oxygenation, illness, deprivation, injury, treatment, catch-up support, or an explicit transformation. They are not inferred from the realised foundation value, so the clock cannot circularly declare a component mature because that component already appears strong. The final projection preserves milestone order, component-specific advance and lag bounds, prerequisite gates, and non-negative coordinate velocity.

The developmental target in CAL0-Q4B therefore becomes:

\[
D_{s,k}(\theta_{p,k}(t)),
\]

while realised foundation still follows endowment, support, disruption, structural availability, and rise-or-loss lag under CAL0-Q1B. Two people or two components at the same chronological age can consequently occupy different developmental coordinates without age ceasing to be measurable.

### Boundary and causality rules

- Conception, birth, environmental exposure, treatment, injury, and transformation retain their real event times; the component coordinate does not rewrite history.
- A component cannot advance through a prerequisite transition whose required structure or coupling is absent.
- Ordinary timing variation is bounded and order preserving. Unbounded advance, reversal, or skipped topology requires an explicit exceptional process.
- Catch-up accelerates coordinate progression only when the causal support and remaining developmental plasticity permit it; it does not repay missed growth automatically.
- Ageing turns are declared events with their own calibrated clocks and loss processes, not a hidden negative value applied to every foundation.
- Cross-species comparison uses homologous event meanings where valid and retains species-specific events where no honest homology exists.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q7B parameters

- the canonical event vocabulary and coordinate values for the human reference programme;
- component-specific advance, lag, synchronisation, and prerequisite bounds;
- correlated inherited timing distributions and their separation from environmental timing effects;
- (Z^+), (Z^-), \(\eta^+\), \(\eta^-\), and resynchronisation functions;
- rules for variable gestation, premature birth, delayed transitions, catch-up, magical development, and transformation;
- coordinate precision, update cadence, and cross-species homologous-event mappings.

## CAL0-Q10B — Correlated developmental timing factors

### Cohort sampling topology

Population variation is sampled through shared latent causes rather than independent offsets for every component. For person \(p\), component \(k\), and developmental event family \(m\), define the ordinary inherited timing seed:

\[
\xi_{p,k,m}
=
\mathbf l_{s,k,m}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf g_p
+
\sigma^{\mathrm{res}}_{s,k,m}\epsilon_{p,k,m},
\]

where \(\mathbf g_p\) contains registered species or population, systemic, Body, Mind, Soul, and other justified shared factors; \(\mathbf l_{s,k,m}\) is the component loading vector; and \(\epsilon_{p,k,m}\) is a component residual. The latent vector is sampled from a registered zero-centred joint distribution with correlation matrix \(\Sigma_s\). Residuals are zero-centred and independent of the shared factors unless an explicit covariance is registered.

The seed does not directly set capability. It supplies bounded initial conditions or tempo modifiers to the causal coordinate model in CAL0-Q7B:

\[
\Delta^{\mathrm{seed}}_{p,k,m}
=
b_{s,k,m}\tanh(\xi_{p,k,m}),
\qquad b_{s,k,m}\geq0,
\]

after which nutrition, illness, support, injury, treatment, magical influence, and other lived causes update the trajectory through \(Z^+\) and \(Z^-\). Event times are projected through the ordered constraint set \(\Omega_s\), so sampled variation cannot reverse prerequisites, create negative gestation, or silently skip a required transition.

Shared factors create coherent developmental families without forcing lockstep. A positive systemic factor may advance several components, a Body factor may primarily affect somatic components, and component residuals may make one structure earlier or later than its neighbours. Body, Mind, and Soul factors are not assumed statistically independent; their covariance is itself registered and tested.

### Boundary and cohort rules

- Every random draw is seed-reproducible and recorded in the run manifest by distribution version, correlation matrix, truncation or projection rule, and sampling order.
- Marginal timing distributions and the realised joint distribution must both be validated; plausible marginals do not excuse incoherent combinations.
- Correlation never substitutes for causation in an individual history. Once sampled, later changes require recorded causal modifiers.
- Ordinary residuals are bounded and cannot overwhelm prerequisite topology. Exceptional development requires an explicit exceptional process rather than a tail draw.
- Species, ancestry, population, maternal or gestational environment, and individual factors remain separately scoped; group coefficients cannot be used as deterministic individual destiny.
- Selection, mortality, missing observations, and developmental disruption are retained rather than silently resampling an inconvenient birth.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q10B parameters

- factor vocabulary, component loadings, covariance matrices, and residual scales by species and population scope;
- distribution families, tail treatment, truncation, and ordered-event projection method;
- inheritance, maternal or gestational, environmental, and Soul-factor separation;
- event-family coupling across prenatal, childhood, adolescent, mature, and ageing transitions;
- calibration targets for within-person coherence, population dispersion, and rare but ordinary outliers;
- sensitivity and identifiability tests required before any factor receives a causal interpretation.

## CAL0-Q13B — Gaussian copula with authored bounded marginals

### Sampling rule

The dependence structure and every factor's marginal distribution are registered separately. For a seeded person (p), draw a reproducible standard-normal vector

\[
\mathbf z_p\sim\mathcal N(\mathbf 0,\Sigma_s),
\qquad
u_{p,r}=\Phi(z_{p,r}),
\qquad
g_{p,r}=F^{-1}_{s,r}(u_{p,r}),
\]

where \(\Sigma_s\) is a valid correlation matrix, \(\Phi\) is the standard-normal cumulative distribution function, and each authored marginal \(F_{s,r}\) has declared finite support, shape, scope, and boundary behaviour. The resulting \(\mathbf g_p\) replaces the abstract shared-factor draw in CAL0-Q10B. Component residuals may use the same construction under a separately registered copula, or declared independent bounded marginals; they never inherit \(\Sigma_s\) implicitly.

This choice supersedes no developmental cause. It only specifies how ordinary cohort variation is sampled before causal life history updates the trajectory. Correlation parameters are rank-dependence controls, not direct Pearson correlations in the transformed factors; realised Pearson, Spearman, and tail behaviour must be measured rather than assumed.

### Boundary and anti-reification rules

- Every marginal has authored lower and upper bounds; exceptional development cannot be manufactured by extending an ordinary tail beyond those bounds.
- Inverse-CDF evaluation uses a registered numerical method and open-unit convention so exact `0` and `1` never create undefined endpoints.
- \(\Sigma_s\) must be symmetric, unit diagonal, and positive semidefinite. Any repair or projection is explicit, versioned, and reported rather than silently applied.
- The sampler preserves each registered marginal under adequate cohort size; changing correlation does not silently change the intended one-dimensional distribution.
- Species, population, gestational, Body, Mind, Soul, systemic, and residual scopes remain distinct. Statistical dependence does not establish causal coupling.
- No factor value assigns a person to an archetype, caste, destiny, or categorical developmental kind.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q13B parameters

- bounded marginal families and parameters for each shared factor and residual;
- correlation matrices, permitted structural zeros, and any justified conditional dependence by scope;
- positive-semidefinite validation, repair policy, inverse-CDF precision, and sampling order;
- cohort diagnostics for marginal recovery, rank dependence, transformed Pearson dependence, and boundary accumulation;
- treatment of missing observations, selection, mortality, and uncertain population targets;
- sensitivity thresholds at which conclusions must be reported as dependence-model contingent.

## CAL0-Q16B — Structured sparse dependence blocks

### Dependence construction

Let registered latent blocks (b\in\mathcal B_s) represent species or population, systemic, Body, Mind, Soul, and any justified cross-domain causes. For standard-normal latent block scores \(\mathbf h_p\sim\mathcal N(\mathbf0,I)\), component scores are

\[
\mathbf z_p=L_s\mathbf h_p+\boldsymbol\epsilon_p,
\qquad
\boldsymbol\epsilon_p\sim\mathcal N(\mathbf0,\Psi_s),
\]

where \(L_s\) is sparse by declared block membership. Residual covariance \(\Psi_s\) is diagonal unless a specific residual link is registered with causal or empirical justification. The raw covariance is

\[
C_s=L_sL_s^\top+\Psi_s,
\]

and the copula correlation supplied to CAL0-Q13B is

\[
\Sigma_s=D_s^{-1/2}C_sD_s^{-1/2},
\qquad D_s=\operatorname{diag}(C_s).
\]

Every row must have strictly positive variance. Structural zeros in \(L_s\) and \(\Psi_s\) are binding absence claims at that parameter-set version, not missing values silently estimated at runtime.

### Boundary and governance rules

- Principal dependence comes from named blocks whose scope, loadings, signs, and rationale are registered; an unexplained dense matrix is not an admissible shortcut.
- Residual links are sparse exceptions. Each names both endpoints, sign, permitted magnitude, evidence basis, and sensitivity priority.
- Normalisation must produce a symmetric, unit-diagonal, positive-semidefinite \(\Sigma_s\). A failed validation rejects the parameter set rather than silently repairing it.
- A shared block may create dependence without forcing identical timing, rank, or outcome; bounded marginals and component residuals remain active.
- Statistical blocks do not become biological substances, social castes, or individual destinies.
- Changing one block or residual link creates a new distribution version and requires marginal, dependence, and downstream sensitivity checks.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q16B parameters

- block vocabulary, membership, loading signs, loading bounds, and allowed cross-domain blocks by scope;
- residual-variance floors, permitted residual graph, and maximum residual-link density;
- identifiability conventions, sign orientation, and equivalent-factor-rotation policy;
- parameter-set rejection tolerances and diagnostics for near-singular covariance;
- cohort targets for within-block, cross-block, and residual rank dependence;
- escalation criteria for replacing the Gaussian copula if validated asymmetric or tail dependence cannot be represented.

## CAL0-Q2C — Typed multi-timescale adaptation network

### Governing model

Purposeful activity creates typed stimuli. It does not award generic training points or fixed attribute increments. Each episode identifies the structures and control problems actually loaded, then updates acute fatigue, damage risk, adaptation opportunity, consolidation, chronic adaptation, interference, and decay on different timescales.

For adaptation channel \(j\), an episode produces stimulus:

\[
u_j
=
\mathcal U_j
(\text{load},\text{duration},\text{volume},\text{specificity},
\text{novelty},\text{technique},\text{feedback},\text{foundation},\text{condition}).
\]

The minimal reference network is:

\[
\dot f_j=k^{f}_j u_j-\frac{f_j}{\tau^{f}_j},
\]

\[
\dot d_j
=
k^{d}_j[u_j-q_j]_+^{m_j}
-
r^{d}_j h_j d_j,
\]

\[
\dot a_j
=
k^{a}_j u_j n_j b_j
-
\frac{a_j}{\tau^{a}_j},
\]

\[
\dot c_j
=
k^{c}_j a_j h_j I_j
-
\frac{c_j}{\tau^{c}_j},
\]

\[
\dot O_i
=
\sum_j S_{ij}c_j h_j I_j B_{ij}(R,F)
-
\delta_i O_i.
\]

Where:

- \(f_j\) is acute fatigue or temporary load;
- \(d_j\) is typed damage or maladaptive burden;
- \(a_j\) is an adaptation opportunity signal;
- \(c_j\) is safely consolidating change;
- \(O_i\) is the provenance ledger for contingent organic contribution to attribute \(i\);
- \(q_j\) is the safe stimulus boundary under current conditions;
- \(n_j\) is novelty or unresolved developmental content;
- \(b_j\) is usable feedback and correction quality;
- \(h_j\) is recovery and resource support;
- \(I_j\) is cross-training, growth, illness, injury, work, and reinforcement-assimilation interference;
- \(S_{ij}\) maps the adaptation channel to genuinely loaded attributes;
- \(B_{ij}(R,F)\) applies foundation compatibility, present ceiling, and diminishing returns;
- \(\delta_i\) governs detraining or decay where the adaptation is not maintained.

The equations define topology, not settled coefficients. A safe low-load episode may produce little fatigue and meaningful calibration; an extreme episode may produce large fatigue and damage but little successfully consolidated adaptation. Injury, pain, fear, danger, repetition, or elapsed time never guarantees Will, Vitality, Skill XP, or any other gain.

Press-ups, distance running, an assault course, difficult mathematics, memorisation, meditation, Mana practice, and Soul practice therefore produce different stimulus vectors even when their duration is equal.

\Pi_i(t)=R_i(t)\,\chi_i(f,d,\text{resources},\text{condition}),
\]

where \(\chi_i\) is normally at most `1.00` unless a separately supported temporary effect applies. Chronic organic adaptation changes rated capacity only after consolidation; fatigue itself is not stat growth.

## CAL0-Q8B — Typed hybrid adaptation-dose vector

### Episode record

Every purposeful episode (e) records what it actually demanded from person (p) in each adaptation channel (j). Equal duration, perceived effort, danger, or narrative importance does not make two episodes equivalent. The minimal normalised dose record is:

\[
\mathbf z_{e,p,j}
=
\left(
a_{e,j},
\rho_{e,p,j},
v_{e,j},
\tau_{e,j},
n_{e,p,j},
b_{e,j},
\boldsymbol\ell_{e,p,j}
\right),
\]

with:

\[
a_{e,j}=\frac{d^{\mathrm{abs}}_{e,j}}{d^{\mathrm{ref}}_j},
\qquad
\rho_{e,p,j}
=
\frac{d^{\mathrm{abs}}_{e,j}}
{\max(C^{\mathrm{task}}_{p,j},\epsilon_j)}.
\]

The fields are:

- (a_{e,j}\geq0): absolute task demand relative to a channel reference, retaining the difference between objectively small and objectively large work;
- \(\rho_{e,p,j}\geq0\): challenge relative to the person's current task-conditioned available capacity, not merely their displayed attribute;
- (v_{e,j}\geq0): exposure, work volume, duration, repetitions, distance, solved operations, or another channel-appropriate integral;
- \(\tau_{e,j}\in[0,1]\): execution quality and method compatibility, which may route load as well as change productive efficiency or harm risk;
- (n_{e,p,j}\in[0,1]): unresolved novelty or developmental content relative to the person's existing solution repertoire;
- (b_{e,j}\in[0,1]): usable feedback, error visibility, and opportunity for correction;
- \(\boldsymbol\ell_{e,p,j}\): a non-negative vector identifying the tissues, neural circuits, cognitive operations, Mana structures, Soul structures, and control relationships actually loaded. Its entries are exposure weights, not probabilities, and need not sum to one.

Episode provenance also retains task, environment, equipment, assistance, interruption, intent, and measurement uncertainty. These fields are not silently compressed into the dose vector when they materially change interpretation.

### Typed response projection

The vector does not collapse immediately into one universal training score. Channel-specific functions project it into at least three distinct signals:

\[
u^{\mathrm{opp}}_{e,p,j}
=
\mathcal U^{\mathrm{opp}}_j(\mathbf z,F,\text{condition}),
\]

\[
u^{\mathrm{fat}}_{e,p,j}
=
\mathcal U^{\mathrm{fat}}_j(\mathbf z,F,\text{condition}),
\qquad
u^{\mathrm{harm}}_{e,p,j}
=
\mathcal U^{\mathrm{harm}}_j(\mathbf z,F,\text{condition}).
\]

The opportunity signal feeds (a_j) in CAL0-Q2C; fatigue and harm feed the acute-load and damage pathways. Productive opportunity can peak and then fall as challenge becomes excessive, while fatigue and harm may continue rising. Technique can move demand toward intended structures and away from compensatory ones. Novelty primarily affects learnable opportunity, feedback primarily affects correction and consolidation, and neither erases real physical or metaphysical load.

Press-ups, heavy lifting, running, assault courses, mathematics, puzzles, meditation, Mana work, and Soul practice therefore differ through their absolute demands, relative challenges, volume measures, execution requirements, novelty, feedback, and loaded-structure vectors. A single episode may stimulate several channels, but unsupported channels receive no gain merely because another channel was strongly loaded.

### Boundary and anti-exploit rules

- Zero loaded structure means zero adaptation opportunity for that structure even when time and effort are non-zero.
- Extremely low absolute demand cannot become an unlimited developmental engine solely because the actor is weak; relative challenge and absolute work remain separate.
- Extremely high absolute demand cannot guarantee improvement solely because it was survived; excessive demand may route primarily to fatigue, harm, or protective inhibition.
- Repetition lowers novelty when the actor has solved the developmental problem, although it may still maintain capacity, refine execution, or accumulate work-specific fatigue.
- Assistance and equipment alter absolute demand, relative challenge, execution, and loaded structures explicitly rather than applying a generic penalty.
- One event record may support organic adaptation, Skill XP, Class XP, and attestation, but each projection uses its own causal rules and provenance ledger.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q8B parameters

- channel-specific absolute-demand units, references, exposure integrals, and task-conditioned capacity functions;
- measurement and inference rules for technique, novelty, feedback, and structure loading;
- productive, fatigue, and harm response surfaces over absolute and relative challenge;
- interaction terms, uncertainty propagation, and dose aggregation across interrupted or compound episodes;
- maintenance-dose, minimum-effective-dose, saturation, overload, and recovery-spacing behaviour;
- cross-channel transfer rules for complex activities and mixed physical, cognitive, magical, or Soul practice.

## CAL0-Q11B — Channel-specific hormetic windows

### Productive, fatigue, and harm responses

Each adaptation channel maps its typed dose vector to a scalar effective challenge coordinate only inside that channel's response function:

\[
\chi_{e,p,j}
=
\mathcal C_j
\left(\mathbf z_{e,p,j},F_p,\text{condition},\text{recovery history}\right),
\qquad \chi_{e,p,j}\geq0.
\]

This projection is not a universal training score and is not transferable between channels. A candidate productive-window family uses four ordered channel parameters:

\[
0\leq \chi^{\mathrm{maint}}_j
<\chi^{\mathrm{peak}}_j
<\chi^{\mathrm{excess}}_j
<\chi^{\mathrm{stop}}_j.
\]

The productive opportunity multiplier is a continuous non-negative function:

\[
W^{\mathrm{opp}}_j(\chi)=
\begin{cases}
0, & 0\leq\chi\leq\chi^{\mathrm{maint}}_j,\\
S_j\!\left(\dfrac{\chi-\chi^{\mathrm{maint}}_j}{\chi^{\mathrm{peak}}_j-\chi^{\mathrm{maint}}_j}\right),
& \chi^{\mathrm{maint}}_j<\chi\leq\chi^{\mathrm{peak}}_j,\\
1-S_j\!\left(\dfrac{\chi-\chi^{\mathrm{peak}}_j}{\chi^{\mathrm{stop}}_j-\chi^{\mathrm{peak}}_j}\right),
& \chi^{\mathrm{peak}}_j<\chi<\chi^{\mathrm{stop}}_j,\\
0, & \chi\geq\chi^{\mathrm{stop}}_j,
\end{cases}
\]

where \(S_j:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]\) is a registered monotone shape function with \(S_j(0)=0\) and \(S_j(1)=1\). The excessive boundary marks the point at which the falling opportunity curve is accompanied by materially accelerating risk; it need not be a discontinuity.

Fatigue and harm are separate outputs:

\[
W^{\mathrm{fat}}_j(\chi)\geq0,
\qquad
\frac{dW^{\mathrm{fat}}_j}{d\chi}\geq0,
\]

\[
W^{\mathrm{harm}}_j(\chi)
=
k^{\mathrm{harm}}_j
[\chi-\chi^{\mathrm{excess}}_j]_+^{m_j},
\qquad m_j>1.
\]

Low doses may maintain existing adaptation even when they create little new opportunity. Novelty, technique, feedback, sleep, resources, acute condition, accumulated fatigue, injury, Mana stability, and Soul stability may alter the location, width, or attainable height of the window, but every shift is typed and bounded. Poor recovery normally narrows or lowers the productive region rather than making the same dose more valuable because it feels harder.

### Boundary and anti-exploit rules

- Surviving an excessive dose does not guarantee productive opportunity, XP, attestation, envelope expansion, or a breakthrough.
- Opportunity may fall to zero while fatigue and harm continue increasing.
- Maintenance, new adaptation, Skill evidence, and heroic risk remain distinct projections from the same event.
- A stronger person may require greater absolute demand to occupy the same relative region, but absolute demand, loaded structures, and task feasibility remain preserved.
- Repeated sub-threshold work may maintain capacity or technique; it cannot accumulate fractional challenge until it retrospectively becomes one enormous session.
- Multiple episodes interact through recovery state and accumulated load rather than being evaluated as isolated fresh actors.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q11B parameters

- challenge-coordinate functions and window boundaries for each physical, cognitive, perceptual, magical, and Soul channel;
- shape functions, minimum effective doses, maintenance responses, and peak widths;
- fatigue and harm functions, exponents, protective inhibition, and stop conditions;
- effects of novelty, technique, feedback, recovery, development, health, equipment, and assistance on each window;
- session aggregation, spacing, repeated-bout effects, and cross-channel interference;
- calibration observables distinguishing productive overload from survivorship bias and hidden injury.

## CAL0-Q14B — Constrained asymmetric beta productive windows

### Window function

For \(\chi^{\mathrm{maint}}_j<\chi<\chi^{\mathrm{stop}}_j\), normalise challenge as

\[
x_j=\frac{\chi-\chi^{\mathrm{maint}}_j}
{\chi^{\mathrm{stop}}_j-\chi^{\mathrm{maint}}_j}\in(0,1).
\]

Author the mode \(m_j\in(0,1)\) and concentration \(\kappa_j>2\), then derive

\[
\alpha_j=1+m_j(\kappa_j-2),
\qquad
\beta_j=1+(1-m_j)(\kappa_j-2),
\]

so \(\alpha_j>1\), \(\beta_j>1\), and the single interior mode is exactly \(m_j\). Define

\[
\widetilde W_j(x)=x^{\alpha_j-1}(1-x)^{\beta_j-1},
\qquad
W^{\mathrm{opp}}_j(\chi)=
\frac{\widetilde W_j(x_j)}{\widetilde W_j(m_j)},
\]

with \(W^{\mathrm{opp}}_j=0\) outside the open productive interval. The peak challenge is therefore

\[
\chi^{\mathrm{peak}}_j
=
\chi^{\mathrm{maint}}_j
+m_j(\chi^{\mathrm{stop}}_j-\chi^{\mathrm{maint}}_j).
\]

The excessive-load boundary remains an independently authored point satisfying \(\chi^{\mathrm{peak}}_j<\chi^{\mathrm{excess}}_j<\chi^{\mathrm{stop}}_j\); it controls accelerating harm, not the location of the opportunity peak. Channel and condition modifiers may move the interval, mode, or concentration only through declared bounded transforms that preserve the ordering constraints.

### Boundary rules

- The opportunity multiplier is continuous, non-negative, zero at both interval endpoints, and normalised to exactly `1.00` at its unique interior peak.
- Mode and concentration are the authored parameters; unconstrained \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) may not be fitted independently and then accepted without validation.
- A narrow productive window does not imply greater peak reward; width and maximum attainable opportunity remain separate.
- Fatigue and harm retain their own monotone or accelerating functions and are never inferred by subtracting opportunity from one.
- Numerical evaluation uses a log-domain implementation near the boundaries to avoid underflow without converting tiny positive opportunity into a discontinuous reward.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q14B parameters

- maintenance and stop boundaries, modes, concentrations, and excessive-load boundaries by channel;
- bounded modifiers for development, health, recovery, technique, novelty, equipment, Mana stability, and Soul stability;
- minimum reporting precision and log-domain numerical tolerances;
- separate fatigue and harm functions and their coupling to the same challenge coordinate;
- calibration targets for peak location, productive width, asymmetry, overload reversal, and session spacing;
- cross-channel constraints preventing implausibly narrow or broad windows from being used as hidden balance multipliers.

## CAL0-Q17B — Typed bounded condition transforms

### Transform contract

For channel \(j\), condition source \(c\), and baseline parameter vector

\[
\theta_j=(\chi^{\mathrm{maint}},\chi^{\mathrm{stop}},m,\kappa,H^{\max},\theta^{\mathrm{fat}},\theta^{\mathrm{harm}}),
\]

the registry declares an effect mask \(M_{c,j}\) and bounded transform \(\mathcal T_{c,j}\). A source may change only fields allowed by its mask. Transforms compose in a declared causal order:

\[
\theta'_{p,j}=\Pi_{\Omega_j}\!left(
\mathcal T_{c_n,j}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal T_{c_1,j}(\theta_j)
\right),
\]

where \(\Pi_{\Omega_j}\) is a validation projection or rejection rule for the admissible set

\[
\chi^{\mathrm{maint}}<\chi^{\mathrm{peak}}<
\chi^{\mathrm{excess}}<\chi^{\mathrm{stop}},
\quad m\in(0,1),\quad\kappa>2,\quad H^{\max}\ge0.
\]

Challenge-coordinate transforms, interval shifts or scaling, mode shifts, concentration changes, height multipliers, and fatigue or harm changes remain distinct operations. Technique may reroute demand between channels; poor recovery may narrow or lower productive opportunity; illness may raise fatigue and harm; equipment may reduce absolute demand on one structure while increasing it elsewhere. None of these effects is inferred from a universal condition multiplier.

### Boundary and anti-exploit rules

- Every transform is identity at neutral condition and continuous within its declared domain unless a real threshold process is explicitly modelled.

## CAL0-Q9B — Layered channel-specific organic-adaptation envelopes

### Three adaptation layers

Each channel \(j\) separates rapid functional calibration, slower structural remodelling, and foundation-limited sustainable integration. Let \(x^r_{p,j}\) be realised adaptation in layer \(r\in\{\mathrm{cal},\mathrm{str},\mathrm{sus}\}\), and let \(E^r_{p,j}>0\) be that layer's current soft envelope. A candidate diminishing-return term is:

\[
B^r_{p,j}
=
\left[
1-\frac{x^r_{p,j}}{E^r_{p,j}}
\right]_+^{\beta^r_j},
\qquad \beta^r_j>0.
\]

Consolidating opportunity is routed between the layers rather than copied into each one:

\[
\dot x^r_{p,j}
=
k^r_j\pi^r_{p,j}c_jB^r_{p,j}h_jI_j
-
\delta^r_j m^r_{p,j}x^r_{p,j},
\qquad
\sum_r\pi^r_{p,j}=1.
\]

The fast layer represents task-specific calibration, coordination, recruitment, attentional control, or equivalent low-structure functional change. The structural layer represents slower remodelling of tissues, neural organisation, cognitive schemas, Mana pathways, Soul boundaries, and other durable substrates. The sustainable layer represents the integrated, maintainable contribution permitted by current foundations and supporting structures. Only the compatible integrated projection contributes to the contingent organic attribute ledger:

\[
O_{p,i}
=
\sum_j S_{ij}\,
\mathcal I_j
\left(x^{\mathrm{cal}}_{p,j},x^{\mathrm{str}}_{p,j},x^{\mathrm{sus}}_{p,j};F_p\right).
\]

The routing fractions, integration function, and exact layer units remain calibratable. The topology is binding: rapid familiarity is not identical to structural growth, and neither becomes lasting rated capacity without compatible consolidation and integration.

### Moving envelopes

An envelope is current causal headroom, not a fixed genetic maximum and not a reward. Its supported target is:

\[
\widehat E^r_{p,j}
=
\mathcal E^r_j
(F_p,\text{health},\text{resources},\text{chronic compatible load},
\text{methods},\text{environment},\text{transformation}),
\]

and its movement is slower than filling the corresponding layer unless an explicit transformation changes the topology:

\[
\dot E^r_{p,j}
=
\nu^{r,+}_j[\widehat E^r_{p,j}-E^r_{p,j}]_+
-
\nu^{r,-}_j[E^r_{p,j}-\widehat E^r_{p,j}]_+.
\]

Maturation, supported chronic loading, recovery, nutrition, teaching, magical infrastructure, and earned restructuring can expand relevant headroom causally. Inactivity, ageing, deprivation, injury, incompatible concurrent load, or loss of supporting structures can shrink availability or the sustainable envelope. Merely increasing (E^r) grants no capacity: new headroom must still be filled through real stimulus, recovery, consolidation, and integration.

Within unchanged foundations, structures, methods, and environment, repeated solved practice approaches its current envelopes and increasingly becomes maintenance rather than indefinite stat growth. Very large long-term growth remains possible through cumulative remodelling, foundation development, new structures, new methods, magical or Soul development, bloodline expression, evolution, or explicit transformation; the model therefore imposes local causal bottlenecks rather than a universal mortal hard cap.

### Maintenance, detraining, and interaction boundaries

- Fast calibration normally rises and decays faster than structural remodelling; sustainable integration is the slowest and most foundation dependent.
- Maintenance stimulus may offset decay without producing measurable new rated capacity.
- Detraining removes unsupported organic adaptation according to its layer and channel; it does not revoke Skill levels, class levels, or their guaranteed reinforcement.
- Injury can reduce available expression, damage foundations, and lower or block envelopes. The pain or danger of injury is not itself an adaptation bonus.
- System reinforcement remains a separate guaranteed claim and assimilation pathway. Organic envelopes do not confiscate reinforcement, though both processes may compete for recovery and structural accommodation.
- An explicit transformation may replace envelope functions or topology, but its source, costs, conserved state, and discontinuities must be recorded.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q9B parameters

- layer units, routing fractions, integration functions, and diminishing-return exponents;
- channel-specific envelope target functions and foundation bottlenecks;
- timescale ordering, fill rates, motion rates, maintenance demand, and detraining constants;
- chronic-load history, recovery, interference, and injury effects on envelope movement;
- rules for plateaus, catch-up, breakthroughs, transformation, and post-transformation conserved adaptation;
- interaction between organic adaptation, natural maturation, temporary effects, and reinforcement assimilation.

## CAL0-Q12B — Continuous remodelling plus evidence-bearing phase transitions

### Ordinary envelope motion

Most envelope change remains continuous. Compatible consolidated load, foundation support, recovery, chronic history, and injury update each layer's envelope through the CAL0-Q9B motion rule. Merely filling an envelope, accumulating time, or approaching a round number does not create a transition.

### Structural transition record

A selected reorganisation may define a versioned transition \(b\) with:

- typed prerequisite state \(\mathbf P_b\), including required foundations, structures, chronic adaptation, methods, and safely resolved load;
- evidence ledger \(\mathcal L_b\) showing how those prerequisites were causally established;
- attempt event \(e_b\), with task, method, condition, assistance, risk, and provenance;
- transition resolution \(Y_b\in\{\text{failed},\text{partial},\text{successful},\text{maladaptive}\}\);
- mandatory recovery and stabilisation state \(R_b(t)\);
- post-event validation tests \(\mathcal V_b\);
- a declared state-transfer map \(\mathcal T_b\) applied only after successful validation.

Eligibility is necessary but insufficient:

\[
G_b(t)
=
\mathbf 1
\left[
\mathbf P_b(F,x,E,\mathcal L_b,\text{condition})=\mathrm{true}
\right].
\]

The transition commits only when the actor was eligible, a real attempt occurred, recovery completed, and validation succeeded:

\[
\mathrm{Commit}_b
=
G_b(t_e)
\land
(e_b\ \mathrm{occurred})
\land
(Y_b=\mathrm{successful})
\land
(R_b=\mathrm{complete})
\land
\bigwedge_{v\in\mathcal V_b}v.
\]

If committed, \(\mathcal T_b\) may alter envelope parameters, routing, coupling, maintenance cost, or structural topology. It does not itself add realised capacity unless the authored transition physically creates and validates such capacity; ordinarily it creates new headroom or reorganises how later adaptation can be integrated. Existing state is conserved, transferred, lost, or temporarily unavailable exactly as declared by \(\mathcal T_b\); silent duplication is prohibited.

Failed or partial attempts still produce their real fatigue, injury, learning, evidence, and recovery consequences, but grant no successful-transition state. A later attempt uses the updated history and cannot replay the earlier evidence as a second event. Explicit System evolutions, bloodline awakenings, divine interventions, species transformations, and other exceptional transformations remain separately typed even if they invoke the same state-transfer machinery.

### Boundary and anti-exploit rules

- A phase transition is a rare structural process, not a mandatory tier attached to every plateau or attribute band.
- Prerequisite completion creates eligibility, not guaranteed success and not immediate capacity.
- Transition probability or resolution cannot be rerolled by renaming the same uninterrupted attempt, reloading presentation state, or splitting one event into fragments.
- Recovery and validation occur in world time and can reveal partial, unstable, or maladaptive outcomes.
- Failure cannot be made optimal through guaranteed consolation growth; only causally produced learning or adaptation is retained.
- The transition ledger records costs, assistance, damage, conserved state, and changes in future maintenance demand.

- which structural reorganisations justify phase transitions rather than continuous remodelling;

## CAL0-Q15B — Deterministic latent-state resolution with epistemic uncertainty

Each transition attempt \(e_b\) receives an immutable provenance identity and a fully specified latent resolution state

\[
\Lambda_{b,e}
=
\mathcal R_b
(F,x,E,\mathcal L_b,\text{condition},\text{method},

\]

The deterministic resolver maps that state to the authored outcome set:

\[
Y_{b,e}=\mathcal D_b(\Lambda_{b,e})

\]

If execution contains fine-grained processes modelled through a seeded solver, the attempt seed is derived once from world seed, entity identity, transition identity, and immutable attempt identity. \[
\widehat P_{o,t}(Y=y)
=
\mathcal E_{b,o}(I_{o,t}),
\qquad
\sum_y\widehat P_{o,t}(Y=y)=1.

## CAL0-Q18B — Constraint-first multicriteria transition resolver

### Resolution topology

For transition family \(b\), evaluate an attempt in ordered stages:

1. **Identity and admissibility:** confirm one immutable attempt, permitted method, and valid target transition.
2. **Hard constraints:** evaluate required structures \(G_b\), categorical incompatibilities \(I_b\), and non-compensable safety minima.
3. **Typed dimensions:** only if admissible, calculate bounded vectors for readiness \(\mathbf r\), execution \(\mathbf e\), immediate stability \(\mathbf s\), and recoverability \(\mathbf v\).
4. **Outcome rule:** apply the registered multicriteria decision surface without collapsing the vectors to one compensatory average.
5. **Recovery and validation:** commit successful, partial, failed, or maladaptive state only after the evidence required by CAL0-Q12B exists.

Formally,

\[
A_{b,e}=\mathbf1[G_b=\mathrm{true}]\mathbf1[I_b=\mathrm{false}],
\]

\[
Y^{\mathrm{provisional}}_{b,e}=
\begin{cases}
\mathcal F_b(\text{failed or maladaptive cause}), & A_{b,e}=0,\\
\mathcal M_b(\mathbf r,\mathbf e,\mathbf s,\mathbf v), & A_{b,e}=1.
\end{cases}
\]

Each \(\mathcal M_b\) may use conjunctive minima, vetoes, and limited declared trade-offs inside compatible dimensions. It may not permit exceptional strength in one dimension to compensate for an absent required structure or a vetoed incompatibility. Determinism and observer uncertainty remain governed by CAL0-Q15B.

### Boundary and anti-exploit rules

- Hard constraints are evaluated before scores and preserve the precise failure cause; an ineligible attempt cannot become eligible through averaging.
- Partial success must name the structures changed, unchanged, unstable, or damaged. It is not a generic fraction of full success.
- Maladaptation requires a causal adverse change, not merely a low score or narrative punishment.
- Failed eligibility, failed execution, failed stabilisation, and failed recovery remain distinguishable for learning, treatment, future attempts, and forecasts.
- A provisional success cannot grant committed headroom or capacity before recovery and validation.
- Resolver versions, inputs, vetoes, branch decisions, and output provenance are recorded for replay and adversarial audit.

## CAL0-Q3A — Smooth power-law cumulative XP

### Governing curve

For accepted lineage form \(s\) with \(N_s\) reinforcement-bearing thresholds, define cumulative XP required to reach threshold \(\ell\) as:

\[
T_s(\ell)
=
K_s\left(\frac{\ell}{N_s}\right)^{p_s},
\qquad
0\leq \ell\leq N_s,
\qquad p_s>1.
\]

The XP cost of one threshold is therefore:

\[
\Delta T_{s,\ell}
=
K_s\left[
\left(\frac{\ell}{N_s}\right)^{p_s}
-
\left(\frac{\ell-1}{N_s}\right)^{p_s}
\right].
\]

Where:

- \(K_s\) is the form-local cumulative XP scale;
- \(p_s\) is the smooth steepness exponent;
- \(N_s\) is normally `100` but may differ without changing the complete reinforcement budget.

This curve controls numerical accumulation only. XP remains lineage-local and arises from the provenance-preserving recognition function already fixed by SKL1.1D or CLS1.2D:

\[
\Delta X_s=\mathcal R_s(E,H_s).
\]

Threshold \(\ell\) is earned only when both conditions hold:

\[
X_s\geq T_s(\ell)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathcal G_{s,\ell}(H_s)=\text{true},
\]

where \(\mathcal G_{s,\ell}\) is the qualitative attestation gate over the lineage's evidence history and mastery profile. Numerical XP cannot purchase missing method coverage, judgement, independence, integration, reliability, safe termination, or another required claim.

The curve does not:

- award XP before a lineage is accepted;
- make XP transferable or comparable as a universal currency;
- convert ordinary maturation or organic adaptation into XP;
- grant a level without its evidence gate;
- multiply XP merely because an event is dangerous, rare, or dramatic;
- change the fixed reinforcement budget delivered by the form's levels.

## CAL0-Q5B — Type-and-grade XP scaling

### Governing scale

The cumulative curve remains smooth, but its total scale is resolved by lineage form type and evolutionary grade:

\[
K_s
=
K^{(0)}_{\tau(s)}\,\Gamma_{\tau(s)}(g_s),
\qquad
\Gamma_\tau(0)=1,
\qquad
\Gamma_\tau(g+1)>\Gamma_\tau(g),
\]

where \(\tau(s)\in\{\mathrm{Skill},\mathrm{Class}\}\) and \(g_s\) is the form's evolutionary grade. The initial candidate implementation is geometric:

\[
\Gamma_\tau(g)=\gamma_\tau^g,
\qquad \gamma_\tau>1,
\]

but \(K^{(0)}_{\mathrm{Skill}}\), \(K^{(0)}_{\mathrm{Class}}\), \(\gamma_{\mathrm{Skill}}\), and \(\gamma_{\mathrm{Class}}\) remain uncalibrated. A registered monotone grade table may replace the geometric candidate if deterministic and cohort tests show that one multiplier cannot represent later grades.

Rarity is deliberately absent from \(K_s\). A rarer accepted form may have more unusual prerequisites, evidence profiles, dependencies, risks, or reinforcement, but rarity alone does not slow XP accumulation. Individually authored lineage modifiers are also absent from the base model; distinctiveness primarily enters through evidence recognition and qualitative attestation.

The full threshold function is therefore:

\[
T_s(\ell)
=
K^{(0)}_{\tau(s)}\Gamma_{\tau(s)}(g_s)
\left(\frac{\ell}{N_s}\right)^{p_s}.
\]

Evolution to a successor creates a new prospective form-local curve. Parent XP, incomplete thresholds, grade, rarity, or unearned progress do not transfer unless an explicit conserved rule already permits it.

## CAL0-Q6C — Provenance-weighted blocked-XP taper

### Governing cap and taper

Let \(L_s\) be the highest completed threshold in a form. If numerical XP reaches \(T_s(L_s+1)\) while the qualitative gate remains false, the lineage enters the ATTESTATION_BLOCKED state. Its temporary XP ceiling is:

\[
X^{\max}_s(L_s)
=
T_s(L_s+1)
+
\omega_s\,\Delta T_{s,L_s+2},
\qquad 0\leq\omega_s\leq1,
\]

with the terminal-form boundary handled separately. Thus the maximum overhang is no more than one following threshold cost and cannot by itself bank several future levels.

For an incoming evidence record \(e\), ordinary recognition first calculates raw lineage-local XP \(\Delta X^{\mathrm{raw}}_{s,e}\). While blocked, define:

\[
z_s
=
\operatorname{clamp}
\left(
\frac{X_s-T_s(L_s+1)}
{\omega_s\Delta T_{s,L_s+2}},
0,1
\right),
\]

\[
\Delta X^{\mathrm{blocked}}_{s,e}
=
\min\!\left(
\Delta X^{\mathrm{raw}}_{s,e}
r_{s,e}(1-z_s)^{q_s},
X^{\max}_s-X_s
\right),
\qquad q_s>0.
\]

Here \(r_{s,e}\in[0,1]\) is a provenance-derived relevance weight for genuine development still pertinent to the lineage. It is not a discretionary bonus. Evidence that merely repeats an already-satisfied dimension, is outside the lineage, or attempts to substitute quantity for the missing attestation dimension receives \(r_{s,e}=0\). Evidence can satisfy the missing qualitative gate through its actual content even when it receives little or no numerical XP.

When the gate becomes true, the System awards exactly the now-dual-satisfied threshold, retains any exact overhang, then reevaluates the next threshold and its own gate. The event is not replayed, and the bounded overhang cannot cause an automatic multi-level cascade. At the cap, further events remain in the evidence history and may change attestation, offers, or diagnosis, but add no formal XP until the block resolves.

If \(\omega_s=0\), the selected family reduces locally to a hard numerical pause while still preserving evidence. Division by zero is therefore avoided by bypassing \(z_s\) and crediting zero blocked XP.

- the deterministic relevance rubric used to calculate \(r_{s,e}\);
- terminal-threshold behaviour and the relationship to successor offers;
- UI disclosure of blocked XP, capped XP, missing evidence, and uncertainty.

## CAL0-Q26B — Counterfactual ablation ledger

Every registered interaction \(r\) carries a versioned ledger evaluated over declared scenarios \(s\). \[
\Delta_{r,s}=L(A_{r,s},y_s)-L(J_{r,s},y_s),
\]

where \(L\) is a registered scenario-appropriate loss or validation measure. Retention requires the interaction to remain causally admissible, materially useful in its declared scope, stable under sensitivity tests, and non-degrading at protected boundaries. Failure places the term into `review`, `restricted`, or `retired` state according to authored rules. ## CAL0-Q27B — Typed lexicographic commit policy

Candidate dependency-closed safe subsets are compared by an ordered tuple whose dimensions retain their types:

\Lambda(C)=\bigl(h(C),i(C),o_1(C),\ldots,o_m(C),s(C),d(C),\tau(C)\bigr).
\]

Hard safety and identity validity \(h,i\) are feasibility conditions rather than compensable scores. Among feasible subsets, the transition definition supplies an ordered list of declared objectives \(o_1\ldots o_m\); stability then dominates dependency coverage, and the immutable canonical subset identity \(\tau\) breaks any remaining tie. Comparison stops at the first unequal dimension. A lower-priority total, however large, cannot compensate for losing a higher-priority critical structure. ## CAL0-Q28B — Evidence-gated versioned reallocation

An overlap-reservation partition is immutable inside its parameter-set version. A proposed reallocation from source bucket \(u\) to destination bucket \(v\) records a signed transfer \(\delta_{u\rightarrow v}\), its causal justification, ablation ledger, sensitivity envelope, factor-identity test, residual-sufficiency test, and affected scopes. The successor partition is

\[
B^{(n+1)}_{k,j}=B^{(n)}_{k,j}+\sum_u\delta_{u\rightarrow j}-\sum_v\delta_{j\rightarrow v},
\qquad
B^{(n+1)}_{k,j}\ge 0,
\qquad
\sum_j B^{(n+1)}_{k,j}\le B_k.
\]

No transfer commits unless every registered gate passes and the successor preserves the component ceiling, minimum primary reservation, minimum residual reservation, permitted signs, and declared factor identity. The reallocation creates a new parameter-set version with explicit parentage; it never mutates the old binding, rewrites historical manifests, or grants capacity to a person. Runs continue to resolve against the partition named in their manifest. A failed proposal records its evidence and rejection reason without partially moving capacity.

## CAL0-Q29B — Hierarchical scope-specific interaction retention

Each interaction retains one global causal identity \(r\) and a versioned status map over a declared scope lattice: species, population, life stage, condition, and scenario. A scope node may be `active`, `restricted`, `retired`, or `unsupported`. Resolution walks from the exact requested scope toward its ancestors and applies the nearest admissible binding only when its evidence-support predicate covers the request:

\[
\operatorname{status}(r,s)=
\operatorname{nearest}\{b\preceq s:\operatorname{covers}(E_{r,b},s)=1\}.
\]

Child evidence may restrict or retire an inherited term locally without cloning its identity or changing sibling scopes. Evidence at a child does not flow upward automatically, and evidence at an ancestor cannot authorise extrapolation into a child whose support conditions are untested. `Unsupported` means no interaction contribution is applied; it is not evidence of zero causal effect. Every resolved contribution retains the identity, source binding, inherited path, parameter version, and applicable boundary tests.

## CAL0-Q30B — Typed template with constrained refinement

Every structural-transition family instantiates a registered objective template. The universal prefix `hard safety`, then `identity validity`, is fixed and non-compensable. \Lambda_f(C)=\bigl(h(C),i(C),o_{f,1}(C),\ldots,o_{f,m_f}(C),s(C),d(C),\tau(C)\bigr).

A transition family may omit irrelevant optional objectives or refine their order only through a versioned template binding with rationale, fixtures, and approval provenance. ## CAL0-Q31B — Directed acyclic parameter-set version graph

Parameter sets form an immutable directed acyclic graph (G_P=(V_P,E_P)), not a mutable sequence and not an unordered collection of alternatives. Every node (P_v) records its stable identity, complete parent set, declared scope, binding delta, compatibility contract, review provenance, and replay boundary:

\[
P_v=\bigl(id_v,\operatorname{parents}(v),S_v,\Delta_v,K_v,R_v\bigr),
\qquad
(u,v)\in E_P \Longrightarrow u\notin\operatorname{descendants}(v).
\]

A branch may specialise a parent for a declared population, scenario, mechanism, or calibration experiment without becoming the universal successor. A merge node has at least two parents and a declared common ancestor. Its candidate bindings are produced by a typed three-way comparison against that ancestor; every same-binding conflict, changed invariant, scope collision, and compatibility claim must be resolved or explicitly rejected before the node can commit:

\[
P_m=\operatorname{merge}(P_a,P_b\mid P_l,K_m),
\qquad
P_l\in\operatorname{ancestors}(a)\cap\operatorname{ancestors}(b).
\]

Shared ancestry does not itself prove replay, output, migration, compositional, or merge compatibility. Those are separate claims inside (K_v), each with its tested direction, scope, tolerances, and excluded observables. No run selects a parameter set through an implicit `latest`, `nearest`, or `best match` rule: its manifest names one exact node. Adding a branch or reviewed merge therefore cannot change an earlier manifest, reinterpret an old binding, or alter a character state.

## CAL0-Q32B — Conservative partial evidence pooling

Evidence may cross scope boundaries only through a directed, registered support relationship from source scope (t) to target scope (s). The relationship records its causal justification, typed path, maximum support distance, admissible observables, transfer uncertainty, effective-sample ceiling, boundary tests, and conflict predicate. Pooling is forbidden when any required relationship is absent, the target lies outside declared support, a protected boundary fails, or local and transferred effects conflict beyond the registered tolerance.

For an admissible path, attenuation (a_{t\rightarrow s}\in(0,1]) reduces precision while an explicit transfer variance (v^{\mathrm{tr}}_{t\rightarrow s}\ge0) widens the source uncertainty:

\[
\widetilde v_{t\rightarrow s}
=\frac{v_t}{a_{t\rightarrow s}^{2}}+v^{\mathrm{tr}}_{t\rightarrow s},
\qquad
\widetilde n_{t\rightarrow s}
=\min\!\left(n^{\max}_{t\rightarrow s},a_{t\rightarrow s}^{2}n_t\right).
\]

The target estimator may combine admitted contributions only through its registered typed model, subject to a total borrowed-effective-sample cap. It must preserve source event identities so common evidence arriving along two paths is deduplicated rather than counted twice. Direction is not implied to be symmetric: child-to-adult, adult-to-child, population-to-species, or scenario-to-general transfer requires its own supported edge. Rejected, conflicting, or `unsupported` evidence contributes nothing to the target estimate but remains in the provenance ledger; this is not a claim of zero effect.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q32B parameters

- transfer-variance floors, per-source and total effective-sample caps, and uncertainty coverage targets;
- conflict tests, multiple-source reconciliation, deduplication, and protected-boundary tolerances;

## CAL0-Q33B — Partial-order objective comparison

Each registered objective (o) supplies a typed preorder only over values it can meaningfully compare. The relation (x\succeq_o y) may therefore return `better`, `equivalent`, `worse`, or `incomparable`; `incomparable` is not converted to equality, zero difference, or a hidden numerical weight. Safety and identity feasibility remain fixed prior constraints.

For the candidate set surviving those constraints, comparison proceeds through the frozen objective template. At each tier, strict typed dominance may remove a candidate only when the relevant values are comparable. Candidates on a genuine partial-order frontier remain eligible for the registered transition-family resolver (R_f):

\[
\mathcal F_o(X)=\{x\in X:\nexists y\in X\text{ with }y\succ_o x\},
\qquad
X' = R_f\!\left(\mathcal F_o(X),o,K_f\right).
\]

The resolver is versioned and frozen before candidate outcomes are known. It may apply only registered, type-preserving, non-compensating rules; it cannot reverse an earlier strict dominance result, consult an undeclared lower-priority scalar proxy, or move any result ahead of safety or identity. If governed rules exhaust without selecting one candidate, the immutable canonical subset identity supplies the final deterministic tie-break. Every comparison records which pairs were comparable, which were incomparable, which rule narrowed the frontier, and which version produced the result.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q33B parameters

## CAL0-Q34B — Typed directional compatibility matrix

Compatibility is recorded as a typed claim about one directed use of one parameter set with another, not as one property inherited from shared ancestry. Let

\[
C_d(P_a\!\rightarrow\!P_b\mid s,O,\tau,v)
\in
\{\texttt{supported},\texttt{conditional},\texttt{incompatible},\texttt{unsupported}\},
\]

where \(d\) is the compatibility dimension, \(s\) is the declared scope, \(O\) is the protected output or invariant set, \(\tau\) is the typed tolerance contract, and \(v\) identifies the witness-suite and claim versions. The initial dimensions are:

| Dimension | Claim being tested |
|---|---|
| Replay | The target execution environment reproduces the source manifest's protected outputs under the declared tolerances. |
| Observational comparison | Named outputs may be compared after declared mappings without implying identical internal semantics. |
| Migration | A registered directional transform carries admissible state into the target semantics while preserving named invariants. |
| Composition | Named components or outputs may coexist in one declared run without violating units, provenance, dependencies, or protected boundaries. |
| Merge | Version deltas may be combined through the reviewed common-ancestor and conflict-resolution process. |

Every matrix cell records its source and target versions, direction, scope, prerequisites, mappings, protected outputs, tolerances, witness fixtures, evidence, expiry or invalidation dependencies, and decision provenance. `Conditional` names requirements that must be satisfied by the requesting run; `unsupported` means no justified claim exists and is not evidence of incompatibility. A claim in one dimension never supplies another dimension, and no claim is symmetric or transitive unless a separate reviewed contract and witness suite establishes that exact direction and composition.

Historical manifests retain the compatibility-claim version they used. A later claim may permit a new comparison, migration, composition, or merge, but it cannot retroactively make an earlier run replay-compatible or change its outputs.

## CAL0-Q35B — Typed path attenuation with bottleneck and conflict gates

Evidence may cross several registered scope edges only when every edge supports the same observable and transfer purpose. For edge \(e_j\) on a directed path \(\pi=(e_1,\ldots,e_m)\), let \(a_{e_j,q}\in(0,1]\) be the attenuation for observable type \(q\), \(v^{\mathrm{tr}}_{e_j,q}\ge0\) its transfer-variance increment, \(n^{\max}_{e_j,q}\) its effective-sample ceiling, and \(g_{e_j,q}\) its combined support, boundary, and conflict gate.

An admissible path propagates uncertainty and effective support recursively:

\[
v_0=v_t,\qquad n_0=n_t,
\]

\[
v_j=\frac{v_{j-1}}{a_{e_j,q}^{2}}+v^{\mathrm{tr}}_{e_j,q},
\qquad
n_j=\min\!\left(n^{\max}_{e_j,q},a_{e_j,q}^{2}n_{j-1}\right),
\]

with total attenuation

\[
A_{\pi,q}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}a_{e_j,q}.
\]

If any \(g_{e_j,q}=0\), the entire path resolves `unsupported` or `conflicted` according to the failing gate and contributes nothing. Later edges cannot erase earlier transfer variance, restore support removed by a ceiling, widen an admissible observable set, or bypass a protected species, population, life-stage, condition, or scenario boundary. Direction and edge order remain part of the path identity.

Every admitted contribution preserves the source event identities used by CAL0-Q32B. Splitting one relationship into extra edges, routing the same evidence along several paths, or choosing a convenient path after observing the result cannot create new evidence. Selection or combination of multiple simultaneously admissible paths remains a separately governed decision.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q35B parameters

- edge attenuation, transfer-variance, effective-sample ceilings, and maximum path lengths by observable and scope dimension;
- canonical edge decomposition and equivalence rules that prevent path-length or path-splitting exploits;
- boundary and conflict tests, including whether any protected edge forces a stronger terminal status than `unsupported`;
- governed selection and aggregation when several non-dominated admissible paths connect the same source and target.

## CAL0-Q36B — Registered typed elimination cascade

After safety, identity, strict typed dominance, and the frozen objective template have produced a Pareto frontier \(F_0\), a transition-family cascade applies an ordered sequence of registered elimination predicates \(E_1,\ldots,E_r\). Each predicate declares its input types, family and scope, causal rationale, required evidence, preserved capabilities, invariance tests, and version before the attempt begins.

For each stage,

\[
\widehat F_j=E_j(F_{j-1}),
\qquad
F_j=
\begin{cases}
\widehat F_j,&\varnothing\ne\widehat F_j\subseteq F_{j-1}\text{ and the predicate is applicable},\\
F_{j-1},&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Thus a predicate may eliminate candidates but may not reintroduce them, reverse an earlier strict dominance result, aggregate incomparable values into a scalar, consult undeclared lower-priority information, or empty the frontier. `Inapplicable`, `no distinction`, and `invalid result` remain distinct logged outcomes. Evaluation stops when one candidate remains; if the frozen cascade exhausts with several candidates, immutable canonical subset identity provides the final fallback.

Every predicate must be invariant to proposal traversal, cosmetic identity, equivalent units, and rescaling or duplication of information outside its declared inputs. Changing predicate semantics, order, applicability, or version creates a new cascade version and cannot alter a recorded attempt.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q36B parameters

- the first registered predicate vocabulary, permitted input types, and transition-family templates;
- non-emptiness, monotonicity, renaming, unit, perturbation, and lower-priority invariance fixtures;
- handling of partially observed predicate inputs and observer-specific uncertainty without changing the latent outcome;
- review, retirement, and migration rules when a predicate or cascade version fails validation.

## CAL0-Q37B — Dependency-bound witness lifecycle

Every typed compatibility claim separates its compatibility verdict from the lifecycle state of the evidence supporting that verdict. A claim version \(K_v\) records its source and target parameter sets, direction, compatibility dimension, scope, protected outputs, mappings, tolerances, dependency fingerprints, witness-suite version, verdict, lifecycle state, and decision provenance:

\[
K_v=\bigl(id_v,P_a,P_b,d,s,O,M,\tau,D_v,W_v,c_v,\ell_v,R_v\bigr),
\]

where \(c_v\in\{\texttt{supported},\texttt{conditional},\texttt{incompatible},\texttt{unsupported}\}\) remains the CAL0-Q34B verdict and \(\ell_v\in\{\texttt{current},\texttt{stale},\texttt{invalid},\texttt{retired}\}\) is the independent witness-lifecycle state. A stale claim has evidence whose continued applicability must be re-established; it is not evidence that the parameter sets are incompatible. An invalid claim has lost a protected semantic basis and cannot authorise a new use. Neither state rewrites the original verdict or the historical attempts that cited it.

Every dependency edge declares a typed impact rule. Given a successor change set \(\Delta\), the impact on a claim is resolved only across dependencies reachable from \(D_v\):

\[
I(K_v,\Delta)=\bigvee_{x\in\operatorname{closure}(D_v)\cap\Delta}\rho_x(\operatorname{change}(x)),
\qquad
\rho_x\in\{\texttt{unaffected},\texttt{stale},\texttt{invalid}\}.
\]

An empty relevant intersection leaves the claim current. A witness implementation, evidence source, or permitted numerical dependency change may make it stale under its registered rule; a changed protected output, mapping semantics, direction, scope boundary, or invariant may invalidate it immediately. There is no release-wide invalidation unless the affected dependency set genuinely reaches every claim.

Revalidation executes the registered witness suite against the successor dependencies and creates a new immutable claim version. Passing may produce a new current verdict; failing records the witnessed failure without reviving the predecessor or mutating its history. New runs may rely only on a current claim satisfying their exact direction, dimension, scope, prerequisites, and manifest bindings. Historical replay continues to resolve the claim version and lifecycle evidence recorded when the original run was admitted.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q37B parameters

- the typed dependency vocabulary, transitive-closure rules, and semantic fingerprint construction;
- which change classes make each dependency unaffected, stale, or immediately invalid;
- revalidation witness coverage, expiry, review, and failure-to-verdict rules by compatibility dimension;
- presentation rules distinguishing current evidence, stale evidence, invalidated semantics, incompatibility, and lack of support.

## CAL0-Q38B — Provenance-aware bounded path set

For one source–target–observable transfer request, all candidate paths are enumerated from the registered directed support graph before the target estimate is observed. Paths failing a CAL0-Q35B support, boundary, purpose, or conflict gate are excluded with their terminal reason. Among the remainder, a path may be removed as dominated only when another path covers the same declared evidence and purpose with no weaker support, no greater propagated uncertainty, no lower effective-sample ceiling, no weaker protected-boundary guarantee, and at least one strict improvement.

The retained set is a deterministic, versioned selection

\[
\Pi^\star_q
=\operatorname{budgeted\_nondominated}
\bigl(\{\pi:g_{\pi,q}=1\},B^{\mathrm{path}}_q,R^{\mathrm{select}}_q\bigr),
\qquad
|\Pi^\star_q|\le B^{\mathrm{path}}_q.
\]

The selection rule, dominance relation, path budget, canonical route identities, and conflict policy are frozen before aggregation. Splitting an edge, adding an equivalent route, or selecting only after seeing the estimate cannot alter the admitted evidence.

Every retained path carries source-event identities and provenance-overlap metadata. Event-level support is counted once across the union \(\mathcal E^\star=\bigcup_{\pi\in\Pi^\star_q}\mathcal E_\pi\). Repeated arrival of event \(e\) may strengthen a registered corroboration assessment, but it cannot create another observation or another unit of effective sample size:

\[
n^{\mathrm{borrow}}_q
\le
\min\!\left(
n^{\max}_q,
\sum_{e\in\mathcal E^\star}w_e
\right),
\qquad
0\le w_e\le w^{\max}_e\le 1.
\]

Path and event weights are derived through a registered conservative dependence model; they are not summed as though routes were independent. Shared edges, sources, summaries, witnesses, and causal ancestors remain explicit overlap. Genuinely distinct evidence may contribute within per-source and total borrowing ceilings. A protected conflict propagates according to its registered scope and cannot be hidden by discarding an inconvenient path after outcomes are known. Unsupported and conflicted paths remain in the provenance ledger even when they contribute zero.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q38B parameters

- path-dominance relations, canonical route equivalence, and maximum retained-path budgets by observable;
- event, source, edge, witness, and causal-ancestor overlap representation;
- conservative dependence aggregation, corroboration credit, uncertainty widening, and effective-sample ceilings;
- conflict propagation, pre-outcome path freezing, and sensitivity tests against graph densification or edge splitting.

## CAL0-Q39B — Typed predicate registry with governed family templates

Each elimination predicate has one reusable, immutable registry identity and version. Its definition declares input and output types, admissible transition families and scopes, applicability conditions, causal rationale, required evidence, preserved invariants, permitted eliminations, uncertainty handling, diagnostics, and fixture suite. A transition-family template references exact predicate versions and a fixed order; it does not copy or redefine their semantics:

\[
E_v=\bigl(id_v,T_{\mathrm{in}},T_{\mathrm{out}},A_v,I_v,Q_v,W_v,R_v\bigr),
\qquad
\Theta_{f,u}=\bigl(id_u,f,[E_{v_1},\ldots,E_{v_r}],A_u,I_u,F_u,R_u\bigr).
\]

The compiled template must retain CAL0-Q36B's subset-narrowing contract at every stage. Before activation it must pass at least:

- **non-emptiness:** every applicable predicate leaves at least one valid candidate;
- **monotonicity:** a predicate only preserves or removes candidates and cannot restore an earlier elimination;
- **perturbation:** cosmetic renaming, equivalent units, traversal order, and changes outside declared inputs cannot alter its result, while declared decision boundaries are tested explicitly;
- **liveness:** the finite cascade terminates, distinguishes inapplicability from no distinction, and reaches either one candidate or the canonical fallback without a loop or deadlock.

Family templates may use only authorised predicates whose types, scopes, and preserved invariants compose. Universal safety, identity, strict dominance, and objective-template constraints remain outside and prior to the family cascade. A family cannot insert a bespoke predicate during an attempt, inspect candidate outcomes before choosing an order, or reinterpret `inapplicable` as permission to use undeclared information.

Changing predicate semantics, predicate order, family applicability, uncertainty treatment, or fallback creates a new version with behavioural witnesses and compatibility claims. Retirement prevents new template compilation but preserves old definitions for replay. Every attempt freezes its template and predicate versions before resolution and records every stage result.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q39B parameters

## CAL0-Q40B — Typed semantic dependency graph with registered impact rules

Compatibility evidence is governed by a versioned semantic dependency graph rather than by file reachability or whole-record hashes. A graph version records typed semantic nodes, directed dependency edges, canonical semantic projections, impact rules, and provenance:

\[
G^{\mathrm{sem}}_v=(V_v,E_v,\Phi_v,\mathcal R_v,R_v).
\]

Each node has a stable identity and separately fingerprinted semantic facets, including meaning, units and domain, direction, scope, mapping, protected invariants, executable behaviour, evidence basis, and witness implementation. Ordering, whitespace, display labels, comments, and equivalent serialisation remain logged changes but do not alter a semantic fingerprint unless a registered projection says that they carry meaning. An edge \(u\xrightarrow{\tau}x\) means that protected record \(u\) depends upon facet \(x\) through dependency type \(\tau\), such as `semantic`, `value-within-contract`, `mapping`, `scope`, `implementation`, `evidence`, or `witness`.

Every successor delta is classified before claim use. The initial change classes distinguish at least representation-only revision, value change within an existing contract, tolerance-boundary change, witnessed behaviour-preserving implementation replacement, unwitnessed implementation replacement, evidence revision, scope change, mapping or unit redefinition, protected-output change, and invariant change. Each traversed edge applies a registered rule that returns `unaffected`, `stale`, or `invalid` with an explanation witness. Path results compose in the strict order

\[
\texttt{unaffected}<\texttt{stale}<\texttt{invalid},
\qquad
I(K,\Delta)=\max_{\delta\in\Delta}\max_{\pi\in\operatorname{paths}(K,\delta)}
\operatorname{impact}_{\mathcal R_v}(\pi,\delta).
\]

Thus an invalid semantic path cannot be hidden by an unaffected route, while a representation-only change cannot stale an unrelated claim merely because both records share a release. Every result retains the changed facet, traversed path, edge rules, semantic fingerprints, severity, and required revalidation suite. Simultaneous changes are resolved as one immutable change set; enumeration order cannot change the result. A dependency cycle is invalid unless its nodes are explicitly contracted into one atomic semantic bundle with one fingerprint and one impact boundary.

`Unaffected` requires semantic equivalence under the registered projection; absence of an observed output difference is insufficient. `Stale` blocks new reliance until the named witnesses re-establish the claim. `Invalid` blocks new reliance immediately because a protected semantic basis no longer holds. These impacts update the independent lifecycle state defined by CAL0-Q37B and do not overwrite the claim's historical compatibility verdict or any manifest that already cited it.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q40B parameters

- the first semantic-facet, dependency-edge, and successor-change vocabularies;
- canonical projection, fingerprint, equivalence, and atomic-bundle rules;
- the complete typed impact table and cross-edge composition witnesses;
- claim-specific revalidation planning, explanation presentation, and change-set review thresholds.

## CAL0-Q41B — Provenance-overlap graph with bounded dependence-aware aggregation

Every retained CAL0-Q38B path is connected to the provenance elements that can make its evidence dependent upon another path. For request \(q\), the bipartite graph

\[
G^{\mathrm{prov}}_q=(\Pi^\star_q,Z_q,E^{\mathrm{prov}}_q)
\]

links paths to typed nodes for source events, subjects or cohorts, collection processes, source institutions, summaries, transfer edges, witness suites, causal mechanisms, and shared ancestors. Event identities are still deduplicated before aggregation. The wider graph captures dependence that remains even when two paths contain different event IDs but share a source, instrument, summary, witness, or causal mechanism.

A versioned overlap registry compiles each path pair to justified correlation bounds \([\underline\rho_{ij},\overline\rho_{ij}]\). Exact duplicate evidence under semantically equivalent transforms receives perfect dependence and zero incremental support. Declared independent provenance may receive a bounded near-zero interval; missing provenance never defaults to independence. The admissible dependence set is

\[
\mathcal C_q=
\left\{
C=C^\mathsf T\succeq0:
C_{ii}=1,
\underline\rho_{ij}\le C_{ij}\le\overline\rho_{ij},
C\text{ satisfies every registered sign and block constraint}
\right\}.
\]

An empty set is a failed dependence specification, not permission to project or repair a convenient matrix silently. Path estimates \(x\), propagated standard deviations \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_m)\), and non-negative pre-outcome weights \(a\) are frozen with their source and total borrowing caps. The transferred estimate and conservative uncertainty are

\[
\widehat\mu_q=a^\mathsf T x,
\qquad
V^{\mathrm{borrow}}_q=
\max_{C\in\mathcal C_q}a^\mathsf T DCDa+u^2_{\mathrm{struct},q}.
\]

The effective support may not exceed the unique-event ceiling from CAL0-Q38B, any source or target cap, or the dependence-limited design effect:

\[
n^{\mathrm{eff}}_q
\le
\min\!\left(
n^{\mathrm{event}}_q,
n^{\max}_q,
\frac{(\sum_i a_i)^2}{\max_{C\in\mathcal C_q}a^\mathsf TCa}
\right).
\]

Weights cannot be negative, cancel conflicts, or be selected after observing transferred effects. A protected conflict is resolved before aggregation and remains in the ledger even when its contribution is zero. Adding duplicate routes, splitting an edge, renaming provenance nodes, or densifying the graph with semantically equivalent paths cannot reduce the conservative variance or raise effective support. Sensitivity reports must show both the selected overlap bounds and the worst admissible dependence case.

### Unresolved CAL0-Q41B parameters

- the first provenance-node types, equivalence rules, and graph-construction contracts;
- overlap-to-bound compilation, unknown-dependence defaults, and PSD-feasibility rules;
- path-weight, source-cap, structural-uncertainty, and effective-support policies;
