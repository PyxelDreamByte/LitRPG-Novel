# Author Authority

## Constitutional rule

The Author is the sole final authority over:

- canon and continuity;
- premise, plot, endings, themes, and reader promises;
- character identity, motivation, arcs, and voice;
- prose acceptance and publication readiness;
- worldbuilding decisions and retcons;
- CAL0 successor changes, story-specific System extensions, and calibration choices;
- exceptions to repository workflow.

No agent, council, script, reviewer, majority vote, test result, or generated artifact can substitute for Author acceptance.

## Delegated work

Agents may:

- research and summarise;
- generate alternatives;
- draft plans, prose, state deltas, and change proposals;
- identify contradictions, risks, and implications;
- run deterministic validation;
- recommend acceptance, revision, deferral, or rejection.

Delegation is bounded by the task contract. It grants permission to propose or edit the stated work product, not to decide canon outside it.

## Reserved gates

The Author must explicitly approve:

1. A new or changed binding decision.
2. A chapter and its complete canon/state delta.
3. A retcon or contradiction resolution.
4. A change to CAL0 or a story-specific rule that could be mistaken for CAL0 canon.
5. A branch merge into `main` when it changes accepted creative material.
6. Final publication text.

## Council rule

Councils advise. The orchestrator records independent findings, exposes material conflicts, and presents a synthesised recommendation. Reviewers do not vote fiction into canon, and the reviser does not approve its own work.

Silence, absence of objections, passing tests, or an agent marking work complete never counts as Author approval.
