# The Sealed Step

Five minutes before the storm, the bronze east-vault key would not turn.

Nera set her lamp on the dry step and leaned close. White salt furred the shutter's keyway. The ordinary procedure was to fetch a wire brush, tell the senior keeper, and wait. Waiting would leave the sealed stacks to the first surge.

She scraped the salt out with the corner of her chalk. The key moved a finger's width, stopped, then turned under both hands.

The shutter opened on a breath of cold mineral air. Beyond it, the east stair descended six steps to a narrow shelf. Black water trembled below the shelf, already nosing through the lowest courses of stone. Nera kept one hand on the wall and her eyes above the water. She did not need to swim. She needed to be quick.

Her lamp found the tablet wedged behind the shutter's counterweight: fired glass, clouded grey, no larger than her palm. It should not have been there. If the counterweight dropped, it would crush the tablet. If she left the shutter open, the storm would reach the sealed stacks.

Nera lifted the glass free.

A hairline mark brightened beneath her thumb. Not words—not any catalogue hand she knew—but three pale lines that joined, separated, and joined again when she tilted the tablet toward the lamp. She could not tell what they meant. She could tell the response came from the tablet.

Water slapped the stone below.

She could set the tablet on the shelf and close the shutter. That was procedure. It was also a lie dressed as obedience: the next surge would take the shelf, and the archive would lose whatever record had been hidden here.

Nera put the tablet in her empty satchel.

The counterweight groaned as she hauled the shutter closed. The bronze key came free warm from her grip. Chalk, lamp, key, tablet. She counted each thing before she climbed the east stair.

At the top, less than five minutes had become less than one.

Boots struck the public gallery above—fast, descending, and too heavy to belong to the storm. Someone had seen her lamp below the shutter.

Nera tightened the satchel strap across her chest and kept climbing toward them.
