## Option A — STRAND-BRAID

Several persistent origin strands keep distinct household traditions —
foodways, teaching lines, memory-keeping, work customs — while daily work,
defence, and seasonal festivals braid them together under negotiated common
institutions. No strand dissolves and no strand dominates; households remain
recognisably of a strand, but almost every working crew, defence muster, and
festival is deliberately mixed. The strand classes present are qualitative
classes, not named cultures: locally rooted pre-collapse families; displaced
arrivals from failed districts; descendants of ex-imperial functionaries or
garrison personnel; and itinerant-craft or trade lineages. The class list is
open-ended, and individual households may straddle classes by marriage or
fostering.

Daily rhythm ties the braid together. Lake-road haulage within
WLD-LOCATION-002's fair-weather bands is crewed across strands: a loaded
shore round trip consumes most of a working day, and in surge seasons —
when haulage, watches, and field work compete for the same days — mixed
crews are the negotiated, deliberate compact-era custom, not an arithmetic
necessity. The spring abundance pulse (WLD-ECOLOGY-006) and the
twice-yearly crown-corridor seasons (WLD-ECOLOGY-004) impose settlement-wide
closures, watch duties, and labour surges that cut across household lines;
the festivals that mark those seasons are the braid's most visible common
institutions, with strand traditions contributing distinguishable strands to
shared observances rather than holding separate ones.

Views of the imperial inheritance run as a lived spectrum distributed across
and within strands: salvage-pragmatism (reuse what works), nostalgia (the
peace and roads are missed), resentment (what the retreat abandoned or what
the power once did), and taboo (some works and records are better left
alone). Functionary-descended households lean toward nostalgia and literate
salvage but contain resenters; locally rooted families lean pragmatic but
keep taboos; no strand speaks with one voice. System practice likewise varies
by class of use rather than by strand: everyday pragmatic use (most people,
most professions); hoarded craft advantage (some craft and trade lineages
treat hard-won interface readings and training sequences as household
property); specialist study (a few individuals across strands compare notes
and records); and folk belief and misinterpretation (widespread, strand-
flavoured, often wrong). Every such claim sits on the belief, access,
interpretation, or presentation layer — the System's natural behaviour is
unchanged, no readout is guaranteed, and no strand's account is privileged.

## Option B — CRUCIBLE

One emergent settlement identity dominates public life. Thirty years of
shared walls, shared closures, and shared losses have forged a shared
self-understanding rooted in the place itself (an author-facing gloss —
the actual self-designation, if any, remains a protected unknown) that
governs work, defence, festivals, and public speech; origin traditions persist mainly privately — in households, in
kitchen practice, in bedtime memory — and are generationally fading. The same
open-ended strand classes exist (locally rooted pre-collapse families,
displaced arrivals, ex-imperial functionary or garrison descendants,
itinerant-craft or trade lineages), but publicly they matter less each year;
the founding generation carries them, the walls-born generation increasingly
does not, and that gap is itself a live tension as the first succession
approaches.

Daily rhythms are the crucible's forge. Haulage crews, corridor-season
watches, pulse-season labour surges, and terrace and field work are organised
by task and capability, not origin, and the settlement's common festivals
commemorate shared events of the founding period rather than any strand's
older calendar. Imperial-inheritance attitudes still span the full
salvage-pragmatism / nostalgia / resentment / taboo spectrum, but in public
they are compressed toward a serviceable settlement consensus of pragmatic
salvage, with nostalgia, resentment, and taboo surviving as private,
household-level positions that surface under stress.

System practice shows the same compression: a broad everyday-pragmatic
common stock of usage lore is publicly taught and treated as "how everyone
does it," while hoarded craft advantage persists quietly inside lineages,
specialist study is rare and individual, and folk belief converges on a
settlement-common body of story and error faster than in the other options.
The convergent lore is belief and presentation, not natural law: a widely
shared misinterpretation is still a misinterpretation, and the crucible
grants no one better access or truer readouts than the braid or the mosaic
would.

## Option C — QUARTER-MOSAIC

Origin communities cluster spatially — by neighbourhood within the walls and
by customary working areas on the apron — and recognised brokers or
intermediaries mediate between them. Each cluster keeps its traditions,
teaching, and memory largely intact; common institutions exist but are thin,
and much practical coordination runs through broker households trusted on
two or more sides. The same open-ended strand classes apply, but here they
have addresses: a cluster of locally rooted families, a displaced-arrivals
cluster (itself internally diverse), a functionary-descended cluster, and
itinerant-craft households who are themselves natural brokers because their
work crosses every boundary. No cluster boundary, quarter map, or building
is fixed by this option — clustering is a qualitative social pattern, not a
settlement plan.

Daily rhythms are negotiated rather than fused. Lake-road haulage days,
corridor-season closures, and pulse-season labour surges require cross-
cluster agreement each season, brokered turn-taking, and occasional disputes;
festivals are cluster-held with reciprocal guesting rather than common.
Imperial-inheritance attitudes are more visibly sorted than in A or B — one
cluster's salvage-pragmatism sits beside another's taboo, and the
functionary-descended cluster's nostalgia is a standing irritant to
neighbours who remember the retreat differently — yet each cluster still
contains its own internal spectrum; sorting is a tendency, not a wall.

System practice sorts the same way: clusters maintain partially distinct
usage lores, teaching customs, and folk beliefs; hoarded craft advantage is
strongest here because lineage knowledge doubles as cluster capital;
specialist study is fragmented, with rival interpretive habits in different
clusters; and brokers accumulate unusual comparative knowledge of how
differently the same System behaviour is read across the settlement. All of
it remains belief, access, interpretation, and presentation. No cluster's
lore is the true one, and broker knowledge is comparative and fallible, not
privileged access.
