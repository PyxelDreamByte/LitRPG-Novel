## Shared constraints shown with the options

All three options arrive after the mass breeder, rear mobile young for one warm
season, and escort survivors seaward. The young forage on mass-breeder eggs and
juveniles but also eat other prey. Family care reduces danger without
guaranteeing survival. No option establishes invisibility, a pooled family
level, a free Mana effect, or automatic population recovery.

## Options retained

### A — BROOD-VAULT STRIDER (selected)

The displayed card described a low, three-lobed living vault using a ring of
short hydrostatic paddle-columns and travelling margin waves. Mobile young
shelter beneath it, then spill outward to forage; the illustration caption
added that they regroup beneath the refuge.

Displayed strengths: moderate migration, excellent protection, excellent
visual contrast, and a mobile-refuge care style.

Displayed weakness: high drag and reliance on passable shallows or
bottom-resting stages through the outlet. This is retained as a feasibility
risk, not as approval of a route, a depth profile, bottom-resting behaviour, or
successful passage.

### B — HELIX-TOW ESCORT (not selected)

A stiff spindle swims through travelling waves along three helical muscle-bands.
Young forage in a patrolled court and temporarily grip finite breakaway
tow-filaments in dangerous water.

Displayed strengths: excellent migration, moderate protection, excellent
visual contrast, and a convoy-escort care style.

Displayed weakness: towing adds drag and entanglement risk; exposed stragglers
and severed filaments create hard parental choices.

### C — FOLD-CAGE ROSETTE (not selected)

A stiff central barrel carries seven muscular vane-ribs. They wave for
propulsion, fold into a migration spindle, and flare into a perforated refuge
while young rest or predators press.

Displayed strengths: good migration, strong protection, excellent visual
contrast, and a deployable-refuge care style.

Displayed weakness: operating the cage is expensive; crowding creates oxygen,
waste, parasite, and small-predator risks.
