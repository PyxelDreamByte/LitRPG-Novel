## Options retained

### A — MARGIN-RATCHET PROCESSION (selected)

The displayed card stated:

> Paddle-columns brace in successive groups while a margin wave drives a short
> advance. The vault settles low between surges. Autumn descent uses the same
> structures to brake and steer current-assisted slides.

Displayed qualitative rhythm:

`settle → ripple → brace → surge → settle`

Displayed metrics:

- Anatomy added: low
- Route demand: bed or margin chain
- Care pattern: regroup before advances
- Scene visibility: excellent

Displayed weakness: slow, conspicuous passage; abrasion, column injury,
fatigue, crowding, or a broken chain of resting opportunities can stop it.

The card's legend identified tracks, disturbed feeding trails, predictable
predator ambushes, and exposed transitions as possible consequences. Those are
retained as conditional design implications, not selected facts: they still
require suitable substrate, route traffic, predators, observation, and later
ecology or geography decisions.

### B — THREE-LOBE EDDY SLALOM (not selected)

The vault would move obliquely through deeper water, rotating between lobes as
it crossed from one lower-current pocket to another. Autumn descent would use
the main flow but repeatedly turn aside to recover.

Displayed qualitative rhythm: `angle → rotate → cross → recover`.

Displayed route demand: eddies or recovery pockets. Young would shift toward
the lee side during crossings. The main weakness was increased demand on
control, sensing, buoyancy, usable energy, and endurance; a failed pivot could
strip young from the refuge.

### C — VAULT-CHAIN LEAPFROG (not selected)

Temporary groups would alternate holding and advancing while young crossed
between overlapping undersides or protected wakes.

Displayed qualitative rhythm: `hold → advance → transfer → repeat`.

Displayed route demand: overlapping stations. The care pattern required
temporary refuge sharing and substantial social behaviour. The main weakness
was dependence on sufficient concurrent migrants, with crowding, disease,
panic, fatigue, and failed transfers as risks.
