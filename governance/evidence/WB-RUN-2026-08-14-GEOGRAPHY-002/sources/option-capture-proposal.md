## Options retained

### A — BEDROCK STEP-POOL CHAIN (not selected)

Low stone sills would alternate with shallow resting basins and deeper pools.
This would provide a durable and defensible route form, with debris jams and
low-water disconnection as major risks.

### B — ALTERNATING MARGIN SHELVES (not selected)

A swift central channel would be bordered by staggered ledges, backwaters, and
eddies. This would create strong lateral route dependence, predictable
crossings, possible ambush points, and serious crowding pressure.

### C — MIXED OUTLET SEQUENCE (selected)

A narrow bedrock throat opens into boulder steps, side shelves, and holding
pockets. The combination is geologically and ecologically varied while still
providing a coherent migration corridor for later feasibility modelling.

This selected wording establishes a connected mixed-reach physical template,
not permanent or universal passability.

## Selected feature boundary

The Author selected only the express content of option C:

1. the outlet corridor includes a narrow bedrock throat;
2. downstream of that throat, the corridor transitions into a mixed sequence
   containing boulder steps, lateral or side shelves, and holding pockets;
3. the outlet is therefore a connected mixed-reach corridor rather than one
   uniform chute, shelf stair, or regular step-pool chain;
4. those forms create qualitative physical heterogeneity and the potential for
   later habitat differentiation, without establishing any actual habitat or
   species distribution; and
5. the sequence supplies a qualitative opportunity set of bed-or-margin
   contact surfaces and potentially lower-velocity pockets for later
   migration-feasibility work, without proving a complete usable
   support-and-pause chain or guaranteeing that any species or individual can pass under
   any particular condition.

`Mixed outlet sequence`, `bedrock throat`, `boulder step`, `side shelf`,
`holding pocket`, and `coherent migration corridor` are author-facing
qualitative descriptions. They are not in-world names, exact survey classes,
engineered structures, or proof of a continuous safe route.

## Consistency and causal review

- This is a forward extension of `WLD-GEOGRAPHY-001`, not a retcon. It retains
  the freshwater through-flow lake, narrow defensible gap, downstream marsh
  country, layered river–glacial region, and seaward drainage.
- The selected landform sequence is compatible with a river corridor that has
  experienced resistant-bed constriction, glacial reworking, sediment and
  boulder movement, and later fluvial adjustment, but it does not choose which
  process formed each feature or import an Earth glacial chronology.
- `Bedrock` and `boulder` are broad material and size classes. Exact rock type,
  provenance, weathering, jointing, stability, mobility, mineral value, and
  Mana properties remain open.
- `Throat` establishes a narrow constricted reach, not a gorge, waterfall,
  cataract, cave, tunnel, dam, engineered gate, or fixed cross-section.
- `Boulder steps` are irregular bed transitions, not a regular staircase,
  constructed weir, fish ladder, or promise that every step is braceable.
- `Side shelves` are lateral landform surfaces. Their number, continuity,
  height, width, slope, material, exposure, inundation, vegetation, and use
  remain open; they are not roads, quays, terraces, or fortifications.
- `Holding pockets` are local channel recesses or pools that may be less
  exposed to the main flow under some water states. Their actual depth,
  current, turnover, respiratory suitability, food, sediment, crowding,
  predation, and usability depend on later hydrology and ecology. They are not
  guaranteed refuges, resting sites, recovery zones, or safe checkpoints.
- A coherent corridor means the landforms occupy one connected outlet system.
  It does not establish year-round passage, a complete usable support chain at
  every water level, safe rests, adequate route capacity, or successful spring
  ascent or autumn descent.
- `WLD-ECOLOGY-006` continues to govern the existence and qualitative timing of
  the two lake migrations. `WLD-ECOLOGY-010` continues to govern only the
  family-care migrant's organism-side passage choreography.
- Exact flow distribution, current speed, discharge, depth, turbulence,
  eddies, seasonal water levels, flood effects, ice, debris, erosion,
  deposition, tidal reach, salinity front, and storm-surge reach remain open.
- Exact route use by the mass breeder, family-care migrant, resident fauna,
  land animals, flying or bird-like organisms, humans, boats, or travellers
  remains open.
- The narrow gap remains only naturally defensible. No crossing, road, bridge,
  fort, gate, settlement, toll, ownership, border, fishery, trap, or law is
  established.
- Finite sediment, water, usable energy, habitat area, route capacity,
  disturbance, erosion, deposition, injury, mortality, recruitment, and

## First-order implications, not selected facts

- Different reaches can support distinct current, depth, substrate, shelter,
  feeding, attachment, and predation conditions once later records establish
  them.
- Boulder steps and shelves can retain or lose sediment, organic matter,
  tracks, eggs, juveniles, carcasses, or debris depending on actual flows and
  biology.
- Constrictions, shelves, and pockets can concentrate migrants, predators,
  fishers, observation, crowding, disease, injury, or conflict, but none of
  those events, practices, or locations is established here.
- Spring melt, sudden thaws, autumn flow, storms, and ice can change which
  parts of the sequence are usable, but their exact effects remain a later
  quantitative and seasonal gate.
- A human crossing or defensive position could exploit the throat, but its
  existence, design, controller, history, and relationship to the initial
  settlement require separate decisions.
