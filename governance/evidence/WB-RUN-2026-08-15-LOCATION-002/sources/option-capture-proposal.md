## Options retained

### A — CLOSE DAILY ORBIT (not selected)

- Downhill, unladen: **30–60 minutes**
- Uphill, laden: **1–1½ hours**
- Displayed effect: the lake becomes part of the settlement's ordinary daily
  orbit. Small fresh loads move frequently, and a delayed departure is
  inconvenient rather than automatically serious.
- Displayed trade-off: fast scene movement, but the road and intervening alien
  habitats carry less logistical and narrative weight.

### B — HALF-DAY WORKING ROUTE (selected)

- Downhill, unladen: **1–1½ hours**
- Uphill, laden: **2–3 hours**
- Displayed effect: a useful shore visit and loaded return consume most of a
  working day. Haulage, freshness, daylight, and delays matter, while the lake
  remains part of regular life.
- Displayed trade-off: best match for “fairly far but not too far”; the road
  becomes an alien ecological transect without making every visit an
  expedition.

### C — OUTER DAILY REACH (not selected)

- Downhill, unladen: **2–3 hours**
- Uphill, laden: **4–6 hours**
- Displayed effect: a laden lake trip occupies nearly the whole day. Short
  daylight, shore work, weather, or wildlife delays can threaten the same-day
  return.
- Displayed trade-off: strong travel stakes, but regular haulage begins to
  press toward unselected caches, shelters, transport animals, or lakeshore
  specialists.

Options A and C are retained only as nonbinding decision evidence. Their
journey bands, traffic rhythms, logistical pressures, infrastructure pressures,
and narrative effects are not setting facts.
