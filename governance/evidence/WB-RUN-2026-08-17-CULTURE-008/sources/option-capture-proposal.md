## Option A — DEAD-STANDARD COINS

Old imperial coin, in qualitative higher-worth and lower-worth metal classes,
still circulates as the region's one trusted movable medium. Its trust rests
on two legs — metal content and long familiarity — and on no surviving
authority: no functioning mint or treasury outlived the drawdown, and no
local power strikes coin anew. The stock only wears, clips, hides, and
slowly drains. Its sources are pre-drawdown household holdings, salvage
finds, and inflow through cross-lake exchange.

Daily exchange inside each settlement leans instead on tally-and-trust
credit: witnessed debts, remembered household obligation, and notched or
marked tallies (qualitative media only — no ledger institution, unit, or
record form is fixed). Coin serves storage of worth, once-off transfers,
and dealings between strangers — above all inter-settlement trade, where
obligation networks do not reach.

Cross-lake settlement in coin is exactly where WLD-EVENT-001's
contested-exchange-terms pressure bites: which coin classes count as good,
how worn is too worn, whose judgment tests a doubtful or clipped piece are
standing argument classes — tendencies, never occurred incidents. Whether
the outlet settlement's metalworking capability (WLD-LOCATION-005) lets it
test, re-melt, or even re-strike coin is an explicit open question this
option does not close; no minting or validation practice exists anywhere.

Value-culture texture: original-culture households tend to treat coin as a
stranger's medium and cold storage rather than the heart of household
worth; imperial-remnant tradition tends to treat coin as the residue of
legitimate order, its wear a daily reminder of what withdrew; the
hillfort's hybrid braid runs the full spectrum, negotiated crew by crew.
All tendencies with internal variation, never unanimity, and coin-facing
attitude only — where each culture locates worth at large is the
culture-frame proposals' space. Salvage coin from remnant ground means
dispersed single or small finds within WLD-LOCATION-004's salvage activity
class — finite, diminishing, contestable, with no right, law, market, or
price; whether any intact coin hoard survives there is an explicit open
question under that record's no-cache default, never an implied find.
Household hoarding is a live attrition channel: the trusted stock visibly
thins within living memory, and folk accounts of where the coin goes are
belief, not fact.

## Option B — WEIGHED-METAL AND TALLY

Coin faith broke with the empire: a coin buys nothing its metal would not.
The money-object classes are weighed metal in qualitative worth classes and
workable metal stock; old imperial coins persist as bullion (weight, not
face) or as keepsakes and memory-tokens. No count-based currency exists on
either shore.

Inside each settlement, notched-tally credit carries daily life: witnessed
debts, obligation kept in memory and in marked media, and in-kind return.
Fair-weighing knowledge enters disputes as sworn testimony through
WLD-CULTURE-002's existing expert-testimony channel — no weighing office,
standard, or new compact term is created.

Between the settlements, weighing settles trade, and the outlet settlement's
metalworking advantage (workable metal in its hills, water-power at its
nucleus, per WLD-LOCATION-005) makes it the natural supplier and validator
of weighed metal — a qualitative lever in the rivalry. Contested exchange
terms here run through whose balance, whose fire, and whose judgment of
fineness is trusted: pressure classes, never incidents, and capability,
never institution. Whether the outlet actually casts marked metal pieces —
a first step back toward coin — is an explicit open question.

Value-culture texture: original-culture households tend to treat weighed
metal as one store of worth among several, weighing merely formalising how
they already deal with strangers; imperial-remnant tradition holds the
broken coin faith across its full inheritance spectrum — mourned, salvaged
into bullion pragmatism, or repudiated — with some households keeping dead
coin as standing and memory; the hybrid braid negotiates, and coin
keepsakes cross strands by marriage and fostering. Salvage coin and metal
from remnant ground feed the bullion stream as ordinary salvage-class finds
under WLD-LOCATION-004: a found coin is a metal find, its imperial origin
cultural recognition rather than accurate knowledge.

## Option C — DUAL-SPHERE

Two spheres of worth ride one above the other. The external or prestige
sphere runs on surviving imperial coin (qualitative worth classes; sources:
pre-drawdown holdings, salvage finds, cross-lake inflow): inter-settlement
trade, storage of worth, prestige transfers, and — where a consented
communal burden is discharged in movable worth at all — the customary
medium of that discharge, without any levy, rate, treasury, or
compact-finance institution being established (WLD-CULTURE-002's burden
space stays open). The internal or customary sphere runs on obligation:
witnessed debts, labour-exchange, tally customs, and in-kind return inside
each settlement.

Converting between spheres is socially marked. Paying a neighbour in coin
reads as distancing or as ceremony; turning standing obligation into coin,
or coin into standing, is noticed, storied, and sometimes resented. This is
qualitative social meaning only — no rule, prohibition, or conversion rate
exists, and the marking itself varies by household and situation.

The external sphere is where WLD-EVENT-001's contested-exchange-terms
pressure lives: which coin classes both sides accept, wear and clipping
disputes, and competitive claims of standing expressed through coin
display, gift, and refusal — tendencies, never occurred incidents. The
outlet settlement, primarily imperial-remnant in culture, tends to treat
the coin sphere as native ground; the hillfort tends to treat it as an
instrument for dealing outward. New minting or metal-validation is an
explicit open question: the outlet's metal capability (WLD-LOCATION-005)
is the natural candidate, but no mint, assay, or validation institution
exists in this option.

Value-culture texture: original-culture households tend to keep their
dealings in the internal sphere and to use coin as a boundary tool for
strangers; imperial-remnant tradition tends to treat coin as a bearer of
record and standing, though never unanimously; in the hybrid braid,
fluency in both spheres is itself a braid skill, and households straddling
strands often broker conversions. Salvage coin from remnant ground means
dispersed single or small finds within WLD-LOCATION-004's salvage activity
class — finite, diminishing, contestable, no right or market; whether any
intact hoard survives there is an explicit open question under that
record's no-cache default, and salvage coin entering the external sphere
carries contestable provenance that fuels good-coin arguments without
settling any.

## Recommended option

Option C (dual-sphere). It lets the trade rivalry and settlement politics
both speak through money without inventing a single institution, gives
each of the three cultures a distinct, live relationship to the same coin
stock rather than a shared attitude, and keeps minting and validation open
as a future story lever for the outlet settlement's metal advantage.
