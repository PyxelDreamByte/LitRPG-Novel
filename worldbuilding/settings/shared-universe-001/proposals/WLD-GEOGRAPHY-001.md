# WLD-GEOGRAPHY-001 — Initial-Region Physical Scaffold

## Purpose

Establish enough physical causality to design an alien but realistic regional
ecosystem and to describe it from the protagonist's bounded perspective,
without importing Earth geography, species, cultures, or chronology.

## Existing constraints

- Setting authority: `WLD-SETTING-001A`
- Coupled basin ecology and wider horizon: `WLD-ECOLOGY-005`
- Existing lower-basin corridor and ridge-spur relationship: `WLD-ECOLOGY-004`
- Existing seasonal, flood, fire, and finite-recovery ecology:
  `WLD-FLORA-001`
- Work-local design source:
  `stories/series/project-hearthway-main/workbench/runs/2026-08-14-initial-region-physical-scaffold/proposal.md`
- Earth analogues are author-facing inspiration only.

## Proposed claims

1. The immediate region is broadly arranged as wet rocky uplands in the west
   and northwest and a sheltered central lake basin. East and southeast of the
   outlet gap, downstream lower marsh country leads to a broken drowned coast.
2. The basin contains a freshwater through-flow lake fed by multiple rivers and
   streams and drained toward the sea by an outlet river.
3. The outlet leaves the basin through a narrow, naturally defensible gap.
4. The present basin is a geomorphic palimpsest: an ancient river-carved
   lowland was later reshaped by glaciation.
5. The coast was inundated in the ancient past. Its broad regional outline is
   now stable, while local channels, bars, and small landforms remain vulnerable
   to storm-driven change and loss.
6. Drowned valleys, tidal marshes, peat islands, sandbars, wooded islands, and
   narrow sea passages form parts of the coastal mosaic.
7. The immediate region has a transitional maritime–continental climate.
8. The western windward terrain supports a major alien temperate-rainforest
   biome, while scattered damp refugia persist within the basin.
9. Upland snow accumulates reliably and feeds a strong spring melt. Basin snow
   comes most years but can thaw and return several times within a winter.
10. Sheltered lake bays, wetlands, and slow river reaches freeze regularly.
    Continuous surface ice across the lake's deeper central waters occurs only
    in unusually severe winters.
11. Sudden thaws can produce flooding, moving ice, and dangerous river crossings.
12. Summers are normally mild to warm, with long daylight and regular rain.
    Occasional hotter dry spells can last several weeks, while severe drought
    remains uncommon.
13. The western rainforest and damp ravines usually remain moist during those
    dry spells, while basin open ground, habitat edges, and peaty margins can
    become dry enough to support surface fire.

## Mechanisms and consequences

### Causal basis

Successive river erosion and glacial reshaping produce a varied terrain rather
than a single-origin basin. Maritime moisture meeting
the western uplands supports wet windward habitats; shelter and distance from
the strongest oceanic influence permit greater seasonal contrast inside the
basin. The lake and its outlet connect snowmelt, runoff, wetlands, lowland
flooding, and the sea while leaving exact tidal influence and the downstream
salinity transition open; the lake remains freshwater.

### Geography, ecology, and material constraints

- A freshwater through-flow lake requires a viable long-term water balance.
- The outlet must descend overall toward the sea. Its gradient, head of tide,
  freshwater tidal influence, salt front, and storm-surge reach remain open;
  the lake remains freshwater.
- Reliable upland snow and sudden-thaw floods require ordinary water storage,
  temperature change, and drainage constraints.
- Temperate-rainforest conditions require persistent moisture, but no exact
  rainfall, wind system, canopy form, or taxonomic composition is selected.
- Fire opportunity does not imply annual fire, basin-wide burning, or rapid
  recovery. Existing finite ecological recovery rules remain in force.

### Society and culture

The gap, rivers, lake, coast, wetlands, forests, and seasonal hazards create
possible constraints on settlement, travel, defence, subsistence, and trade.
This proposal establishes no fortification, road, port, toll, boundary,
institution, ownership, cultural practice, or interpretation.

### History and chronology

“Ancient river basin,” “glaciation,” and “ancient inundation” establish relative
sequence and process categories only. They establish no dates, Earth event,
drowned civilization, ruin, historical migration, or cultural memory.

### Story implications

- The protagonist can encounter a visible gradient from wet uplands through a
  sheltered basin to marshy lowlands and a broken coast.
- Snowmelt, variable ice, local floods, storms, and occasional dry spells can
  create grounded seasonal pressures when later story records select them.
- The gap is a terrain affordance only. Any fort, crossing, controller, or
  conflict there requires a separate decision.
- Later migration, travel, import, and tale design can use this scaffold but
  must still establish exact routes, logistics, evidence, and knowledge.

### Character-knowledge implications

The complete regional scaffold is author-side causal truth. A character knows
only the places, patterns, reports, records, or observations available to them.
Earth analogue labels and hidden geological history are not automatically
in-world terms or common knowledge.

### CAL0 implications

This is ordinary world-level geography and ecology under CAL0 `WLD1.2D` and
`WLD2.1A+D`. It creates no System rule, climate-control mechanism, ambient
spawn, ecological reset, inexhaustible resource, or automatic recovery.

## Alternatives

| Option | Distinguishing choice | Benefits | Costs/risks | Reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GEO-A — Predominantly glacial closed basin | A scoured inland lake with no ordinary seaward outlet | Strong glacial identity and isolation | Conflicts with the selected regional connection and reduces river-to-coast exchange | Low after hydrology is developed |
| GEO-B — Layered river–glacial through-flow region | Older river lowland, later glacial reshaping, open lake, constrained outlet, and stable drowned coast | Supports varied terrain, realistic water and migration connections, and the selected inspiration blend | Requires explicit local hydrology, soils, and seasonal accounting | Moderate before detailed maps |
| GEO-C — Tectonic or karst-led basin | Fault or dissolution processes dominate the lake and drainage | Strong caves, scarps, springs, or mineral features | Adds active-geology or underground-drainage obligations not needed by the current story | High before detailed maps |
| GEO-D — Continuing regional inundation | The drowned coast is visibly retreating over current generations | Strong current-event pressure | Makes sea-level change a major story driver and destabilizes routes | Low after settlement history |

For climate, the Author considered ocean-tempered, transitional
maritime–continental, and continental-leaning regimes and selected the
transitional model with a western rainforest and basin refugia.

## Consistency review

- The through-flow system supplies flooding, runoff, lake–land transfer, and a
  wider ecological connection required by `WLD-ECOLOGY-005`.
- The broad terrain leaves `WLD-ECOLOGY-004`'s exact corridor geometry,
  direction, endpoints, calendar, and herd scale open.
- The selected winter, flood, summer-dry, and fire possibilities support but do
  not quantify `WLD-FLORA-001` disturbance and recovery.
- No Earth organism, Earth culture, magical climate cause, or System change is
  imported.
- No contradiction or retcon has been identified.

## Residual unknowns

- Exact map, scale, dimensions, elevations, travel times, river courses, lake
  form, outlet profile, gap geometry, salinity boundary, and coastal shape
- Quantitative geology, hydrology, soils, climate, weather frequency, lake ice,
  drought, fire, and productivity
- Rainforest composition, extent, in-world name, history, and human use
- Exact habitats, migrations, roads, settlements, ports, cultures, trade,
  current events, stories, and character knowledge
- Ancient doorway and offworld relationships, which this record does not touch

## Recommendation

Select `GEO-B` with the transitional climate and approved seasonal pattern. It
provides the most useful causal foundation for the coupled basin ecosystem while
keeping quantitative maps, biological composition, and human geography open.
