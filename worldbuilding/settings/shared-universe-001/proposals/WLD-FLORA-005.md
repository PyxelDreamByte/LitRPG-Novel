# WLD-FLORA-005 — Tower-flora foundational growth architecture

## Purpose

First Phase 1 scaffold under the development plan: fix the K2 tower-flora
kingdom's foundational growth architecture at qualitative strategy grain, so
woodland, rainforest-candidate, and later timber-analogue development has a
defined alien foundation instead of implicit Earth trees.

## Existing constraints

- Accepted world canon: `WLD-FLORA-004` (K2 slot: long-lived, tall,
  self-supporting woody-analogue structures), `WLD-GEOGRAPHY-001` (western
  temperate rainforest, basin woodland, seasonal climate with hard winters,
  spring melt, occasional dry spells, surface fire on open ground),
  `WLD-ECOLOGY-001` (crown forms process woody growth), `WLD-ECOLOGY-005`
  (pulse-mosaic exchange)
- CAL0 rules: WLD1.2D and WLD2.1A+D
- Deliberately unresolved boundaries: species, taxonomy, dimensions,
  chemistry, exact reproduction, kingdom assignments of named vegetation

## Proposed claims

Common to all options:

1. Tower flora are individual, non-networked organisms (contrast with K1);
   any root contact between towers is ordinary competition or incidental,
   not kingdom-defining resource sharing.
2. Towers are long-lived and slow to replace; loss of mature towers is a
   lasting landscape change (ties to finite-recovery discipline).
3. Seasonal growth is strongly pulsed, compatible with the established
   climate and pulse-mosaic ecology.
4. The architecture is qualitative; no Earth wood, bark, ring, or tree
   homology is established.

## Alternatives

| Option | Distinguishing choice | Benefits | Costs/risks | Reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Living core, dead outer casing (inverted from Earth trees) | Clean K1 contrast; alien but legible; rich consequences for fire, harvest, disease | Core-dependence needs consistent handling in every later record | High at this grain |
| B | Stacked semi-independent modules | Resilient, regrows lost tops | Blurs into K1's modular identity; weakens the kingdom contrast | High |
| C | Braided multi-strand trunks | Distinctive silhouette; redundancy drama | More complex; partial-death towers complicate every later description | High |

### Option A — Core-and-casing towers (recommended)

A tower grows from a single persistent **living core column**. Each growth
season the core lays down an external structural **casing layer** that then
dies, so a mature tower is a living column sheathed in successive dead,
self-supporting casings — the inverse of an Earth tree's dead heartwood and
living outer ring. Consequences at theme level: surface damage, scorching, and
girdling-equivalents are survivable because the living tissue is deep;
breaching or rotting through to the core is the mortal wound; casing can in
principle be taken without killing (no harvest practice established); growth
in height and girth is slow and front-loaded on the casing budget. Crown
growth, leaf-analogue structures, and reproduction stay open, with **episodic
heavy seeding years** (masting-analogue pulses) flagged as the candidate
reproduction rhythm for later records.

### Option B — Stack towers

Towers are vertical stacks of repeating semi-independent modules; upper
modules can die or shed and be regrown. Resilient and croppable, but modular
organisation is K1's defining theme, and the kingdom contrast suffers.

### Option C — Braid towers

Several living strands rise from separate bases and fuse into one
self-supporting braided tower; strands die individually while the tower
persists. Striking fluted silhouettes and partial-death states, at the cost of
permanent extra complexity in every later description.

## Mechanisms and consequences

### Causal basis

Deep divergence of the K2 foundation; ordinary biology, no System cause.

### Geography, ecology, and material constraints

Slow replacement makes mature stands finite capital; fire and storm effects
depend on casing and core properties left open; compatible with crown-form
feeding on woody growth without specifying any diet link.

### Story implications

Woodland scenes gain an alien signature (layered casings, living-core stakes);
future timber-analogue, fire, and disease stories get grounded hooks.

### Character-knowledge implications

None established; folk knowledge of cores and casings remains open.

### CAL0 implications

Application only; ordinary ecology.

## Residual unknowns

- Species, branch structure, dimensions, lifespan, chemistry, energy capture,
  leaf-analogues, root form, and exact reproduction and dispersal
- Kingdom assignments of the rainforest and basin woodland
- Casing material properties, decay, and any human use
- Mana patterns, pests, diseases, and symbioses (including any K6 partnership)

## Recommendation

Option A. The living-core/dead-casing inversion is the strongest alien-but-
legible signature, gives the cleanest contrast with K1's networked modularity,
and seeds consequential, boundable hooks (core wounds, survivable scorching,
kill-free casing harvest, masting pulses) that later records can develop
without retcon pressure.
