# WLD-LOCATION-002 — Initial Ridge-Spur Lake-Road Practical Travel Scale

## Purpose

Give the established lakeward road a bounded practical journey scale so later
daily-life, logistics, ecology, and story development can treat the lake as
regularly reachable without making it adjacent or fixing a surveyed route,
transport system, customary itinerary, or guaranteed duration.

## Existing constraints

- WLD-LOCATION-001 establishes a constructed road descending in bends from the
  initial ridge-spur settlement toward a lake beyond the immediate managed
  apron but within regular local travel and haulage reach.
- WLD-GEOGRAPHY-001 establishes the wider physical and seasonal frame while
  leaving exact distances, local route geometry, and exact travel times open.
- WLD-ECOLOGY-004 leaves possible exposure around some roads and lake
  approaches unresolved; WLD-ECOLOGY-005 and WLD-ECOLOGY-006 establish the
  wider habitat mosaic and seasonally varied lake without fixing traffic.
- CAL0 WLD1.2D and WLD2.1A+D preserve ordinary finite world causality.
- CAL0 ITM1.2D preserves task-conditioned encumbrance through mass, volume,
  balance, terrain, duration, and fatigue without creating a new inventory or
  travel mechanic.
- The option and selection capture is:
  stories/series/project-hearthway-main/workbench/runs/2026-08-15-initial-ridge-spur-lake-road-practical-travel-scale/proposal.md

This is a forward location extension, not a replacement or retcon.

## Proposed claims

1. The established lake road uses the author-facing practical scale
   “half-day working route.”
2. Under the displayed fair-weather comparison, an ordinary unladen downhill
   journey has a broad 1–1½-hour reference band.
3. Under the same comparison, a routine laden uphill return has a broad
   2–3-hour reference band.
4. A useful shore visit followed by a loaded return can consume most of a
   working day.
5. The lake remains part of regular settlement life even though haulage,
   freshness where goods are perishable, remaining daylight, and delays can
   matter.

## Mechanisms and consequences

### Causal and material basis

The uphill laden reference is slower than the downhill unladen reference
because direction, carried burden, balance, terrain, duration, and fatigue can
change ordinary task performance. The selected bands do not define a standard
person, load, pace, distance, gradient, road state, or probability
distribution.

### Geography, ecology, and material constraints

The bands apply only to the displayed fair-weather comparison. Actual journeys
can fall outside them because of traveller condition, load, rests, route
knowledge, road condition, weather, season, obstruction, injury, or animal
movement. No exact habitat sequence, secondary migration-path overlap,
predator encounter, closure, or delay is selected.

### Society and culture

The practical scale creates no road authority, toll, convoy, patrol,
occupation, schedule, transport custom, shore settlement, landing, cache,
shelter, preservation method, trade, or ownership rule.

### History and chronology

No construction date, travel tradition, road-development sequence, seasonal
calendar, workday length, or history of access is established.

### Story implications

- A same-day lake visit can remain plausible while load, daylight, shore work,
  and delay create meaningful decisions.
- Later scenes can use the road as a transition between managed settlement
  ground and lakeward environments without making every trip an expedition.
- Perishable goods or frequent loads can create later logistical questions,
  but no product, practice, institution, or journey outcome follows
  automatically.

### Character-knowledge implications

The bands are author-facing planning truth rather than automatic character
knowledge. A character requires experience, instruction, testimony, records,
or another available source to estimate a journey, and one observed trip does
not reveal a universal time or exact distance.

### CAL0 implications

This is an ordinary world-level travel input under CAL0 WLD1.2D, WLD2.1A+D,
and ITM1.2D. It creates no inventory rule, encumbrance formula, travel
mechanic, movement modifier, route readout, fast travel, safe road, free
energy, free Mana, spawn, reset, or guaranteed recovery.

## Alternatives

| Option | Distinguishing choice | Benefits | Costs/risks | Reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — Close daily orbit | 30–60 minutes downhill unladen and 1–1½ hours uphill laden | Frequent movement and rapid scene transitions | Makes the lake feel more adjacent and gives the road less logistical weight | Moderate before mapping and story use |
| B — Half-day working route — selected | 1–1½ hours downhill unladen and 2–3 hours uphill laden | Keeps the lake regularly reachable while giving load, daylight, delay, and habitat transition weight | Lake work requires planning rather than casual movement | Moderate before mapping and story use |
| C — Outer daily reach | 2–3 hours downhill unladen and 4–6 hours uphill laden | Strong journey stakes and route exposure | Presses regular haulage toward unselected support infrastructure | Moderate before mapping and story use |

Options A and C remain nonbinding decision evidence.

## Recommendation

Select B — Half-day working route. It is the smallest coherent scale that
matches “fairly far but not too far,” keeps regular lakeward activity
plausible, and gives the intervening alien landscape material and narrative
weight without requiring a shore settlement or transport system.
