# WLD-ECOLOGY-005 — Initial-Basin Coupled Pulse-Mosaic Exchange

## Purpose

Establish the ecological design frame for the initial basin without treating it
as a sealed world or turning every mentioned organism into a local resident.
The frame must support a coherent alien ecosystem, wider migrations, possible
nonlocal animals and foods, and uncertain tales while protecting locality and
knowledge boundaries.

## Existing constraints

- Setting authority: `WLD-SETTING-001A`
- Existing regional ecology: `WLD-ECOLOGY-001` and `WLD-ECOLOGY-004`
- Existing open-ground producer anchor: `WLD-FLORA-001`
- CAL0 ecology: `WLD1.2D` and `WLD2.1A+D`
- Story workspace:
  `stories/series/project-hearthway-main/project-hearthway-main.work-manifest.json`
- Design source:
  `stories/series/project-hearthway-main/workbench/runs/2026-08-14-basin-ecosystem-scaffold/ecosystem-scaffold.md`

The current records establish one large-consumer lineage, one major but
non-exclusive open-ground vegetation guild, a lower-basin migration corridor,
and lake and woodland-margin contact. They do not establish the basin's whole
food web, wider geography, trade network, or local knowledge of distant life.

## Proposed claims

1. The initial basin is a coupled pulse-mosaic: open ground, woodland, wet
   margins, and lake habitats provide distinct seasonal productivity pulses.
2. Organisms, flooding, runoff, decomposition, and migration transfer matter
   and nutrients among those habitat channels.
3. No single habitat, producer guild, migration, disturbance, or animal lineage
   is the basin's sole ecological engine.
4. The initial basin is nested within a wider regional ecology rather than
   treated as a closed system.

## Binding development and evidence consequences

- Development of nonlocal life must distinguish confirmed direct wild presence,
  seasonal or transient wild presence, imported remains or products, rare
  traveller-carried companion or captive live presence, and reported-only
  knowledge. These are evidence or exposure classes, not taxonomic or residency
  classes.
- Evidence in one class does not establish another: a direct sighting proves
  presence only at that time; a product does not prove local range or verified
  provenance; a migrant or captive individual does not establish residency or a
  self-sustaining population; and a tale does not establish existence or local
  presence.

## Mechanisms and consequences

### Causal basis

The basin contains several production channels whose peaks need not coincide.
Transfers through water, organisms, waste, remains, and decomposition couple
those channels without making the system perfectly efficient or closed.
Longer-range animal movement and human carriage can connect the basin to
external habitats, while testimony and material evidence can connect local
understanding to places that characters have not seen.

### Geography, ecology, and material constraints

- Any migration proposed to cross the basin boundary requires supporting
  habitats beyond it, an organism-specific trigger, a viable route, travel and
  mortality costs, bottlenecks, and the material, energy, water, Mana, or other
  inputs applicable to that organism.
- Any imported food or biological material requires a source, travel time,
  transport, preservation where necessary, spoilage risk, and a plausible
  supply frequency.
- Any proposed traveller-carried animal requires a plausible transport and
  relationship arrangement accounting for its actual sustenance, water or
  fluid, atmospheric and thermal, shelter, waste, handling, and recovery needs.
  Its carriage also creates organism-specific disease or parasite risks; no
  effective disease-control practice is established here.
- Escape, release, or viable imported propagules do not automatically create a
  wild population. Establishment requires survival, reproduction, habitat, and
  sufficient continuity.
- Transfers can remove material from one habitat while enriching another;
  coupling does not imply complete recycling or equal productivity.

### Society and culture

The same nonlocal organism could be understood as a curiosity, companion,
captive, status symbol, food, medicine, craft material, danger, falsehood, or
sacred story by different people. This proposal establishes none of those
specific interpretations. `Pet` remains a relationship label rather than proof
of domestication, ownership, loyalty, consent, or nonsapience.

### History and chronology

No trade route, migration origin, migration destination, cultural contact, or
historical distribution is selected here. The model permits ecological and
material connections over different distances without establishing their age,
frequency, or institutional form.

### Story implications

- The wider living world can enter the protagonist's experience without an
  immediate journey beyond the basin: through migrations, travellers, traded
  or gifted meats, produce and biological materials, reports, and stories.
- Familiarity with a name or product does not grant field knowledge of the
  living organism.
- A rare nonlocal animal can be genuinely extraordinary locally without being
  globally unique.
- Seasonal trouble inside the basin may have causes or consequences beyond its
  boundary, but each causal chain requires explicit support.

### Character-knowledge implications

Every nonlocal-life reference should record its evidence channel and confidence.
Direct observation, repeated seasonal observation, an imported body part,
merchant testimony, inherited folklore, and a surviving record, if one exists,
support different conclusions. Tales may be accurate, distorted, composite,
mistaken, or invented until evidence narrows them.

### CAL0 implications

This is a world-level application of CAL0 `WLD1.2D` and `WLD2.1A+D`, not a
System change. Living migrants, captives, escaped animals, and viable imported
propagules retain real life cycles and ecological costs. Imported remains and
products retain causal provenance, transport, preservation, and spoilage
constraints. Arrival is not ambient spawning. This proposal establishes no
level, Skill, attribute, or power transfer from consuming nonlocal biological
products.

## Alternatives

| Option | Distinguishing choice | Benefits | Costs/risks | Reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ECO-ENGINE-A — Land-led basin | Open-ground and woodland production dominate; the lake is important but subordinate | Simpler modelling and strong agricultural pressure | Risks reducing the lake to scenery and utility | High before detailed food-web work |
| ECO-ENGINE-B — Lake-led basin | Aquatic and littoral production supplies much of the basin's usable biomass | Strongly distinctive lake-centred ecology | Risks concentrating ecology, economy, and danger around one feature | Moderate before trade and settlement detail |
| ECO-ENGINE-C — Coupled pulse-mosaic | Open ground, woodland, wet margins, and lake provide distinct pulses linked by cross-habitat transfers | Supports ecological redundancy, varied scenes, and several alien lineages | Requires explicit seasonal and cross-habitat accounting | Moderate after family design begins |

The Author also directed that the selected basin model consider a wider
ecological horizon supporting migrations, rare traveller-carried animals,
distant foods and produce, and tales of animals absent locally.

## Consistency review

- The coupled model preserves `WLD-FLORA-001` as major but non-exclusive.
- It extends the basin role of migration without selecting new routes or
  assigning an external range to Lineage II beyond `WLD-ECOLOGY-004`.
- `WLD-ECOLOGY-002`, `WLD-ECOLOGY-003`, `WLD-FLORA-002`, and `WLD-FLORA-003`
  remain authoritative within their bounded physiology, inheritance,
  patch-topology, and fragment-viability scopes; this proposal neither changes
  nor broadens them.
- It requires small-life, decomposer, aquatic, woodland, and other consumer
  roles to be developed later rather than silently inventing them here.
- It preserves ordinary life cycles, finite renewal, carrying capacity, and
  causal transport under CAL0.
- The evidence and exposure classes prevent product, testimony, captivity, migration, and
  residence from collapsing into accidental omniscience or false geography.
- No contradiction or retcon has been identified.

## Residual unknowns

- Exact basin hydrology, productivity, pulse timing, limiting resources, and
  cross-habitat transfer strengths
- Outer-region geography, climate gradients, habitats, and ecological support
- Additional migratory populations, origins, destinations, routes, calendars,
  and population scales
- Trade routes, transport speeds, preservation methods, prices, volumes, and
  who can obtain nonlocal foods or biological materials
- Companion or captive species, origins, cognition, legal treatment, husbandry,
  disease risks, escape risk, and establishment potential
- Which stories are accurate, distorted, composite, mistaken, or invented
- The wider horizon governed here is regional and planetary; this proposal
  establishes no offworld organism, traveller, product, route, or connection

## Recommendation

Select `ECO-ENGINE-C` and apply the wider ecological horizon. It best supports
an alien but coherent ecosystem, makes the nearby lake ecologically meaningful,
keeps existing Lineage II and reserve-web records non-exclusive, and lets the
wider world enter the protagonist's experience through evidence with explicit
provenance.
