# WLD-FLORA-008 — Thread-decomposer foundational body architecture

## Purpose

Fourth Phase 1 scaffold: fix the K6 thread-decomposer kingdom's foundational
body-and-feeding architecture. K6 closes the food web — dead casings, shed
streamers, litter, dung, and carcasses need recyclers — and is the standing
candidate partner for producer-root symbioses.

## Existing constraints

- Accepted world canon: `WLD-FLORA-004` (K6 slot: non-photosynthesising
  thread-and-body networks in decomposer, recycler, and mutualist roles),
  `WLD-FLORA-005` (dead casings), `WLD-FLORA-006` (shed streamers),
  `WLD-ECOLOGY-002`/`003` (Lineage II digestive symbionts are
  microorganisms — a separate scale that K6 must not absorb)
- CAL0 rules: WLD1.2D and WLD2.1A+D
- Deliberately unresolved boundaries: species, chemistry, reproduction,
  toxicity, edibility, any human use

## Proposed claims

Common to all options:

1. Thread decomposers are non-photosynthesising heterotrophs feeding on dead
   or living organic material through thread-form feeding bodies.
2. They are macroscopic organisms, distinct from microbial life; the
   Lineage II digestive symbionts remain separate microorganisms.
3. They are the planet's primary macroscopic decomposer-recycler kingdom.
4. Mutualist partnerships with producer kingdoms remain a bounded branch
   possibility, not the kingdom default.

## Alternatives

| Option | Distinguishing choice | Benefits | Costs/risks | Reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Threads weave dead material into consolidated wrapped masses | Visible alien signature; material-culture hooks (felts, husks); localized decomposition texture | Wrapping logic must stay bounded | High at this grain |
| B | Diffuse hidden threads with episodic fruiting eruptions | Familiar; low maintenance | Least alien; nearly an Earth fungus transplant in feel | High |
| C | Partnership-first: most members live plugged into living producers | Strong symbiosis stories | Makes decomposition secondary; crowds the microbial symbiont boundary | High |

### Option A — Weaver decomposers (recommended)

Feeding threads do not just penetrate dead material — they **bind and wrap**
it, drawing litter, shed streamers, fallen casings, dung, and carcasses into
consolidated felted masses that decompose from within. The visible signature
of K6 at work is wrapped deadfall, felt-sheeted litter layers, and husk
shells left when a mass is spent. Consequences at theme level: decomposition
is visible, localized, and paced — a wrapped mass is a working site, not an
instant process; spent husks and felts are candidate (not established)
material resources; fruiting or spore-analogue reproduction remains open;
root partnerships remain an open branch possibility.

### Option B — Bloom decomposers

Threads live diffusely and invisibly in soil and dead wood, erupting in
episodic fruiting bodies after rain and thaw. Familiar to the point of
reading as Earth fungi renamed.

### Option C — Partner-first decomposers

The kingdom's defining habit is living partnership with producer roots and
tissues, with free-living decomposition secondary. Strong symbiosis texture,
but the food web needs decomposition as the primary K6 job, and living-tissue
partnership crowds the microbial-symbiont boundary.

## Mechanisms and consequences

### Causal basis

Deep divergence of the K6 foundation; ordinary biology.

### Geography, ecology, and material constraints

Wrapped masses tie decomposition rates to local K6 presence and conditions;
unwrapped material can persist abnormally long where K6 is absent; husks and
felts enter later material-culture design as candidates only.

### Story implications

Forest floors, shorelines, and kill sites gain an alien working texture:
wrapped and felted masses in every stage of consumption, husk shells, and
the notable absence of decay where weavers are missing.

### Character-knowledge implications

None established.

### CAL0 implications

Application only; ordinary ecology.

## Residual unknowns

- Species, thread and mass anatomy, chemistry, digestion, and pacing
- Reproduction, dispersal, fruiting structures, toxicity, and edibility

## Recommendation

Option A. Weaving gives the kingdom a visible, alien, story-usable signature
that neither Earth fungi nor the other kingdoms occupy, keeps decomposition
front and centre, preserves the microbial boundary, and leaves symbiosis and
materials as clean later hooks.
