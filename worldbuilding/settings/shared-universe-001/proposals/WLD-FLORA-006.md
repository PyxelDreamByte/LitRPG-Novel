# WLD-FLORA-006 — Water-flora foundational growth architecture

## Purpose

Second Phase 1 scaffold: fix the K4 water-flora kingdom's foundational growth
architecture. K4 members must live with the established hydrology — spring
freshet scouring, seasonal bay ice, water-level swings, floods, currents, and
a brackish coastal fringe — and K4 is the candidate home of the lake's sturdy
egg-substrate plants (`WLD-ECOLOGY-006`, unassigned).

## Existing constraints

- Accepted world canon: `WLD-FLORA-004` (K4 slot: submerged, floating, and
  wet-margin producers), `WLD-GEOGRAPHY-001` (through-flow lake, freshet,
  seasonal ice, floods, drowned coast), `WLD-ECOLOGY-006` (egg ribbons on
  sturdy plants and mineral surfaces; flooded vegetation margins),
  `WLD-ECOLOGY-005` (pulse-mosaic exchange)
- CAL0 rules: WLD1.2D and WLD2.1A+D
- Deliberately unresolved boundaries: species, chemistry, exact reproduction,
  the actual egg-substrate assignment, salinity tolerances

## Proposed claims

Common to all options:

1. Water flora are individual, non-networked organisms (contrast with K1).
2. The kingdom spans submerged, floating-surface, and wet-margin emergent
   forms.
3. Kingdom life rhythms are strongly coupled to the seasonal water cycle:
   freshet, high-water margins, summer growth, and winter ice or die-back.
4. Disturbance tolerance — flood scouring, ice, level swings — is a
   kingdom-defining pressure, met differently by different members.

### Option A — Anchor-and-streamer flora (recommended)

Each organism has two qualitative parts: a durable, long-lived **anchor**
gripping bed, stone, or bank — the organism's persistent core, holding stored
reserves — and expendable flexible **streamer growth** (ribbon, frond, and
stem-analogue forms) that extends through the growing season and is shed or
sacrificed to ice, scouring, and grazing. Winter and flood survival is
anchor-and-reserves survival; spring regrowth follows the freshet. Mature
anchored forms with stiffened streamer bases supply the natural (still
unassigned) candidate for the lake's sturdy egg-substrate plants. Emergent
wet-margin members raise seasonal streamers above shallow water; floating
members remain a bounded branch possibility rather than the kingdom theme.

### Option B — Buoy flora

Members are buoyant vessels — gas- or gel-supported floats, tethered or free —
that ride the surface in summer and ballast down for winter. Striking, but
sturdiness for egg ribbons and freshet survival both need extra machinery.

### Option C — Turf flora

Members form dense, low clonal turfs carpeting beds and margins: persistent
underwater meadows. Durable, but clonal carpet organisation blurs the K1
contrast and overlaps K5's low-cushion identity.

## Mechanisms and consequences

### Causal basis

Deep divergence of the K4 foundation; ordinary biology.

### Geography, ecology, and material constraints

Anchors make water flora finite, place-bound capital while streamers renew
annually; scouring and grazing remove streamers without necessarily killing;
anchor loss is the lasting damage. Compatible with spring productivity rise
and dispersed egg deposition without establishing either mechanism.

### Story implications

Lake and shore scenes gain seasonal texture: bare anchors after the freshet,
summer streamer beds, shed drift after storms; later fishery, forage, and
nursery records get grounded hooks.

### Character-knowledge implications

None established.

### CAL0 implications

Application only; ordinary ecology.

## Recommendation

Option A. It survives the established freshet-and-ice regime with the least
special pleading, makes the sturdy egg-substrate connection natural without
forcing it, spans all three K4 habits, and keeps the individual-organism
contrast with K1 crisp.
