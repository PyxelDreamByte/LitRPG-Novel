# WLD-FAUNA-004 — Lineage VI foundational body plan

## Purpose

Third Phase 2 scaffold: give Lineage VI — the small-bodied segmented
exoskeletal lineage — a bounded foundational body plan. This is the lineage
the ecological direction leans on hardest for "abundant ordinary organisms":
pollinator-analogues, litter fauna, parasites, swarm prey, and small flyers.
Its defining problem is the one every exoskeleton faces — how it grows — and
the answer shapes the whole lineage's size range and life cycle.

## Existing constraints

- Accepted world canon: `WLD-FAUNA-001` (Lineage VI slot: small-bodied
  segmented animals with exoskeletons, including small-scale flyers; every
  lineage contains abundant ordinary forms), `WLD-FAUNA-002`/`003` (the
  internally supported lineages, by contrast), `WLD-FLORA-008` (K6 thread
  decomposers are the macroscopic recycler kingdom; VI's decomposer roles sit
  alongside them, not in place of them)
- Staging context: the biosphere needs abundant ordinary organisms and should
  not read as a catalogue of spectacular remixes
- CAL0 rules: WLD1.2D and WLD2.1A+D
- Deliberately unresolved boundaries: species, names, taxonomy, sizes, local
  presence, Mana patterns

## Proposed claims

Common to all options:

1. Lineage VI has a segmented body enclosed in a jointed external skeleton,
   with segment counts and regional specialisation varying by branch.
2. Most members are small-bodied, and the lineage is the planet's most
   species-rich macroscopic animal radiation.
3. Some branches fly; flight is small-scale and independent of the flight
   evolved in Lineages I and III.
4. The lineage supplies pollinator-analogue, litter and soil, parasitic,
   scavenging, predatory, and swarm-prey roles.

## Selected structure — growth mode as a branch axis

On 2026-08-17 the Author directed that all three growth modes exist as
branches within the single lineage, and that some members exceed Earth
arthropod sizes. Growth mode therefore becomes a branch-level axis rather
than a lineage-wide rule, and it predicts the kind of animal:

- **Moult branches** shed the whole skeleton periodically, passing through a
  soft vulnerable phase. Moulting permits life-stage transformation, and the
  moult constraint keeps these branches small. These are the lineage's most
  numerous ordinary forms.
- **Seam-growth branches** never shed: the skeleton expands continuously at
  flexible inter-plate seams. No soft phase, no life-stage transformation,
  juveniles resemble small adults — and no moult-imposed size ceiling, so
  these branches include the lineage's large-bodied members, which exceed the
  sizes Earth arthropods reach.
- **Rolling-replacement branches** shed and regrow plates individually on a
  rolling schedule, so they are never wholly soft while retaining some
  capacity to reshape. These occupy the middle ground.

## Alternatives — which growth mode is ancestral

| Option | Distinguishing choice | Benefits | Costs/risks | Reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Moult ancestral; seam growth and rolling replacement derived | Reads as constraint-then-escape; makes large seam-growth branches an earned innovation; keeps small moulting forms as the abundant default | Large branches concentrated in one derived group | High at this grain |
| B | Seam growth ancestral; moulting derived | Makes life-stage transformation the innovation instead | Large size becomes ancestral and ordinary, weakening its significance | High |
| C | Rolling replacement ancestral; both others derived | Neat symmetry | Least intuitive ancestor; middling explanatory value | High |

### Option A — Moult-ancestral (recommended)

The ancestral condition is whole-body moulting with its soft phase and size
ceiling. Two derived escapes followed: rolling replacement, which removes
the wholly-soft window, and seam growth, which removes both the soft window
and the size ceiling. Large-bodied Lineage VI animals are therefore a real
evolutionary achievement rather than a baseline, small moulting forms remain
the abundant everyday default, and each mode's costs stay legible.

## Mechanisms and consequences

### Causal basis

Deep divergence of the segmented exoskeletal foundation; ordinary evolution.

### Geography, ecology, and material constraints

Each growth mode carries its own costs: moult branches are periodically
defenceless, seam-growth branches tie size to sustained feeding and mineral
intake and concentrate weakness at their seams, and rolling-replacement
branches always carry fresh, weaker plates. Body sizes beyond Earth arthropod
scale require respiratory, circulatory, and structural support that this
record does not supply; the mechanism remains open and binding.

### Story implications

Everyday small life — things underfoot, in stores, on flowering growth, in
carcasses — comes largely from this lineage, while its seam-growth branches
provide a biologically earned route to large forms of a kind Earth never
produced.

## Recommendation

Option A. Constraint-then-escape is the most legible evolutionary story: it
keeps abundant small moulting forms as the everyday default, makes each
derived mode's advantage explicit, and turns the lineage's large-bodied
members into an earned innovation of the seam-growth branches rather than an
unexplained baseline.
