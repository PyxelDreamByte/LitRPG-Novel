## Decision boundary

The Author chooses only the qualitative shape of the previous regional power's
cascading retreat across the ~70-year arc: its phase structure, the order in
which function classes (institutions, knowledge transmission, manpower,
infrastructure upkeep, organised ecological suppression) withdrew or failed,
which fragment classes persisted, when megafaunal and wildlife return
accelerated relative to those phases, and where the settlement founding
(25-35 years before story start) falls in the arc and why refuge formation
made sense then. The Author is not choosing: the collapse's cause or trigger,
the former power's name, capital, extent, or rulers, any date finer than the
shared anchors, any named neighbor polity, map, army, or current war, or any
story event. A shared, option-independent bounded neighbor frame accompanies
every option unchanged, as do all settled foundation-capture facts.

## Option cards

### Option A — Frontier-first staged withdrawal, late central snap

The retreat begins at the edges. In the early arc the power deliberately draws
down its outermost holdings, this region among them: field manpower and
garrison rotation are withdrawn first, and organised ecological suppression
lapses regionally soon after, because its specialist cadres travel out with
the soldiers who protected them. In the middle arc the centre still asserts
nominal claims over the region while its remaining local offices decay:
infrastructure upkeep fails piecewise as works are consumed rather than
maintained, and knowledge transmission thins as certification, schooling, and
apprenticeship pipelines dependent on the distant centre stop taking intake.
In the late arc the centre itself suffers an administrative snap — remaining
formal claims, correspondence, and residual expectation of return end
abruptly rather than fading.

Regional order of function-class failure: manpower withdrawal → suppression
lapse → infrastructure upkeep → knowledge transmission → formal institutional
extinction at the snap. Phases overlap; the order is qualitative. Fragments
persist throughout: successor communities coalesce early around former
regional seats; reused works are widespread (quarried, repurposed, or
squatted rather than maintained); partial traditions survive — literacy,
law-forms, record habits — without their certifying centre; and uneven
expertise pockets remain where individuals from withdrawn services stayed.

Wildlife and megafaunal return accelerates early-to-mid arc in this region:
suppression lapsed decades before the centre failed, so the basin experienced
roughly a full generation of accelerating return while the empire nominally
still existed. The settlement founding (25-35 years ago) therefore falls
after sustained, worsening exposure: dispersed lowland holdings had become
progressively untenable, and reviving the pre-imperial hill-fort pattern was
a considered refuge response by people holding living memory of both refuge
tradition and post-imperial exposure. The late central snap falls in the
founding generation's lifetime; whether just before or just after the
founding year is left open, but it extinguished any remaining case for
waiting on imperial protection.

### Option B — Centre-failure first, stranded decay in place

The retreat begins at the heart. In the early arc central institutions fail
first: pay, orders, replacements, and certification stop arriving at the
frontier, stranding regional garrisons and provincial offices intact but
unsupported. The long middle arc is a mosaic of stranded fragments decaying
in place at very different rates: some garrisons keep suppression rounds
running for years on local resources, habit, and self-interest; some offices
continue records and adjudication as self-funded local institutions; others
dissolve within seasons. The late arc is the uneven lapse of the last
effective stranded functions.

Order of failure: central institutions → manpower (attrition without
replacement) → infrastructure upkeep → organised ecological suppression,
which fails last and patchily because stranded garrisons prioritised it for
their own survival; knowledge transmission thins throughout, tied to each
pocket's lifespan. Fragments are correspondingly institutional in character:
successor communities descend directly from stranded garrisons and offices;
reused works are often continuously occupied rather than reclaimed ruins;
partial traditions are strongest of the three options — unbroken but locally
drifting practice; expertise pockets are lineages of former service families.

Wildlife return accelerates patchily and later on average: each district's
return quickened when its own suppression pocket lapsed, compounding into
general acceleration by the mid-to-late arc. The settlement founding (25-35
years ago) coincides with the lapse of the last effective suppression
arrangements bearing on the basin: protection was visibly, recently, and
locally lost, refugees from decayed garrison-lands and failing lowland
districts pooled, and the hill-fort revival replaced a specific failed
protective arrangement within living memory rather than a long-faded one.

### Option C — Oscillating partial recoveries before final lapse

The retreat is a diminishing oscillation. Across two or three cycles the
power contracts, partially reasserts itself in reduced strength, and
re-abandons; each recovery restores fewer functions than the last — a
returning expedition might re-garrison and cull but not restore schools,
archives, or full upkeep. The final lapse comes well before the settlement
founding, and no reassertion follows it.

No single clean failure order exists; each oscillation strips a layer. The
first cycle costs manpower depth and infrastructure upkeep; later cycles
break institutional continuity and knowledge transmission, because each
interruption severs apprenticeships and scatters archives; organised
ecological suppression flickers — crudely restored in recoveries, lost in
abandonments — producing a ratchet in which wildlife return advances during
each abandonment and is only partly pushed back. Fragments are layered and
contradictory: successor communities formed, were partially reabsorbed, and
reformed, leaving rival legitimacy traditions; reused works show interleaved
repair and decay; partial traditions survive in incompatible versions dating
to different cycles; expertise pockets are self-reliant and skeptical, having
watched restoration fail more than once.

Wildlife return is stepwise, ratcheting with each abandonment and becoming
decisive after the final lapse. The settlement founding (25-35 years ago)
follows the final lapse by enough time that the expectation of another
recovery had demonstrably died: refuge formation made sense precisely when
waiting stopped making sense, and communities that had deferred permanent
defensive investment through the oscillations finally committed. This grounds
the founding's provisional character — built by people trained by their own
history to hedge.

## Shared claim set (option-independent)

1. Present political neighbors exist and are known to basin communities only
   through bounded presence classes, reusing WLD-ECOLOGY-005's provenance
   discipline adapted to societies: (a) direct sustained contact — nearer
   communities known by repeated first-hand dealings; (b) seasonal or
   transient contact — groups encountered irregularly, whose home
   arrangements are not directly observed; (c) transported goods — societies
   inferred from objects or materials arriving through exchange, where a good
   proves neither its maker's location, condition, nor intent; (d) resident
   outsiders — rare individuals from distant societies present locally, where
   an individual proves nothing about their society's aims, unity, or reach;
   (e) reported-only — polities known solely through travellers' reports and
   tales, where a report proves nothing about existence, location, or present
   condition.
2. Both human successor communities and returning non-human peoples can occur
   in any presence class. The frame does not sort peoples by species, and no
   class implies hostility, unity, or a single category of "returning" actor.
3. Non-transfer rules bind: contact proves contact only at that time;
   presence-class membership is observer-relative and may differ between
   basin communities; no class instantiates any particular neighbor, good,
   individual, or tale — these are development channels, not existing facts.
4. The collapse's trigger or root cause is explicitly open in every option.
   Cascading retreat is the established shape of the event, not an
   explanation of it.
5. Shared chronology anchors: collapse arc begins ~70 years before story
   start; wildlife and megafaunal return accelerated after organised
   suppression ended (timing per selected option); settlement founded 25-35
   years before story start; the first succession approaches at story start.
6. The former power was centred elsewhere; this region was among its
   peripheral holdings. This qualitative relation follows the capture's
   "imperial retreat" framing and is shared by all options; everything finer
   (location, extent, map relation) stays open.

## Recommended option

Option A. It gives the basin the cleanest causal runway into accepted canon —
a full generation of accelerating wildlife return before the founding fits
WLD-ECOLOGY-004's established corridor pressure without inventing local
imperial machinery the region does not yet need — and its late central snap
hands the founding generation a memorable political hinge while leaving
successor communities forming early and independently, which maximises open
space for neighbor development.

## Claim boundary (Option A, if selected)

1. The previous regional power's retreat was staged frontier-first across the
   ~70-year arc: outer holdings, this region among them, were drawn down
   early, while the centre retained nominal claims until a late
   administrative snap ended them abruptly.
2. Function classes failed regionally in the qualitative order: field
   manpower withdrawal; lapse of organised ecological suppression; piecewise
   failure of infrastructure upkeep; thinning of knowledge transmission;
   formal institutional extinction at the snap. Phases overlap; no phase
   carries a date finer than the shared anchors.
3. Fragment classes persisted: successor communities around former regional
   seats; reused imperial works; partial traditions (including literacy,
   law-forms, and record habits) without their certifying centre; and uneven
   pockets of former-service expertise.
4. Megafaunal and wildlife return accelerated regionally from the
   early-to-mid arc, once suppression lapsed — roughly a generation before
   the settlement founding.
5. The settlement founding (25-35 years before story start) followed
   sustained accelerating exposure that made dispersed lowland settlement
   progressively untenable; the hill-fort revival was a considered refuge
   response drawing on surviving refuge tradition and knowledge of the
   pre-imperial pattern; how that knowledge was carried (living memory,
   transmitted practice, or both) is open, and no claim is made about how
   long ago pre-imperial times lie.
6. The central administrative snap falls within the founding generation's
   lifetime; its exact relation to the founding year is open.
7. The shared claim set (neighbor presence classes, non-transfer rules, open
   trigger, chronology anchors, peripheral relation to the former power) is
   established as stated above.

Staged withdrawal, administrative snap, suppression lapse, successor

## Consistency and causal review

- Foundation capture fidelity: every option is a cascading multi-phase,
  multi-function retreat, never one clean fall; the present dark age is
  relative — fragments preserve society, craft, literacy, and memory; the
  founding window, mixed refuge community, compact, and approaching first
  succession appear unchanged in all options.
- WLD-GEOGRAPHY-001: the collapse is institutional and demographic, not
  environmental; no climate change, ecological reset, or magical cause is
  claimed; the region's physical scaffold is untouched.
- WLD-ECOLOGY-004: accelerating return is the qualitative historical runway
  into the corridor pattern that record establishes at story start; this
  proposal fixes no route geography, seasonal dates, herd scale, or
  route-fidelity mechanism (all declared open there), and attributes
  migration to no hostility, compulsion, or System event.
- WLD-ECOLOGY-005: the provenance discipline is reused by structural analogy
  for societies; no nonlocal organism, product, route, or tale is
  instantiated; class (c) transported goods remains a channel with no
  particular good existing.
- WLD-LOCATION-001/002: no settlement name, founder identity, institution,
  practice, or infrastructure origin is claimed; in particular, whether any
  local work (including the constructed road) is of imperial origin remains
  open.
- No monocausal history: the trigger is open; the retreat itself is a cascade
  across distinct function classes; wildlife return follows lapse of
  suppression, not a single event.
- CAL0: this is an ordinary setting extension. No System event caused,
  accompanied, or ended the collapse; no mechanic, spawning, reset, or
  System-granted institutional power is implied (SOC1.2D); the neighbor frame
  is a records/access/belief-layer structure, and basin beliefs about the
  collapse or about neighbors may be wrong without touching natural law.
- Forward extension, not retcon: no accepted record states any collapse
  detail; the foundation capture explicitly lists "seventy-year collapse
  chronology and the region's present political neighbours" as open design
  work this proposal now addresses.
- First-order consequences are standing pressures at story start, not
  occurred events: succession politics colored by founding-era memory,
  contested attitudes to imperial inheritance, and observer-relative,
  incomplete knowledge of neighbors.

1. The collapse's trigger or triggers — deliberately open; any later answer
   must remain multi-causal or be explicitly bounded by its own proposal.
2. The former power's name, capital, heartland, language, formal structure,
   and relation to the ancient doorways and traveller myths (constitution
   protected unknowns 1-2 govern).
3. Exact phase durations, transitions, and the administrative snap's precise
   relation to the founding year.
4. The identity, number, location, species, and character of specific
   neighbors in each presence class, including which returning non-human
   peoples are present in which classes.
5. Which reused works, partial traditions, and expertise pockets exist in the
   basin specifically — including whether any accepted local feature (such as
   the WLD-LOCATION-001 road) is of imperial origin.
6. The founding population's composition beyond "mixed refuge," the founder's
   origin, and founding-era arrangements (separate institutions proposal).
7. How basin cultures narrate, date, and mythologize the collapse — including
   wrong, partial, and rival beliefs — and how the former power's System
   practice is remembered or hoarded in fragments.
8. What organised ecological suppression consisted of in practice: methods,
   scale, institutions, and costs.

## Author decision

On 2026-08-17 the Author accepted this proposal through explicit
multiple-choice selections, choosing a custom combination of the displayed
options rather than any single card:

- Phase structure, combining the displayed patterns in sequence:
  centre-failure first (central institutions fail first, stranding frontier
  garrisons and offices that decay in place at different rates), then
  oscillating partial recoveries (central power temporarily restored under
  capable leadership more than once before falling again, each cycle
  stripping further function layers), then a final frontier-first drawdown
  (a staged withdrawal of remaining frontier holdings ending organised
  imperial presence in the region).
- Contributing pressures across the empire: wars with other races, internal
  wars, and greed and corruption within imperial institutions — multi-causal,
  none singly sufficient, exact weighting open; the trigger remains
  explicitly open.
- Collapse-era developments: dangerous wildlife gradually increased as
  organised ecological suppression lapsed, and pest-like races spread and
  caused chaos.
- Identification: the pest-like races are the parked sapient pack species;
  "goblin" is author-facing shorthand only; only the collapse-era spread
  becomes bounded history, and all other development stays parked.
- The shared option-independent claim set (neighbor presence classes,
  non-transfer rules, open trigger, chronology anchors, peripheral relation)
  is promoted as displayed, with the settlement founding falling after the
  final drawdown phase had begun regionally.

The selection-time claim boundary for the combined sequence is recorded in
WLD-HISTORY-001; the Option A claim boundary above and all card-only texture
remain nonbinding evidence.
