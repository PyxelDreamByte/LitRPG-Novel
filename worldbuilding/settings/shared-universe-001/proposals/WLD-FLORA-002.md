# WLD-FLORA-002 — Basin Reserve-Web Modular Colonies

## Purpose

Resolve the functional colony organization left open by `WLD-FLORA-001`
without prematurely defining taxa, detailed anatomy, reproduction, Mana use,
or the full physiology of resource transfer.

## Existing constraints

- Accepted world authority: `WLD-FLORA-001`
- CAL0 rules: WLD1.1D and WLD2.1A+D
- Story contract: `work://series/project-hearthway-main` remains proposed
- Deliberately unresolved: module anatomy, patch dimensions, transfer rates,
  reconnection, reproduction, species variation, and human knowledge or use

## Options reviewed

| Option | Distinguishing choice | Benefits | Costs/risks | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FLORA-PATCH-A | Bounded modular colonies with finite local resource sharing | Resilience with real spatial and damage limits | Requires later anatomy and rate modelling | Selected |
| FLORA-PATCH-B | Large continuous clonal megastands | Strong long-range buffering | Risks a basin-scale superorganism and weak local consequences | Not selected |
| FLORA-PATCH-C | Independent unconnected modules | Simple individual accounting | Loses the reserve-web's local colony dynamics | Not selected |

## Selected proposed claims

1. Reserve-web subsurface networks are organized into bounded modular colony patches.
2. Each patch contains numerous rooted growth modules connected by
   a spatially limited living network.
3. Living connections permit finite local sharing of resources.
4. Damage can sever connections, isolate portions of a patch, and kill modules
   or sections when sufficiently severe.
5. Distinct patches do not combine into one continuous basin-wide organism.
6. Local resource sharing does not constitute shared consciousness.

## Mechanisms and consequences

### Causal basis

Real living connections carry local resources between modules. Connection
topology, capacity, condition, and damage therefore constrain what a patch can
support and which portions remain coupled.

### Geography, ecology, and material constraints

Patch boundaries are spatially real. Sufficient local damage may fragment the
network and create uneven survival or recovery. Matter and energy remain finite;
nothing is transferred instantly or across the basin without a later accepted
mechanism.

### Society and culture

No cultivation, mapping, harvesting, ownership, management, terminology, or
cultural understanding is established. These remain future decisions.

### History and chronology

No origin, evolutionary history, age limit, succession sequence, or historical
distribution is established.

### Story implications

Damage can leave local gaps, isolated sections, and uneven recovery rather than
an all-or-nothing stand reset. Characters may eventually observe these effects,
but no character knowledge is established by this proposal.

### Character-knowledge implications

The true topology is setting truth. Which people can recognize modules, patch
boundaries, severed links, or recovery prospects remains unknown.

### CAL0 implications

This is a world-level application of function-specific identity and ordinary
causal ecology. It creates no System successor, personal colony identity,
shared level, pooled statistics, spawn point, refill node, or reward mechanism.

## Residual unknowns

- Module anatomy, individuality, genetic relationship, and independent viability
- Patch dimensions, age, connection density, transfer carriers, rates, losses, and directionality
- Fragmentation survival, sealing, reconnection, fusion, competition, and disease transmission
- Growth, reproduction, dispersal, dormancy, senescence, and variation among guild members
- Mana use, signalling, appraisal, human knowledge, and human exploitation

## Recommendation

Use `FLORA-PATCH-A`. It preserves the reserve-web's resilience while keeping
identity, transfer, damage, and recovery local, finite, and narratively legible.
