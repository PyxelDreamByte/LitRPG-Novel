# WLD-ECOLOGY-008 — Basin-Lake Mass-Breeder Osmoregulation and Buoyancy Balance

## Purpose

Resolve the coupled osmoregulation, buoyancy, and anterior-balance frontier left
open by `WLD-ECOLOGY-007`, while keeping the regulatory and buoyancy systems
anatomically distinct and preserving quantitative, developmental, taxonomic,
Mana, and character-knowledge unknowns.

## Proposed claims

1. Dedicated ion-exchange organs regulate the established migratory form's
   body water and dissolved ions during freshwater–marine acclimation through
   finite carrier-mediated transport.
2. Acclimation is gradual and active. It consumes time, metabolic energy, and
   material resources and can lag, saturate, or fail.
3. The ion-exchange organs are physiologically distinct from the
   passive-buoyancy chambers and are not assigned to the respiratory folds.
4. Multiple small compliant chambers distributed along the body contain a
   metabolically produced low-density wax-like gel.
5. Greater passive buoyant support lies behind the anterior mineralised head
   shield, helping longitudinal balance around its mass.
6. The gel contributes passive buoyancy and fore–aft trim in fresh and marine
   water but does not create perfect neutral buoyancy, instant adjustment, or
   immunity to depth and pressure.
7. Environmental-water density and internal water-and-ion changes can affect
   body density and trim, so the two systems must function compatibly.
8. Both systems require diet-derived material, oxygen, energy, internal
   transport, time, maintenance, waste handling, recovery, and repair. Exchange
   capacity can lag or saturate; chambers can leak, rupture, become infected,
   or lose effectiveness as condition and reserves decline.
9. Existing ordinary Mana support may reinforce their living tissues, but it
   does not create gel mass, move ions without carriers and energy, provide
   instant acclimation, or act as an activated buoyancy ability.

## Mechanisms and consequences

### Causal basis

Carrier-mediated transport supplies a bounded mechanism for correcting water
and ion balance across salinity change. The separate low-density material
provides passive lift without making ion storage itself buoyant. Distributing
small chambers along the body, with greater support behind the shield, lets the
organism offset anterior mass without one large failure-prone cavity. Neither
system removes the need for active swimming, posture control, acclimation time,
or finite biological maintenance.

### Geography, ecology, and material constraints

- Freshwater and sea water differ in both salinity and density, so successful
  transition must coordinate ion and water regulation with changing trim.
- Transport capacity, available energy, diet, temperature, disease, injury,
  starvation, reproductive load, depth, and water conditions can affect
  performance.
- Gel material must be synthesized from acquired matter. Damage and depletion
  persist until repaired or replenished through ordinary physiology.
- The selected mechanisms do not prove that every route state is passable or
  that every individual survives transition.

### Society and culture

No local name, classification, harvesting practice, valued tissue, medicinal
use, law, trade, ritual, or knowledge level is established. A useful-looking
organ is not automatically a known resource or loot component.

### History and chronology

No evolutionary origin, ancestral environment, fossil history, past human use,

### Story implications

Poor condition, abrupt salinity change, damage, or disease can plausibly alter
posture, swimming effort, transition success, or survival. A scene may expose
only external consequences supported by observation; internal organs, gel,
ion transport, and Mana contribution remain hidden without a valid evidence
source.

### Character-knowledge implications

The full author-side mechanism is not automatic protagonist knowledge.
Characters require observation, instruction, records, dissection,
experimentation, or a separately scoped appraisal channel to distinguish
salinity stress, chamber damage, starvation, infection, ordinary fatigue, or
another cause.

### CAL0 implications

This is an ordinary biological and magical ecology extension, not a System
change. Matter, ions, water, energy, oxygen, heat, waste, time, injury, disease,
recovery, and failure remain causally conserved. Mana-supported tissue function
does not become earned CAL0 reinforcement, a buff, Skill, class, spell, aura,
reward, spawn, refill, or free-energy source.

## Consistency review

- This is a forward extension of `WLD-ECOLOGY-007`, not a retcon.
- The systems are coupled by whole-body density and trim but do not become one
  dual-purpose organ.
- The ion-exchange organs are not assigned to the respiratory folds.
- `Wax-like gel` is a functional biological material class, not an Earth wax,
  wax ester, oil, or swim-bladder homology.
- The mechanisms do not establish salinity immunity, perfect trim, instant
  switching, depth immunity, route passability, or guaranteed return.
- Egg and juvenile physiology remain open.
- No CAL0 change, contradiction, or circular dependency has been identified.
