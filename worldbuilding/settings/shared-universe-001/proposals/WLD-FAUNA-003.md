# WLD-FAUNA-003 — Lineage III foundational body plan

## Purpose

Second Phase 2 scaffold: give Lineage III — the low-frame four-limbed
generalists that carry the familiar tetrapod silhouette space and the
two-legged, two-winged flyer space — a bounded foundational body plan. This
is the lineage most likely to supply everyday prey, mesopredators,
burrowers, climbers, and small flyers, so it needs a signature that explains
why it radiated so widely without becoming "generic Earth animals".

## Existing constraints

- Accepted world canon: `WLD-FAUNA-001` (Lineage III slot: low-frame
  four-limbed generalists, possible forelimb-winged branches; every lineage
  contains abundant ordinary forms; roles distributed non-Earth-wise),
  `WLD-FAUNA-002` (Lineage I: six limbs, filament-and-scute covering,
  guarded brood eggs, slow large-body reproduction — the contrast case),
  `WLD-ECOLOGY-001`–`004` (Lineage II: four limbs, tower neck, few slow
  young born live — the other contrast case)
- CAL0 rules: WLD1.2D and WLD2.1A+D
- Deliberately unresolved boundaries: species, names, taxonomy, sizes, local
  presence, Mana patterns

## Proposed claims

Common to all options:

1. Lineage III descends from a four-limbed ancestor with a low-slung limb
   posture; upright and specialised postures are derived branch conditions.
2. An internal supporting frame carries the body; the lineage has no
   exoskeleton and no scute armour comparable to Lineage I's.
3. The outer covering is a flexible skin bearing branch-specific structures;
   it does not combine Lineage I's filaments and scutes.
4. Winged branches derive wings from the forelimbs, leaving two walking legs
   — the two-legged, two-winged silhouette reserved for this lineage.
5. The lineage is the planet's broadest generalist radiation, occupying prey,
   mesopredator, burrowing, climbing, and small-flyer roles.

## Alternatives — the lineage's life-history signature

| Option | Distinguishing choice | Benefits | Costs/risks | Reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Litter-and-disperse: many young, fast turnover, high early mortality | Explains the radiation directly; makes the lineage the world's renewable prey base; sharp contrast with both existing lineages | Large-bodied branches need their own later handling | High at this grain |
| B | Two-form metamorphosis: distinct juvenile form transforming into the adult | Vivid; juveniles and adults split niches | Every later species needs two designs; heavy commitment | High |
| C | Few young with long parental teaching | Rich social and cognitive stories | Collides with Lineage II's slow-few-young identity; undercuts the ordinary-layer role | High |

### Option A — Litter-and-disperse generalists (recommended)

The lineage's signature is reproductive throughput: breeding produces several
to many young at once, juveniles grow fast, disperse early, and suffer high
mortality, and generations turn over quickly. This is the mechanical
explanation for the lineage's breadth — fast turnover plus wide dispersal is
what lets one body plan flood so many niches — and it makes Lineage III the
renewable base the food web needs, in clean contrast with Lineage I's
guarded nests and Lineage II's few slow young. Large-bodied branches may
moderate the pattern; their handling remains open. Parental care exists in
some branches but is not the lineage's defining investment.

### Option B — Two-form generalists

Young hatch or are born as a distinct juvenile form and later transform into
the adult form, so the two stages occupy different niches. Striking, but it
doubles the design cost of every future species in the lineage.

### Option C — Teaching generalists

Few young, extended care, learning-heavy development. Good stories, but it
duplicates Lineage II's reproductive identity and leaves the ecosystem
without an obvious ordinary-prey engine.

## Mechanisms and consequences

### Causal basis

Deep four-limbed ancestry with a low-slung ancestral posture; ordinary
evolution, no System cause.

### Geography, ecology, and material constraints

Fast turnover ties the lineage's local abundance tightly to seasonal
productivity: good years produce visible surges, bad years crash and recover
quickly. This gives predators a variable but renewable base and makes the
lineage the most responsive part of any local food web.

### Story implications

Ordinary animal life — the things people see daily, hunt, keep, or are
pestered by — comes mostly from this lineage, alongside two-winged flyers;
population surges and crashes give seasons visible texture.

## Recommendation

Option A. It supplies the causal reason for the lineage's breadth rather
than asserting breadth by fiat, gives the food web the renewable ordinary
base every other lineage's predators need, contrasts cleanly with both
already-developed lineages' reproductive strategies, and keeps per-species
design cost low, which matters most for the lineage that will need the most
species.
