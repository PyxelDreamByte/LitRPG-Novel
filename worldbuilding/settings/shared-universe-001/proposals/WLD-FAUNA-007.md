# WLD-FAUNA-007 — Lineage VII foundational body plan

## Purpose

Final Phase 2 scaffold: give Lineage VII — the unsegmented soft-bodied
lineage — a bounded foundational body plan, completing the faunal roster.
Its design problem is distinctness: Lineage V is already soft-bodied, so VII
needs a life mode of its own rather than being "the other soft lineage".

## Existing constraints

- Accepted world canon: `WLD-FAUNA-001` (Lineage VII slot: unsegmented
  soft-bodied animals), `WLD-FAUNA-006` (Lineage V: tri-radial hydrostatic,
  margin-wave movement, predominantly aquatic — the lineage VII must be
  distinct from), `WLD-FAUNA-004` (Lineage VI: segmented and
  exoskeletal — the other small-life lineage), `WLD-FLORA-008` (K6 thread
  decomposers wrap and digest concentrated dead material; VII's roles must
  complement rather than duplicate that)
- CAL0 rules: WLD1.2D and WLD2.1A+D
- Deliberately unresolved boundaries: species, names, taxonomy, sizes, local
  presence, Mana patterns

## Proposed claims

Common to all options:

1. Lineage VII bodies are unsegmented and soft, without a rigid internal
   skeleton or a jointed exoskeleton.
2. Bodies are broadly bilateral and elongate along a single axis, in contrast
   with Lineage V's tri-radial organisation.
3. Movement is driven by muscular waves running along the body axis rather
   than by Lineage V's travelling margin waves.
4. The lineage occurs in water, soil, and other moist substrates.

## Selected structure — life mode as a branch axis

On 2026-08-17 the Author directed that all three life modes be included. They
are recorded as branches within Lineage VII rather than as separate lineages,
because they share one body plan — unsegmented, soft, no rigid internal
skeleton or jointed exoskeleton — and differ in how they make a living, which
is the level the roster treats as branch variation:

- **Filter branches** draw suspended material from water, sessile or
  weakly mobile on submerged surfaces.
- **Substrate branches** live inside material — soil, mud, litter, sediment,
  decaying matter — driving through it by muscular waves and processing it as
  they go. These branches own substrate turnover: the mixing, aeration, and
  breakdown that make soils work, complementing rather than duplicating the
  K6 thread decomposers, which wrap and digest concentrated dead material
  from outside. Internal parasitism derives from this habit, since living
  within a body is living within material.
- **Grazer branches** move over exposed surfaces, feeding from them and
  relying on protective secretions against desiccation and abrasion.

## Alternatives — which life mode is ancestral

| Option | Distinguishing choice | Benefits | Costs/risks | Reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Filter ancestral, then substrate, then grazer | Explains the lineage's moisture dependence by aquatic ancestry and its secretions as a late adaptation; gives a clean water → substrate → surface colonisation story | Soil turnover becomes a derived achievement rather than the lineage's origin | High at this grain |
| B | Substrate ancestral; filter and grazer branches derived | Makes soil processing the lineage's core identity | Leaves moisture dependence and secretions unexplained; a soft body originating inside material is a harder starting point | High |
| C | Grazer ancestral; filter and substrate derived | Most visible ancestor | Least plausible origin for a soft body needing moisture; closest to Earth molluscs | High |

### Option A — Filter-ancestral progression (recommended)

The ancestral condition is aquatic filter feeding. From those filterers came
mobile forms that invaded sediment and then soil, becoming the substrate
branches; from the substrate branches, in turn, came grazers that emerged
onto exposed surfaces with protective secretions. The progression explains
what the lineage otherwise leaves unexplained — why soft unsegmented bodies
remain tied to moisture everywhere they occur, and why surface forms need
secretions the others do not — while leaving substrate branches in
possession of soil turnover and parasitism.

## Mechanisms and consequences

### Causal basis

Deep unsegmented soft-bodied divergence; ordinary evolution. Living within
material rewards a smooth, flexible, axially muscular body and penalises
rigid projections.

### Geography, ecology, and material constraints

Substrate dwellers depend on moisture and workable material: they are absent
from dry, frozen, or solid ground, concentrated where organic material
accumulates, and vulnerable to drought, deep freezing, compaction, and
scouring. Their turnover work is invisible but load-bearing for soil fertility
and therefore for agriculture.

### Story implications

The lineage gives digging, ploughing, butchery, flood deposits, and rot their
population of small hidden animals; it also supplies the world's internal
parasites, with the medical and veterinary consequences that implies, without
any being established here.

### Character-knowledge implications

None established.

### CAL0 implications

Application only; ordinary ecology.

## Recommendation

Option A. Filter-ancestral ordering explains the two things the lineage would
otherwise assert without cause — its universal moisture dependence and the
secretions only its surface forms need — and it yields a legible
water → substrate → surface colonisation story. Substrate branches keep
soil turnover and parasitism either way, so nothing ecologically load-bearing
is lost by treating them as a derived achievement rather than an origin.
