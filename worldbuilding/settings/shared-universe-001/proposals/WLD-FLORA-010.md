# WLD-FLORA-010 — Basin returning-woodland composition and succession

## Purpose

First Phase 4 record: say what the woodland returning across the initial
basin actually consists of, and what state seventy years of post-collapse
regrowth has reached. `WLD-LOCATION-001` establishes that woodland resumes
beyond the settlement's managed apron and presses closer on difficult sides,
but leaves its taxa, structure, density, succession, and hazards open. This
record closes that gap using the accepted kingdom scaffolds, and in doing so
assigns the basin woodland to kingdoms for the first time.

## Existing constraints

- Accepted world canon: `WLD-FLORA-005` (tower flora: living core, dead
  casings, slow to replace, episodic seeding years), `WLD-FLORA-007`
  (free-seeders: dormant vault banks in soil, woken by disturbance, buried
  legacies possible under old worked land), `WLD-FLORA-009` (crust-and-cushion
  revival flora colonising dead casings and stone), `WLD-FLORA-008` (thread
  decomposers wrapping deadfall), `WLD-ECOLOGY-001` and `WLD-ECOLOGY-004`
  (crown herds maintain open corridors and vegetation mosaics; ribbon groups
  shape woodland edges at finer scale), `WLD-LOCATION-001` (woodland resumes
  beyond the apron), `WLD-GEOGRAPHY-001` (transitional climate; the western
  rainforest is a separate biome)
- Historical frame: the previous power's roughly seventy-year retreat ended
  organised ecological suppression, and its clearances were abandoned
- CAL0 rules: WLD1.2D and WLD2.1A+D
- Deliberately unresolved boundaries: species, names, exact extent, timber
  practice, ownership, and the western rainforest's own composition

## Proposed claims

Common to all options:

1. The basin's returning woodland is composed principally of K2 tower flora
   with K3 free-seeder thickets, over a ground and surface layer of K5
   crust-and-cushion flora and K6 thread decomposers.
2. Its present state is young: seventy years is a short time against tower
   replacement rates.
3. Lineage II browsing contests the woodland's advance, so succession is not
   uniform across the basin.

## Alternatives — what seventy years of regrowth has produced

| Option | Distinguishing choice | Benefits | Costs/risks | Reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Thicket-and-relict: dense young vault-seeder and young-tower thicket, with scattered surviving old towers as relicts | Uses all three scaffolds causally; gives the settlement a real material constraint and makes old towers precious; hard passage creates honest travel friction | Commits the basin to poor mature timber until later records say otherwise | High at this grain |

### Option A — Thicket-and-relict woodland (recommended)

When suppression ended, buried K3 vault banks under abandoned clearances woke
and erupted, and the basin's woodland today is chiefly the result: dense,
tangled, young growth — vault-seeder thickets giving way patchily to young
towers that have had time to rise but not to thicken. Scattered through it
stand **relict old towers**, survivors that predate the clearances, obvious
by their bulk and their deep-layered casings and often carrying heavy K5
crust-and-cushion cover on the older casing faces. Three consequences follow
at theme level. First, mature timber-analogue material is scarce: the
settlement's woodland is rich in thin stems and poor in old ones, so relict
towers are locally valuable and their felling is consequential. Second,
passage is hard — young thicket is far worse to move through than mature
woodland — which shapes travel, herding, and defence around the apron.
Third, the woodland's advance is contested rather than steady, because crown
herds break openings and ribbon groups work the edges, so the boundary moves
in some places and is held back in others.

### Option B — Advancing tower front

Towers spread steadily from surviving stands into open ground, producing
maturing woodland with workable timber and easy going. Comfortable, but it
ignores both the vault-bank mechanism and the shortness of seventy years
against the tower replacement rate the kingdom record already fixed.

### Option C — Browsing-held parkland

Lineage II browsing holds the basin open, and woodland persists only in
groves on difficult ground. Strong megafaunal texture, but it contradicts
`WLD-LOCATION-001`'s woodland pressing close to the settlement.

## Mechanisms and consequences

### Causal basis

The end of organised suppression released disturbance-cued vault banks;
tower recovery follows on a much slower clock; browsing keeps the outcome
patchy.

### Geography, ecology, and material constraints

Thicket is fuel: dense young growth on drier basin ground raises surface-fire
exposure in the dry spells `WLD-GEOGRAPHY-001` allows. Relict towers are
finite and slow to replace. Deadfall accumulates faster than a young
woodland's decomposer population may process it.

### Society and culture

A settlement ringed by young thicket has fuelwood and small stems in
abundance and structural material in short supply; its woodland economy would
be shaped by that asymmetry, though no practice is established here.

### History and chronology

The woodland dates from the end of suppression, so its age structure is a
readable record of the collapse — a point of evidence available to observers
without granting anyone the knowledge to read it.

### Story implications

Travel off the road is slow and blind; the apron's edge is a real boundary;
old towers are landmarks and resources; fire risk is a live seasonal concern.

### Character-knowledge implications

None established; whether anyone reads age structure as history remains open.

### CAL0 implications

Application only; ordinary ecology.

## Recommendation

Option A. It is the only option that follows causally from the scaffolds
already accepted — vault banks explain the thicket, tower replacement rates
explain the scarcity of old growth, and Lineage II browsing explains the
patchiness — and it converts seventy years of history into a landscape the
reader can see and the settlement has to live with.
