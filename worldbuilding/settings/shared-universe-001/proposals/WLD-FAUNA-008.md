# WLD-FAUNA-008 — Basin-lake resident fauna assemblage

## Purpose

Second Phase 4 record: establish the lake's year-round animal community.
`WLD-ECOLOGY-006` repeatedly refers to resident lake fauna that compete with
both spring migrants, to aquatic predators, terrestrial shore hunters, and
bird-like fishing organisms — but assigns none of them to a lineage or a
niche structure. With all seven lineages now scaffolded, those references can
be resolved into a bounded assemblage.

## Existing constraints

- Accepted world canon: `WLD-ECOLOGY-006` (residents compete with both
  migrants during their lake phases; humans, aquatic predators, terrestrial
  shore hunters, and bird-like fishing organisms exploit the seasonal
  abundance; `bird-like` denotes ecological resemblance only, establishing no
  feathers, flight, or Earth-bird anatomy), `WLD-FAUNA-005` (Lineage IV:
  fully aquatic trunk-and-fan core spanning the full aquatic size range),
  `WLD-FAUNA-006` (Lineage V: tri-radial hydrostatic, predominantly
  aquatic), `WLD-FAUNA-003` (Lineage III: generalists including
  forelimb-winged branches), `WLD-FAUNA-004`/`007` (small-bodied lineages),
  `WLD-FLORA-006` (anchor-and-streamer water flora, the unassigned
  egg-substrate candidate), `WLD-GEOGRAPHY-001` (bays freeze; deep water
  rarely does)
- CAL0 rules: WLD1.2D and WLD2.1A+D
- Deliberately unresolved boundaries: species, names, sizes, population
  figures, human fishing practice

## Proposed claims

Common to all options:

1. The lake holds a permanent resident animal community, present year-round
   and independent of the spring migrant pulses.
2. Resident aquatic animals are drawn principally from Lineage IV, with
   Lineage V, VI, and VII members also present.
3. The `WLD-ECOLOGY-006` bird-like fishing organisms are Lineage III
   forelimb-winged branch members — the two-legged, two-winged silhouette the
   roster reserved for that lineage — resolving their long-open lineage
   assignment. `Bird-like` remains an ecological resemblance only.
4. Terrestrial shore hunters are drawn from Lineages I and III.
5. Residents must survive winter in the lake, including the regular freezing
   of sheltered bays.

## Alternatives — how residents relate to the migrant pulses

| Option | Distinguishing choice | Benefits | Costs/risks | Reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Two-speed community: long-lived slow-breeding residents that endure the migrant surge, plus short-lived residents that boom on it | Explains how a resident community persists against an annual flood of competitors; gives the lake two visibly different animal populations; makes bad migrant years legible | Two population classes to keep track of | High at this grain |
| B | Uniformly suppressed residents outcompeted each spring | Simple; emphasises migrant dominance | A community that loses every year should not persist; makes residents ecologically pointless | High |
| C | Residents structurally dominant; the pulse a large seasonal subsidy | Keeps the lake's own community central and permanently populated; the pulse arrives into an occupied lake rather than defining it | Requires careful scoping so the pulse is not downgraded; resident winter limits still need later work | High |

### Option C — Resident-dominant lake (selected, as scoped below)

The lake's permanent resident community sets its ecological structure and
carries most of its standing animal biomass year-round. The migrant pulse
does not restructure that community: it arrives into an already-occupied
lake, concentrates in particular places and weeks, and functions as a large
seasonal subsidy — spectacular where it lands, and consequential for what
feeds on it — rather than as the lake's organising force.

Scoping recorded on Author confirmation: `dominant` means residents
structure the lake and hold most of its standing biomass. It does **not**
downgrade the migrant pulse, whose dispersed nursery grounds, shifting
feeding grounds, concentrated predators, and human harvest activity remain
exactly as `WLD-ECOLOGY-006` establishes them. The two claims are compatible
because a lake can be structurally resident-dominated while receiving a
large, concentrated, seasonally limited influx.

### Option A — Two-speed resident community (originally recommended)

The residents divide into two responses to the annual surge. **Enduring
residents** are longer-lived, slower-breeding animals — including the lake's
larger Lineage IV predators — that are not displaced by the migrant arrival
and instead exploit it, feeding heavily during the pulse and living off
condition through the lean months; their populations change slowly and track
multi-year conditions rather than single seasons. **Opportunist residents**
are small, short-lived, fast-breeding animals — largely Lineage VI and VII,
with small Lineage IV forms — whose numbers boom on the eggs, juveniles, and
carcasses the pulse delivers, then crash back over winter. The consequence at
theme level is that the lake carries two populations on different clocks: a
stable, slow-changing one whose condition reflects several years, and a
volatile one that visibly answers each spring. A failed migrant year shows up
immediately in the opportunists and only later, and more seriously, in the
enduring residents.

### Option B — Suppressed residents

Residents are outcompeted each spring and persist only marginally. Simple,
but a community that loses annually would not remain a community.

### Alternative reading not selected — pulse downgraded

A stronger reading of resident dominance would make the migrant pulse a minor
seasonal addition. That would contradict `WLD-ECOLOGY-006`'s established
nursery grounds, shifting feeding grounds, concentrated predators, and human
harvest, and would require reopening that record. The Author explicitly
declined it in favour of the compatible scoping above.

## Mechanisms and consequences

### Geography, ecology, and material constraints

Winter is the resident community's binding constraint, since the migrants
leave and the residents do not: bay ice, low productivity, and lean months
set the ceiling on resident populations far more than summer competition
does. Deep water that rarely freezes is therefore load-bearing as
overwintering habitat.

### Society and culture

A two-speed community means the lake offers a dependable but limited
year-round harvest alongside a spectacular seasonal one — a distinction any
fishing settlement would organise around, though no practice is established.

### Story implications

The lake is not empty outside the migration; winter fishing, lean-season
scarcity, and the slow decline of big predators after bad years all become
available.

### Character-knowledge implications

None established; whether anyone distinguishes the two classes remains open.

### CAL0 implications

Application only; ordinary ecology.

## Recommendation

Option A was recommended at presentation, on the grounds that a two-speed
community explains how residents survive an annual flood of competitors. The
Author selected Option C instead, and on confirmation scoped it so that
resident dominance is structural rather than a downgrade of the pulse. Under
that scoping C answers the same question differently and at least as well:
residents are not flooded out because the pulse is spatially and seasonally
concentrated and arrives into a lake the residents already hold.
