# WLD-LOCATION-001 — Initial Ridge-Spur Settlement Working Landscape

## Purpose

Give bounded local form to the already-established fortified ridge-spur
settlement so later daily-life, ecology, logistics, threat, and culture work can
use a coherent immediate landscape without preselecting its people,
institutions, exact map, detailed infrastructure, or operational outcomes.

## Proposed claims

1. The fortified core occupies the local crown of the ridge spur already
   established by WLD-ECOLOGY-004.
2. An unequal-shoulder managed apron surrounds that crown.
3. The innermost part of the apron is a deliberately maintained
   low-vegetation warning belt.
4. Beyond the warning belt, small and incomplete contour terraces occupy some
   usable steeper upper slopes.
5. Broader fields and grazing ground occupy the gentler lower shoulder.
6. Woodland resumes beyond the managed apron and comes closer to the fortified
   core on steep or otherwise difficult sides.
7. A constructed road descends in bends toward a lake that lies beyond the
   immediate managed apron but within regular local travel and haulage reach.
8. A spring emerges within the fortified perimeter and is incorporated into
   settlement water access; wells supplement that access, and cisterns provide
   stored water.

## Mechanisms and consequences

### Causal and material basis

Repeated clearing and management can keep the inner warning belt low and the
worked slopes open, but regrowth, erosion, drainage, terrace condition, road
condition, and water infrastructure remain dependent on finite labour,
materials, and environmental conditions. No automatic or permanent clearing
is established.

The spring, wells, and cisterns form a layered access-and-storage
arrangement. The spring and wells may share groundwater and therefore recharge,
drawdown, contamination, or drought exposure. Cisterns can hold only water
that reaches them and only within their eventual capacity and condition. The
exact hydrogeology and infrastructure remain unselected.

### Geography, ecology, and material constraints

The unequal apron follows qualitative terrain asymmetry: management extends
farther across the gentler shoulder, while steep or difficult sides allow
woodland to approach more closely. This does not establish an exact contour
map, soil distribution, local forest type, slope, or area.

The warning belt is distinct from the fields, grazing ground, and terraces
beyond it. Those outer worked areas need not be uniformly short, treeless, or
equally visible. WLD-ECOLOGY-004 continues to govern exposure around some outer
fields, roads, and lake approaches, without proving that any feature introduced
here is one of those exact overlaps.

### Society and culture

The selected landscape establishes no founder, dynasty, council, population,
culture, tenure system, ownership, labour obligation, crop, domesticate,
occupation, office, water right, rationing practice, road authority, or
maintenance institution.

### History and chronology

The record establishes no settlement age, construction phase, clearance date,

### Story implications

- Later scenes may use a coherent outward sequence from fortified crown through
  low warning belt, worked slopes, broader lower-shoulder land, and returning
  woodland.
- The road can support regular lakeward movement and haulage without fixing
  distance, travel time, destination, load, vehicle, safety, or all-season use.
- Fields, roads, lake approaches, and woodland margins can remain exposed
  beyond the fortified core, but every exact hazard, encounter, closure, and
  loss requires separate evidence.
- Water access can create visible practical work and several possible failure
  points, but no shortage, contamination, siege, dispute, office, or practice
  is selected.

### Character-knowledge implications

The selected layout and water arrangement are author-side setting truth.
People can directly observe local land cover, slopes, road form, visible
waterworks, flow, water level, damage, maintenance, and use only where their
experience or evidence permits. Those observations do not reveal exact
hydrogeology, hidden contamination, recharge, future reliability, ecological
ranges, institutional control, or System status.

### CAL0 implications

This is an ordinary world-level location and land-use extension under CAL0
WLD1.2D and WLD2.1A+D. Clearing, farming, grazing, roads, groundwater access,
and storage retain their causal requirements and consequences. The proposal
creates no System settlement designation, safe zone, defensive modifier,
