# WLD-FAUNA-002 — Lineage I foundational body plan

## Purpose

First Phase 2 scaffold: promote the staging capture's six-limbed
filament-and-scute sketch into a bounded provisional record, and resolve the
one genuinely open question `WLD-FAUNA-001` left inside it — how the
lineage's winged branches, which carry the four-legs-plus-wings mythic
silhouette space, are derived from the six-limbed frame.

## Existing constraints

- Accepted world canon: `WLD-FAUNA-001` (Lineage I slot: six-limbed
  filament-and-scute animals, branches may repurpose limb pairs including
  wing-pair branches; flight arose independently in more than one lineage;
  every lineage contains abundant ordinary forms)
- Staging (non-canonical, promoted by this proposal): six-limbed ancestor
  with a tail; limb pairs repurposed for locomotion, handling, display,
  defence, digging, climbing, swimming, or flight-equivalent functions;
  covering of insulating or sensory filaments plus flexible scutes;
  warm-active; flexible mineral-latticed brood eggs requiring guarded nests
  or brooding; slow reproduction in large-bodied branches
- CAL0 rules: WLD1.2D and WLD2.1A+D
- Deliberately unresolved boundaries: species, names, taxonomy, sizes,
  local presence, Mana patterns

## Proposed claims

Common to all options (promotion of the staged sketch):

1. Lineage I descends from a six-limbed ancestor with a tail.
2. Branches repurpose limb pairs for locomotion, handling, display, defence,
   digging, climbing, swimming, or flight.
3. The outer covering combines insulating or sensory filaments with flexible
   scutes, in proportions varying by branch and body region.
4. They are warm-active animals.
5. Reproduction uses flexible, mineral-latticed brood eggs that require
   guarded nests or brooding; no branch is established as live-bearing.
6. Large-bodied branches reproduce slowly, making breeding adults and
   successful nesting sites ecologically consequential.
7. Winged branches build their flight surfaces from the lineage's existing
   filament-and-scute materials rather than novel membranes; exact structure
   remains open.

## Alternatives — which limb pair becomes the wing pair

| Option | Distinguishing choice | Benefits | Costs/risks | Reversibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Middle pair becomes wings | Four walking legs stay fore-and-aft balanced; classic four-legs-plus-wings silhouette; wings sit over the body's centre | Shoulder-region anatomy carries both wings and forelegs | High at this grain |
| B | Forelimbs become wings | Strong forward launch; wings lead the silhouette | Walking legs bunch rearward; handling and forelimb manipulation lost in every flyer | High |
| C | Hindlimbs become wings | Unusual, distinctly alien | Poor takeoff geometry; awkward terrestrial gait; least legible silhouette | High |

### Option A — Mid-pair flight (recommended)

The middle limb pair — least committed to weight-bearing in a six-limbed
frame, since fore and hind pairs already bracket the body's mass — is the pair
that becomes wings in flying branches. Flyers therefore keep four
well-spaced walking legs plus a wing pair rising near the body's centre of
mass, which is both the mechanically sensible arrangement and the classic
mythic silhouette. Non-flying branches keep the mid pair for grasping,
digging, display, or reduced vestiges, so the same pair carries the
lineage's most variable roles. Wing surfaces derive from lineage filaments
and scutes; whether a given branch's wings are filament-vaned, scute-braced,
or some combination remains open.

### Option B — Forelimb flight

The front pair becomes wings, and flyers walk on the mid and hind pairs.
Launch improves, but the walking legs crowd toward the rear and every flying
branch permanently loses forelimb handling.

### Option C — Hindlimb flight

The rear pair becomes wings. Distinctly alien, but takeoff geometry and
terrestrial movement both suffer, and the silhouette reads least clearly.

## Mechanisms and consequences

### Causal basis

Deep ancestral six-limb frame with branch-level repurposing; ordinary
evolution, no System cause.

### Geography, ecology, and material constraints

Brood eggs plus guarded nesting tie the lineage to defensible nest sites and
make breeding seasons and nest predation consequential; slow large-body
reproduction makes megafaunal branches vulnerable to sustained pressure —
which fits a landscape where such animals are only now returning after
imperial suppression.

### Story implications

The lineage supplies both ordinary six-limbed animals and the world's
four-legs-plus-wings mythic silhouettes; nest sites become defensible,
losable, story-bearing places.

### Character-knowledge implications

None established.

### CAL0 implications

Application only; ordinary ecology.

## Recommendation

Option A. It is the mechanically sensible reading of a six-limbed frame,
preserves four balanced walking legs beneath a centrally mounted wing pair,
delivers the mythic silhouette the roster reserved for this lineage, and
concentrates the lineage's most variable limb roles in one pair, which gives
non-flying branches a clear identity too.
