# WLD-LOCATION-003 — Initial Ridge-Spur Lake-Road Shore Endpoint Arrangement

## Purpose

Give the established lake road a bounded qualitative relationship with the
changeable lake margin so later daily-life, logistics, ecology, and story work
can use a recurring upper-shore landmark and variable final approaches without
prematurely creating a port, shore settlement, transport system,
infrastructure, institution, or guaranteed access.

- WLD-LOCATION-001 establishes a constructed road descending in bends toward a
  lake beyond the immediate managed apron and within regular local travel and
  haulage reach, while leaving its endpoint and shore works open.
- WLD-LOCATION-002 supplies only broad direction-and-load-specific
  fair-weather whole-journey anchors and leaves every shore endpoint,
  infrastructure choice, segment time, and exact trip open.
- WLD-GEOGRAPHY-001 establishes a freshwater through-flow lake and qualitative
  seasonal water, flood, ice, and weather conditions while leaving exact local
  shoreline geometry and behaviour open.
- WLD-ECOLOGY-004 permits secondary crown-herd-path overlap around some roads
  and lake approaches without assigning an overlap to this road or endpoint.
- WLD-ECOLOGY-006 establishes a productive, seasonally varied lake, moving
  biological concentrations, human exploitation, and shore predators without
  placing any specific organism, nursery, fishery, or encounter here.
- CAL0 WLD1.2D, WLD2.1A+D, and ITM1.2D preserve ordinary embodied ecology,
  world causality, carriage, and access.

This is a forward location extension, not a replacement or retcon.

## Proposed claims

1. The established lake road ends at one recurring upper-shore staging place.
2. The staging place is on relatively firm ground set back from the ordinarily
   shifting wet margin.
3. Several short seasonal spurs extend from the staging place toward nearby
   shore contacts.
4. Which shore contact is usable, and therefore which spur can serve as the
   final approach, can vary with seasonal and local conditions.
5. The staging place recurs while the final shore contact can change.
6. The staging place and any individual spur can flood, erode, ice over,
   attract wildlife, or become unusable; several spurs do not guarantee access
   at a particular time.

## Mechanisms and consequences

### Causal and material basis

The arrangement separates the relatively persistent end of the main road from
the more mutable final approaches across the shore margin. Relative firmness,
setback, several short spurs, and variable usability are qualitative
relationships. They establish no surveyed flood level, dimensions,
construction, load rating, maintenance condition, route capacity, or
operational guarantee.

### Geography, ecology, and material constraints

The staging place and spurs remain exposed to ordinary finite ground, water,
ice, erosion, weather, vegetation, obstruction, and wildlife conditions. No
specific flood, ice state, closure, damage, habitat, species, migration-path
overlap, nursery, predator concentration, or harvest site is established.
Multiple possible contacts do not guarantee that any one is usable.

### Society and culture

“Staging” creates no storage, processing, transfer practice, occupation,
staffing, authority, ownership, toll, access rule, schedule, maintenance
obligation, trade, or shore institution. No dock, boat, vehicle, structure,
cache, shelter, defensive work, or lakeside settlement is selected.

### History and chronology

No construction date, development sequence, earlier endpoint, maintenance

### Story implications
