# WLD-ECOLOGY-003 — Lineage II Two-Stage Symbiont Inheritance

## Purpose

Explain how young Lineage II animals acquire a compatible fermentation
community while preserving the ecological importance of family groups, local
diets, fostering, recovery, and disease.

## Options reviewed

| Option | Distinguishing choice | Benefits | Costs/risks | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L2-SYMBIONT-A | Small prenatal founder culture followed by social maturation | Reliable start; local adaptation; meaningful fostering and recovery | Isolation vulnerability and contact-borne disease | Selected |
| L2-SYMBIONT-B | Strict vertical inheritance of a near-complete community | Reliable maternal continuity | Brittle fostering and recovery; forces precise anatomy early | Not selected |
| L2-SYMBIONT-C | Primarily environmental acquisition after birth | Strong habitat dependence | Unreliable compatibility; weakens brood and kin roles | Not selected |

## Selected proposed claims

1. Before birth, each developing young receives a small compatible founder culture.
2. The founder culture establishes a viable beginning but is not the full,
   diverse adult fermentation community.
3. After birth, repeated contact and shared feeding with parents and relatives
   develop the mature community.
4. This social maturation allows family groups to maintain symbionts adapted to
   local diets and makes compatible kin or foster groups biologically important.
5. Orphans can survive when fostered by compatible adults; prolonged isolation
   is dangerous, especially for young or animals recovering from disruption.
6. Social contact can help restore a damaged community, while the same contact
   networks and seasonal routes can spread digestive disease.

## Consistency review

- Extends `WLD-ECOLOGY-002` rather than changing its distributed fermentation lattice.
- Supports the few-young, strong-kin direction in the staging capture without
  fixing a complete social organisation.
- Preserves host compatibility and dietary matter as real constraints.
- Creates both resilience and vulnerability from the same biological process.
- Does not specify reproductive or microscopic transfer anatomy, particular inoculation
  behaviours, microbial taxa, or named diseases.
- Creates no CAL0 reward, level, Skill, trait payload, or progression lineage.
- No contradiction or retcon identified.

## Residual unknowns

- The reproductive containment and exact physical route by which the founder culture reaches a developing young
- The exact post-birth behaviours that diversify the community
- Maturation timing and developmental dietary limits
- Compatibility across parents, relatives, foster groups, herds, and species
- Specific symbiont taxa, functions, and local variation
- Disease identities, pathology, treatment, quarantine, and recovery time
- Species-level differences and the detailed social organisation of either branch
