# WLD-ECOLOGY-001A — Lineage II Vegetation-Architect Ecology

## Question decided

What primary feeding strategy and ecological work should distinguish Lineage
II's crown and ribbon branches?

## Selected rule

Use the **divergent vegetation-architect** model:

- Crown forms use short, reinforced mouth-rays to split and process tough
  mature stalks, brush, and woody growth. Their feeding and movement help
  maintain open corridors and vegetation mosaics.
- Ribbon forms use longer tactile mouth-rays to selectively harvest shoots,
  fruiting bodies, and lakeshore or woodland-edge vegetation. They shape these
  margins at a finer scale.
- Their seasonal movements can affect roads, fields, woodland edges, soil
  fertility, and settlement defences where human and animal ranges overlap.

## Authority and boundaries

This is a `PROVISIONAL` setting decision usable within Project Hearthway fauna
and regional-ecology development. It does not settle taxonomy, species names,
exact anatomy, digestion, migration dates, domestication, hunting law,
specialised Mana organs, System levels, or Ancestry Development.

Family-wide symbiotic patch tending and mixed harvesting were not selected.
Either may still occur in a narrower species when independently justified.

## Author approval

The Author reviewed the three approaches and recommendation, then replied
**“yes”** to adopting the vegetation-architect model on 2026-08-13.

## Implications

- The family's oral divergence must remain functionally connected to diet.
- Crown populations require sufficient bulk vegetation and movement space.
- Ribbon populations can use patchier high-quality foods along complex margins.
- Future regional maps and settlement practices must account for these animals
  where their ranges overlap.
- This decision describes anatomy and ecology only; it creates no new System rule.
