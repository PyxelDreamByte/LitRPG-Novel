# WLD-ECOLOGY-008A — Basin-Lake Mass-Breeder Osmoregulation and Buoyancy Balance

## Question decided

What coupled but anatomically separated osmoregulatory and passive-buoyancy
strategy should govern the mass breeder's established migratory form?

## Selected rule

Use `SALT-ORGANS-AND-BALANCE-COMB`.

Dedicated ion-exchange organs regulate body water and dissolved ions during
freshwater–marine acclimation through finite carrier-mediated transport.
Separately, multiple small compliant chambers distributed longitudinally
contain a metabolically produced low-density wax-like gel, with greater passive
buoyant support behind the anterior mineralised shield.

The gel contributes passive buoyancy and fore–aft balance without perfect
neutral buoyancy, instant adjustment, or depth immunity. Both systems require
finite materials, oxygen, energy, time, maintenance, waste handling, recovery,
and repair and can lag, saturate, leak, rupture, become infected, or lose
effectiveness. Existing ordinary Mana support may reinforce living tissues but
cannot create gel mass, move ions without carriers and energy, provide instant
acclimation, or act as an activated buoyancy ability.

`Balance comb` is an editorial label for the longitudinal arrangement of
internal chambers, not an established in-world term or a comb-shaped organ.

## Author approval

After the complete recommended **“Salt organs + balance comb”** package and its
cost, failure, and Mana boundaries were presented, the Author replied
**“yes”** on 2026-08-14.

## Rationale

The selected package directly addresses salinity transition and the anterior
shield's mass while keeping water-and-ion regulation separate from buoyancy
material. Distributed non-gas chambers provide plausible passive support with
ordinary material, metabolic, maintenance, and damage costs, without making
one organ solve every problem or granting a cost-free magical ability.

## Binding consequences

- The selected systems govern the established migratory form only. Egg,
  juvenile, and other life-stage strategies remain open.
- Ion-exchange organs use finite carrier-mediated transport and are distinct
  from both the respiratory folds and the buoyancy chambers.
- Freshwater–marine acclimation consumes time, metabolic energy, and material
  resources and can lag, saturate, or fail. No salinity immunity or instant
  switching follows.
- Multiple small compliant chambers contain metabolically produced low-density
  wax-like gel and provide greater passive buoyant support behind the anterior shield.
- Passive support contributes to buoyancy and longitudinal trim but does not
  guarantee neutral buoyancy, effortless hovering, equal performance across
  salinities or depths, route passage, or survival.
- Diet-derived matter, oxygen, energy, internal transport, waste handling,
  maintenance, recovery, repair, disease, leakage, rupture, and declining
  condition remain causal constraints.
- Mana may reinforce living tissues only. It does not create gel mass,
  transport ions without carriers and energy, provide instant acclimation, or
  establish an activated ability, Skill, class, buff, reward, spawn, refill, or
  free energy.
- `WLD-ECOLOGY-006` continues to govern migration, egg deposition, adult return
  and mortality, predation, recruitment, and population variability;
  `WLD-ECOLOGY-007` continues to govern the foundational body plan.
