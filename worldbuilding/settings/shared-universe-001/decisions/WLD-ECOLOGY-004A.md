# WLD-ECOLOGY-004A — Lineage II Peripheral Migration Corridor

## Question decided

What broad seasonal movement pattern connects Lineage II animals to the initial
settlement's ordinary life and exceptional threats?

## Selected rule

- Crown herds normally pass through a predictable principal corridor in the
  lower basin near the settlement twice per annual cycle.
- The fortified settlement core lies on a ridge spur above and outside that route.
- Secondary herd paths overlap some outer fields, roads, and lake approaches.
- Ordinary passages cause closures, travel disruption, and crop loss rather
  than a siege.
- Severe weather, digestive disease, or forage failure can divert a crown herd
  toward the settlement and create an exceptional crisis.
- Ribbon groups create smaller, more frequent pressures around woodland and
  lake margins.

## Authority and boundaries

This is provisionally authoritative for Project Hearthway regional ecology and
initial-settlement development. It does not settle the exact map, calendar,
herd scale, route-memory mechanism, human management practices, damage rates,
crisis frequency, or detailed ribbon-group movements.

## Author approval

The Author reviewed the bounded predictable peripheral-corridor model and then
replied **“yes”** on 2026-08-14.

## Implications

- The walls protect the settlement core, not its entire economic catchment.
- Migration belongs to the ordinary seasonal calendar.
- Lower-land livelihoods bear recurring disruption and loss.
- Major herd crises require a causal route diversion rather than automatic aggression.
- No new System rule, progression source, spawn event, or shared herd entity is created.

## Reopening conditions

Reopen for incompatible regional geography, an infeasible seasonal food or
water budget, nonviable ordinary damage, a concrete story contradiction, or an
explicit Author request.
