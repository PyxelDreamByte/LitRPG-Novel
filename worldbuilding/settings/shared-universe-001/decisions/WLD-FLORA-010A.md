# WLD-FLORA-010A — Basin Returning-Woodland Composition and Succession

## Question decided

What does the woodland returning across the initial basin consist of, and
what state has seventy years of post-collapse regrowth reached?

## Selected rule

- The basin's returning woodland is composed principally of K2 tower flora
  with K3 free-seeder thickets, over a ground and surface layer of K5
  crust-and-cushion flora and K6 thread decomposers.
- Its present state is young: seventy years is short against tower
  replacement rates.
- When organised suppression ended, buried K3 vault banks under abandoned
  clearances woke and erupted; today's woodland is chiefly that result —
  dense, tangled vault-seeder thicket giving way patchily to young towers
  that have risen but not thickened.
- Scattered relict old towers survive from before the clearances, recognisable
  by bulk and deep-layered casings and often carrying heavy crust-and-cushion
  cover on older casing faces.
- Mature timber-analogue material is therefore scarce; the woodland is rich
  in thin stems and poor in old ones, making relict towers locally valuable
  and their felling consequential.
- Passage through young thicket is harder than through mature woodland.
- Lineage II browsing contests the woodland's advance, so its boundary moves
  in some places and is held back in others, and succession is not uniform
  across the basin.

## Authority and boundaries

Provisionally authoritative for the basin's returning woodland at qualitative
composition-and-state grain. It does not settle species, names, exact extent,
boundaries, density, relict-tower abundance or distribution, timber or fuel
practice, ownership, management, fire frequency, decomposer sufficiency, the
western rainforest's composition, or which animals inhabit the thicket. It
identifies no particular field, road segment, or lake approach as a
crown-herd overlap.

## Author approval

After the A — Thicket-and-relict, B — Advancing tower front, and C —
Browsing-held parkland cards were presented with A recommended, the Author
selected **"A — Thicket-and-relict"** on 2026-08-17.

## Implications

- The basin woodland gains its first kingdom assignments, drawing on four
  accepted kingdom scaffolds at once.
- Seventy years of history becomes a visible landscape: thicket where
  clearances were abandoned, relicts where they were not.
- The settlement's woodland economy faces an asymmetry — abundant small
  stems and fuel, scarce structural material — without any practice being
  established.
- Travel off the road is slow and blind, making the managed apron's edge a
  real boundary.
- Dense young growth raises surface-fire exposure during the dry spells the
  climate scaffold permits.
- No Earth species, human practice, guaranteed harvest, or System mechanic is
  created.
