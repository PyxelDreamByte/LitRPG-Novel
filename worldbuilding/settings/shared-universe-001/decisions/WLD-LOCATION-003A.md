# WLD-LOCATION-003A — Initial Ridge-Spur Lake-Road Shore Endpoint Arrangement

## Question decided

What qualitative shore-endpoint arrangement should govern how the established
lake road meets the changeable lake margin without fixing exact placement,
engineering, infrastructure, transport, staffing, ecological use, safety, or
guaranteed access?

## Selected rule

Use B — Upper-shore staging + seasonal spurs. The road ends on relatively firm
ground set back from the ordinarily shifting wet margin. Several short
seasonal spurs lead toward whichever nearby shore contacts are usable, so the
staging place recurs while the final shore contact can change.

The staging place and every spur can flood, erode, ice over, attract wildlife,
or become unusable. These are possible impairments, not selected events,
frequencies, or guarantees that a usable route remains.

## Author approval

On 2026-08-15 the Author replied:

> I choose B — Upper-shore staging + seasonal spurs for the lake-road shore endpoint.

## Rationale

The selected arrangement preserves a recurring upper-shore landmark for
later route and haulage development while allowing the final approach to
follow a seasonally changing alien wet margin. It does so without selecting a
water-edge terminal, dock, boat, building, permanent worker, shore settlement,
defensive post, or all-season access.

## Alternatives retained

- A — Single shore work point: one maintained working point at the water's
  edge; not selected.
- B — Upper-shore staging + seasonal spurs: one recurring staging place on
  relatively firm upper-shore ground with several short condition-dependent
  approaches; selected.
- C — Dispersed seasonal access: the constructed road ends at a general
  approach followed by informal or lightly reused paths; not selected.

The exact displayed option language is preserved in the work-local capture.
Options A and C remain nonbinding evidence.
