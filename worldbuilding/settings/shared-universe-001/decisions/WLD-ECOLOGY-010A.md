# WLD-ECOLOGY-010A — Basin-Lake Family-Care Migrant Outlet-Passage Choreography

## Question decided

What qualitative outlet-passage choreography should govern the family-care
migrant without pre-empting the physical geography and passability of the
outlet?

## Author decision metadata

- **Status:** `PROVISIONAL`
- **Accepted by:** Author
- **Accepted on:** 2026-08-14
- **Approval evidence:** after all three complete visual passage-choreography
  cards were displayed and chat confirmation was required, the Author replied
  `A`, selecting `A — Margin-Ratchet Procession`

## Selected operational rule

Use `A — MARGIN-RATCHET PROCESSION`.

Where suitable support is available, paddle-columns brace in successive groups
while a travelling margin wave drives a short advance. The vault settles low
between surges. The directly observable rhythm is
`settle → ripple → brace → surge → settle`.

Before advances, the caregiving form normally attempts to regroup
mobile young beneath the refuge. During autumn descent, the same structures
brake and steer current-assisted slides.

The behaviour requires a compatible bed-or-margin contact and
resting-opportunity chain, but does not establish that one exists or that the
outlet is passable. The card's warning that passage may be slow and
conspicuous, and that abrasion, paddle-column injury, fatigue, crowding, or a
broken chain of resting opportunities may stop it, is retained as a qualitative
feasibility concern rather than a fixed performance, event, or rate claim.

`Margin-Ratchet Procession` is an author-facing editorial label, not an
in-world name, formal ethological category, organised convoy, or fixed social
formation.

## Alternatives considered

1. `A — MARGIN-RATCHET PROCESSION` — successive paddle-column bracing plus
   short margin-wave advances, low pauses, pre-advance regrouping, and
   current-assisted autumn braking and steering; **selected**.
2. `B — THREE-LOBE EDDY SLALOM` — oblique deeper-water movement and rotation
   between lower-current recovery pockets, with young shifting to the lee; not
   selected.
3. `C — VAULT-CHAIN LEAPFROG` — temporary groups alternate holding and
   advancing while young transfer between overlapping refuges or protected
   wakes; not selected.

## Rationale and evidence

The selected package makes difficult movement, care, exposure, fatigue, and
failure directly visible while using only the structures already accepted by
`WLD-ECOLOGY-009`. It extends the
accepted paddle-column and travelling-margin-wave foundation with a bounded
mechanical relationship, but keeps route support and actual passability as a
separate geography problem rather than assuming an accommodating outlet or a
cost-free Mana solution.
