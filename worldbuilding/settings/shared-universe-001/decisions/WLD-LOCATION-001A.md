# WLD-LOCATION-001A — Initial Ridge-Spur Settlement Working Landscape

## Question decided

What bounded local landscape and water arrangement should give physical form to
Project Hearthway's already-established fortified ridge-spur settlement without
pre-empting its exact map, society, detailed infrastructure, ecological use, or
operational reliability?

## Author decision metadata

- **Status:** PROVISIONAL
- **Accepted by:** Author
- **Accepted on:** 2026-08-15
- **Approval evidence:** the Author selected “option A - unequal shoulder
  apron” and directly added a spring inside the fortified settlement,
  supplemented by wells and cistern storage

## Selected operational rule

Use A — Unequal-shoulder apron.

The fortified core occupies the local crown of the ridge spur already
established by WLD-ECOLOGY-004. An unequal-shoulder managed apron surrounds it.
Its innermost part is a deliberately maintained low-vegetation warning belt.
Beyond that belt, small and incomplete contour terraces occupy some usable
steeper upper slopes, while broader fields and grazing ground occupy the
gentler lower shoulder. Woodland resumes beyond the managed apron and comes
closer on steep or otherwise difficult sides.

A constructed road descends in bends toward a lake beyond the immediate
managed apron but within regular local travel and haulage reach.

A spring emerges within the fortified perimeter and is incorporated into
settlement water access. Wells supplement that access, while cisterns provide
stored water. These are layered access and storage, not guaranteed independent
or reliable supplies; the spring and wells may share groundwater, and storage
remains limited to water collected and retained.

## Alternatives considered

1. A — Unequal-shoulder apron — fortified local crown with an asymmetric
   managed apron; **selected**.
2. B — Terminal-spur crown — fort at the end of a spur, steep on three sides
   and connected by one defended neck; not selected.
3. C — Double-knoll/saddle — higher fortified knoll paired with a lower saddle
   or second shoulder; not selected.

The Author added the combined spring, wells, and cistern arrangement directly.
No competing water-option set was presented or rejected. Incidental details of
the rejected spatial alternatives remain nonbinding decision evidence.

## Rationale and evidence

The selected option gives the settlement a coherent daily landscape while
preserving the accepted separation between its fortified ridge-spur core and
WLD-ECOLOGY-004's principal lower-basin crown-herd corridor. Terrain asymmetry
supports a compact warning belt, partial upper-slope terraces, broader
lower-shoulder working land, nearby difficult woodland edges, and a qualitative
lakeward descent without fixing a survey map. The combined water arrangement
adds plausible layers of access and storage while leaving their shared
hydrogeology, capacity, quality, maintenance, and failure unresolved.

## Binding consequences

- The fortified core is placed on the local crown of the accepted ridge spur;
  no exact elevation, contour, area, or orientation is selected.
- The managed apron is unequal. It extends farther across the gentler lower
  shoulder, while woodland comes closer on steep or difficult sides.
- The low inner warning belt is distinct from terraces, fields, grazing
  ground, and returning woodland. It does not make the entire apron uniformly
  bare, short, treeless, or permanently clear.
- Small incomplete contour terraces occur on some usable steeper upper slopes.
  Broader fields and grazing ground occur on the gentler lower shoulder.
- The constructed road descends in bends toward the lake. Its exact alignment,
  switchbacks, dimensions, construction, drainage, destination, travel time,
  capacity, and seasonal usability remain open.
- The lake is outside the immediate managed apron but within regular local
  travel and haulage reach. This is qualitative and establishes no exact
  distance, time, landing, lakeshore settlement, or guaranteed access.
- A spring emerges within the fortified perimeter, supplemented by wells and
  cistern storage.
- The spring and wells are not guaranteed separate aquifers or independent
  sources. Cisterns store finite collected water rather than produce water.
- No spring, well, or cistern is guaranteed secure, perennial, potable,
  inexhaustible, adequate, uncontaminated, freeze-proof, drought-proof,
  siege-proof, magical, or operational.
- Exact water yield, recharge, drawdown, connection, capacity, collection,
  treatment, sanitation, drainage, waste placement, maintenance, and failure
  remain open.
- WLD-ECOLOGY-004 continues to establish exposure around some outer fields,
  roads, and lake approaches, but this decision does not identify any specific
  new field, terrace, or road segment as a secondary-path overlap or guarantee
  an encounter.
- WLD-ECOLOGY-005 continues to govern basin-wide habitat exchange. No local
  species, reserve-web presence, food-web use, productivity, or migration is
  established here.
- Clearing, cultivation, grazing, road upkeep, water access, and storage use
  finite land, water, material, energy, and labour and produce persistent
  environmental and maintenance consequences.
- No settlement age, population, founder, dynasty, council, culture, crop,
  domesticate, tenure, labour institution, wall design, local rainforest,
  particular organism, plot event, Mana property, or System mechanic is
  selected.
- This decision establishes no relationship between the initial settlement and
  WLD-GEOGRAPHY-002's outlet mixed-reach sequence.
