# Generational Language Landscape

## Scope

This is the provisionally authoritative qualitative sociolinguistic
configuration of Project Hearthway's initial region: which speech varieties
exist, how they are used and generationally split at the ridge-spur hillfort
settlement, where literacy sits, the outlet settlement's speech position, and
what interpreter-capable competence is and is not. It is sociolinguistic
logic only and fixes no linguistic content of any kind.

## Provisional claims

1. Two distinct speech varieties are the region's principal ones: the tongue
   of the withdrawn imperial power and the original tongue (author-facing
   labels only). They are principal, not exhaustive — whether the
   displaced-arrival and itinerant-craft or trade strands carry further
   varieties, dialects, or trade-speech habits of their own remains open.
2. The hillfort's landscape is generationally split. The older generation
   lives in parallel bilingualism: both parent tongues stand side by side as
   full daily languages, keyed to strand and situation — households lead with
   their strand leaning, mixed crews switch as the moment demands — and
   practiced code-switching is ordinary adult competence.
3. The younger generation, raised with both tongues from childhood, is
   actively hybridising: using the two together and mixing words from both
   tongues within a single sentence.
4. That hybridising speech is an in-progress generational development, not a
   settled or named third language. How far it will carry, whether it
   stabilises, and whether the parent tongues recede are explicitly open
   questions, argued within the fiction and unsettled here.
5. Neither parent tongue is dying as fact, and no strand owns either tongue.
6. Literacy, where it exists, is anchored to the imperial script tradition,
   which holds the record function — a living skill class descended from
   imperial practice, not from any intact archive or knowledge store. The
   original tongue is customarily unwritten.
7. The outlet settlement is imperial-monolingual by default, so cross-lake
   dealings lean on hillfort speakers — a quiet standing asymmetry.
8. Recognised interpreter-capable individuals exist at the hillfort as
   personal competence and reputation only. They are never an office and
   never part of adjudication, and they create no procedure, standing,
   entitlement, or right to be heard.
9. The proposal's option-independent shared frame is also established: the
   imperial tongue owns the surviving written-record tradition as a living
   practice; comprehension between the two settlements is ordinary, with the
   direction of accommodation left open; and literacy is uneven across
   strands, households, generations, and professions (WLD-CULTURE-001
   restated, not re-litigated).
