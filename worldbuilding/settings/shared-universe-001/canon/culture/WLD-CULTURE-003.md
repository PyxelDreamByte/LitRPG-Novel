# Original Kin-Hall and Season-Court Culture

## Scope

This is the provisionally authoritative qualitative frame of the region's
original culture — the locally rooted pre-imperial human tradition that
survived the imperial period folded rather than erased and now contributes,
non-exclusively, to Project Hearthway's settlement braid: its kin-hall social
unit and season-court federation, its precedent law and speaker class, its
virtues and shames, its faith orientation, its dress marker classes, its
System folk tradition, and its survival mode under the empire.

## Provisional claims

1. The original culture predates the empire in the region and survived the
   imperial period folded — carried inside households and customs — not
   erased.
2. Its social unit is the extended kin-hall: linked households of one
   recognised descent web. A person without a hall is a person in danger.
3. Halls federate through season-courts — recurring assemblies at fixed
   customary places, keyed to the seasonal rhythms, where law is spoken,
   disputes are heard, marriages and fosterings are arranged, and goods are
   exchanged.
4. Its law is customary precedent recited by a memory-trained speaker class
   raised by memory-line apprenticeship: authoritative in standing, fallible
   in fact, and openly arguable at court.
5. Its leadership classes are hall-elders, court-ground hosting lineages, and
   the speaker class. Status runs from full hall-members through dependents
   and fostered outsiders to the hall-less, whose protection is a court
   obligation and a live moral test.
6. Its virtues are hall-hospitality (the guest eats first), true recall
   (precedent spoken whole, against interest if need be), season-duty, and
   refuge-duty — the height denied to none who flee. Its shames are
   hall-betrayal, false recall, refusing refuge, and waste of common stores.
7. The virtues genuinely grind against one another — hospitality against
   stores-prudence, true recall against hall loyalty — an old, unresolved
   argument settled differently by hall. Their cost is real and unevenly
   borne: hall-hospitality and refuge-duty are paid out of common stores by
   people who did not choose the guest, and hall standing leaves dependents
   and the hall-less genuinely exposed. Neither list renders the culture
   simply good or bad.
8. The fortified height is this culture's communal work: refuge in danger,
   highest court ground in peace, wall and law one inheritance. A
   pre-imperial fortified-height tradition is originated here as this
   culture's own background and as a tradition class only; whether any later
   settlement's fortification revives it is available resonance for
   WLD-CULTURE-005, which owns founding claims, and is not claimed here.
9. Its faith orientation reveres three classes, as contested belief only:
   ancestors as the source of precedent, imagined still attentive to their
   halls; land-and-season powers propitiated at year-turns and courts; and
   the System, read as the world's impartial order — the one witness that
   never misremembers.
10. That System reading is folk interpretation and nothing more. No System
    channel bears on testimony or recollection, attests truth, verifies
    precedent, or is available as court evidence; the never-misremembering
    reading is often wrong, and sceptics within the culture say so.
11. Its dress is layered material classes — a woven fibre-cloth body layer, a
    working over-layer, and a heavy felted or hide-class outer. The
    belonging-marker class is a woven edge-pattern on the outer layer marking
    hall and descent web; the status-marker class is worked hard-material
    fasteners, plain to fine.
12. Its System folk practice follows WLD-CULTURE-001's practice layers:
    everyday use in court-and-witness framing; advantage hoarded as hall
    property; a thin specialist tradition inside the speaker class; and
    abundant folk belief — often wrong — reading interface impressions as
    ancestral or seasonal favour. All of it is cultural layering over
    unchanged natural System behaviour.
13. Under the empire, the season-courts were the part displaced and partly
    used: the judgement the courts had carried passed away from them to the
    imperial frame's own arrangement and continued only so far as a
    recognition extended from outside the culture covered it, and gatherings
    the empire found convenient were turned to its own uses.
14. Hall lines went two ways and survived either way. Some kept a recognised
    public standing under the imperial frame, held on that frame's terms and
    withdrawable at its pleasure, which the more ambitious lines sought and
    paid for and their neighbours judged variously. Others folded inward:
    precedent-speaking as household memory-keeping, hall marks as family
    weave, refuge-duty as quiet custom.
15. The heights lapsed mainly through disuse under imperial presence and its
    organised ecological suppression. Any active discouragement is remembered
    differently and remains open.
16. The culture is internally varied — by district, descent line, and
    household — never uniform. No claim above holds for every line, and its
    members disagree about their own tradition.
17. Its speech variety exists and its lifecycle rites exist; neither's content
    is established here.
18. It contributes to the settlement's braid, most visibly within the
    locally-rooted strand class, but equals no strand exclusively. Its people
    and part-descendants appear across strand classes, and WLD-CULTURE-001's
    fabric is unchanged.
19. All faith content in this record is belief, interpretation, and
    presentation only: no deity, cosmology, afterlife, ancestor presence, or
    System character is confirmed, and natural System behaviour is unchanged.

  declared-open human-cultures space and the three-culture frame's original

  ground, hall-less, virtue, shame, marker class, and every other label in
  this record are author-facing qualitative descriptions. They fix no

  canon://world/shared-universe-001/culture/generational-language-landscape
