# Dual-Sphere Exchange Media

## Scope

This is the provisionally authoritative qualitative pattern for how worth
moves in and between Project Hearthway's initial ridge-spur settlement and
the outlet settlement: an external prestige sphere of surviving imperial
coin riding above an internal customary sphere of obligation,
labour-exchange, and in-kind return, with socially marked conversion
between the two.

## Provisional claims

1. Two customary spheres of worth exist, one riding above the other. The
   external or prestige sphere runs on surviving imperial coin:
   inter-settlement trade, storage of worth, prestige transfers, and —
   where a consented communal burden is discharged in movable worth at all
   — the customary medium of that discharge. The internal or customary
   sphere runs on obligation: witnessed debts, labour-exchange, tally
   customs, and in-kind return inside each settlement.
2. Imperial coinage existed, and physical imperial coins survive in the
   region. Coin circulates in qualitative worth classes sourced from
   pre-drawdown household holdings, salvage finds, and cross-lake inflow.
3. No functioning imperial mint or treasury authority survives anywhere in
   the region, and no local authority currently mints or validates coin.
   Wear, clipping, and household hoarding are live qualitative attrition
   concerns, and the trusted stock is thinning.
4. Internal-sphere credit runs on qualitative customs: witnessed debts,
   obligation held in memory and in tally media, labour-exchange, and
   in-kind return. No ledger institution, unit, record form, office, or
   enforcement mechanism exists.
5. Converting between spheres is socially marked. Paying a neighbour in
   coin reads as distancing or as ceremony; turning standing obligation
   into coin, or coin into standing, is noticed, storied, and sometimes
   resented. This is qualitative social meaning only — no rule,
   prohibition, or conversion rate exists, and the marking itself varies by
   household and situation.
6. Exchange between the ridge-spur settlement and the outlet settlement is
   real and ongoing, exactly as WLD-EVENT-001's standing rivalry already
   presupposes.
7. WLD-EVENT-001's contested-exchange-terms pressure lives in the coin
   sphere as argument classes: which coin classes both sides accept, wear
   and clipping disputes, and competitive claims of standing expressed
   through coin display, gift, and refusal. These are tendencies, never
   occurred incidents.
8. The outlet settlement, primarily imperial-remnant in culture, tends to
   treat the coin sphere as native ground; the hillfort tends to treat it
   as an instrument for dealing outward.
9. Each of the three cultures holds a distinct live relationship to the
   same thinning coin stock. Original-culture households tend to keep their
   dealings in the internal sphere — stores, stock, and standing obligation
   — and to use coin as a boundary tool for strangers; imperial-remnant
   tradition tends to treat coin as a bearer of record and standing, though
   never unanimously; in the hillfort's hybrid braid, fluency in both
   spheres is itself a braid skill, and households straddling strands often
   broker conversions. Each is a tendency with internal variation and no
   unanimity, and each concerns money objects and exchange media only.
10. Salvage coin from remnant ground consists of dispersed single or small
    finds within WLD-LOCATION-004's salvage activity class — finite,
    diminishing, contestable, with no right, ownership, law, market, or
    price. Salvage coin entering the external sphere carries contestable
    provenance that fuels good-coin arguments without settling any.
11. Whether the outlet settlement's metal capability ever supports coin
    testing, re-melting, or new striking is an explicit open question. No
    mint, assay, or validation institution exists anywhere in the region.
12. Coin worth is social belief and custom. The System recognises no
    currency, attests no value, and no readout, Skill, appraisal, or office
    guarantees a coin's or metal's worth.
