# Imperial Charter-and-Guild Culture

## Scope

This is the provisionally authoritative qualitative organizing logic of the
withdrawn imperial power's culture — its chartered-body government, its
corporate personhood logic, its virtue and sin field, its faith orientation
class, its dress pattern, and its ecological-suppression value — together
with the drifted living remnant expression of that culture at the outlet
settlement.

## Provisional claims

1. The withdrawn power governed as a lattice of chartered bodies — guilds,
   colleges, companies, temple-corporations — each holding delegated
   authority under written charter. Membership was identity, and law was
   charter interpretation argued between bodies.
2. Full personhood in practice was corporate: standing ran through one's
   body, and the unincorporated held thin standing.
3. Absorbed peoples were incorporated as recognized native bodies under
   limited charters or joined imperial bodies as junior members, and
   ambitious original-culture families earned or bought membership. How the
   region came under the power is not established here.
4. Internal variation existed, at minimum between the metropole's dense
   overlapping charters and the frontier's reliance on one or two bodies
   holding sweeping local grants — a works company, a garrison college.
5. Its virtue set — fidelity to charter, craft excellence, mutual aid within
   the body, and honest accounting between bodies — was genuinely admirable
   in part, for charters restrained arbitrary power and gave the low-born
   collective standing no lone petitioner had. Its sin set — charter-breach,
   encroachment on another body's chartered work, betrayal of craft secrets,
   and letting a chartered work fail — condemned real harms, and the same
   fidelity that protected members froze out the unincorporated. Neither
   list renders the culture simply good or evil.
6. Its faith orientation was a charter-granting cult class: the divine
   imagined as ultimate issuer of warrants, with each body keeping licensed
   observance of its patron-craft cult. Belief only.
7. Dress followed livery: body-membership livery classes and craft-mark
   classes, with grade shown in trim and a forbidden-marks class covering
   other bodies' livery and revoked charters' marks.
8. Its ecological-suppression ideology held that the wild was unchartered
   ground — land became grantable, ownable, and real only once a charter
   covered it — and suppression was contracted out to clearance-companies as
   profitable duty. This is value, not an added collapse cause.
9. At the outlet settlement, membership-identity persists strongly —
   belonging to a working body is who you are — with craft-mark pride and
   the habit of writing agreements down and appealing to old grants. Charter
   authority has hollowed: the granting centre is gone, so documents that
   are partial, disputed, and possibly forged carry weight with no court
   able to renew or adjudicate them. The delegation has inverted: bodies
   that were branches of a lattice now behave as sovereigns of their own
   function. The drift is contested — younger outlet generations treat
   charter-piety as elders' habit, and quiet operators simply work outside
   the old marks.
10. The imperial-descended households admitted into the ridge-spur
    settlement in its early days came out of this culture. That admission,
    its terms, and its consequences belong to
    canon://world/shared-universe-001/culture/settlement-founding-fusion-blend
    and are neither duplicated nor extended here.
11. The imperial culture was literate, standardizing, and engineering-minded,
    consistent with the accepted remnant records, and it administered the
    original culture's region for generations as a peripheral holding of a
    power centred elsewhere.
12. The outlet settlement is the imperial culture's primary living local
    continuation — carried by imperial-descended people through the
    withdrawal and later gathered at the reused nucleus, which was abandoned
    before it was re-occupied. Two clocks run separately: drift from the
    metropole may begin at centre failure, but post-authority remnant drift
    at the outlet begins only when organised imperial presence ended
    regionally, and neither that span nor the re-occupation is dated here.
    The result is improvised, internally varied, and contested — never
    frozen, uniform, or fanatical by default.
13. Internal variation existed historically, at minimum between metropole
    and frontier expressions, and persists in the remnant.
14. The imperial culture also changed across its own history: each
    recovery-and-fall cycle stripped further function layers, and stranded
    frontier offices decayed in place at different rates, so the form this
    region experienced in the arc's later phases was already stripped and
    improvised relative to its metropole ideal. The remnant inherited a late
    degraded form, not the full system. No cycle count, date, or
    stripped-function inventory is added.
15. All faith content in this record is belief and orientation class only;
    no cosmology, rite content, or afterlife claim is confirmed.
16. All imperial and remnant use of System records is cultural practice —
    access, interpretation, presentation — over unchanged natural System
    behaviour, with no guaranteed readout, no System-executed office, and no
    institution owning any person's progression.
17. No imperial work, record, or institution survives functioning as
    designed; remnant recognition is cultural recognition, not accurate
    knowledge of function.
18. Remnant beliefs about the empire, its fall, and its works are attitude
    and memory, not evidence of what actually occurred.
