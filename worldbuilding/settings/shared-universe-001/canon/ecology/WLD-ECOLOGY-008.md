# Basin-Lake Mass-Breeder Osmoregulation and Buoyancy Balance

## Scope

This is the provisionally authoritative freshwater–marine acclimation, passive
buoyancy, and longitudinal-balance strategy for the mass breeder's established
migratory form governed by `WLD-ECOLOGY-006` and `WLD-ECOLOGY-007`.

## Provisional claims

1. Dedicated ion-exchange organs regulate the established migratory form's body
   water and dissolved ions during freshwater–marine acclimation through finite
   carrier-mediated transport.
2. Acclimation is gradual and active. It consumes time, metabolic energy, and
   material resources and can lag, saturate, or fail.
3. The ion-exchange organs are physiologically distinct from the
   passive-buoyancy chambers and are not assigned to the respiratory folds.
4. Multiple small compliant chambers distributed along the body contain a
   metabolically produced low-density wax-like gel.
5. Greater passive buoyant support lies behind the anterior mineralised head
   shield and contributes to longitudinal balance around its mass.
6. The gel contributes passive buoyancy and fore–aft trim in fresh and marine
   water but does not create perfect neutral buoyancy, instant adjustment, or
   immunity to depth and pressure.
7. Environmental-water density and internal water-and-ion changes can affect
   body density and trim, so the two systems must function compatibly.
8. Both systems require diet-derived material, oxygen, energy, internal
   transport, time, maintenance, waste handling, recovery, and repair. Exchange
   capacity can lag or saturate; chambers can leak, rupture, become infected,
   or lose effectiveness as condition and reserves decline.
9. Existing ordinary Mana support may reinforce their living tissues, but it
   does not create gel mass, move ions without carriers and energy, provide
   instant acclimation, or act as an activated buoyancy ability.

## Boundaries

- This record governs the established migratory form only. Egg, juvenile, and
  other life-stage osmoregulation, buoyancy, development, and salinity tolerance
  remain open.
- `Salt organ`, `wax-like gel`, and `balance comb` are author-facing functional
  descriptions, not established in-world names or formal taxonomic terms.
  `Balance comb` does not establish a comb-shaped organ.
- `Ion-exchange organ` establishes a finite transport function rather than a
  gill, kidney, respiratory-fold, or other Earth-organ homology. Exact location
  and exchange surfaces remain open.
- Exact organ number, placement, openings, microanatomy, ion species, carriers,
  pumps, sensors, control loops, internal transport, waste and excretion routes,
  thresholds, rates, acclimation duration, and failure limits remain open.
- `Wax-like gel` establishes a low-density biological material class, not Earth
  wax, wax ester, oil, fat, gas, swim-bladder contents, or exact chemistry.
- Exact gel chemistry, density, compressibility, phase behaviour, synthesis,
  transport, replenishment, temperature response, pressure response, and
  whether it can be metabolised as emergency fuel remain open.
- `Multiple small chambers` and greater support behind the shield are
  qualitative. Exact count, position, geometry, connectivity, volume,
  attachment, growth, maintenance, repair, leakage, rupture, disease, and
  failure thresholds remain open.
- Passive support establishes no perfect neutral buoyancy, effortless hovering,
  conscious or instant control, invariant trim, depth immunity, pressure
