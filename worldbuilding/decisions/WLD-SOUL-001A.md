# WLD-SOUL-001A — Future human-reference rare-Soul prevalence guardrail

## Scope

This is a reusable provisional project-level modelling default for a future
named human-reference population. It binds a work only when that work's
manifest explicitly adopts this decision URI and scope. It is not Enuma canon,
detailed world canon, or a claim about every human population, era, species, or
setting. An independent work may override it only through its own Author
decision; that override does not change the default for other works.

## Author selection

Use the exact-decimal provisional prevalence **`0.001`**, approximately **1 in
1,000**, once a future human-reference population has been named and scoped.

A qualifying rare Soul is a heterogeneous typed profile. It may turn on
relationships among dimensions such as Depth, Coherence, Resonance, boundary
integrity, coupling, recovery, and safe assimilation. It is never a single
scalar Soul score.

This prevalence is **not Mage prevalence**. It must not be reused as the
frequency of magic users, affinity, a class, a Skill, talent, social rank, or
exceptional people generally.

## Boundaries and consequences

- No future society, culture, institution, lineage, or demographic has been created by this decision.
- The future population and its eligible denominator remain undefined.
- The typed rare-Soul qualification threshold remains undefined.
- Correlation with magic, profession, ancestry, age, culture, and environment remains open.
- Other species and populations remain out of scope.
- No work inherits the guardrail silently; explicit manifest adoption is required.
- A work-specific override requires its own Author decision.

## Approval evidence

The Author explicitly selected **Soul A** and instructed that it be recorded
with this bounded project-level future-setting scope on 2026-08-12.

## Reopening conditions

Reconsider when a named human-reference population and typed qualification
threshold exist, evidence supports a different prevalence, modelling exposes a
contradiction, or the Author explicitly reopens it.
